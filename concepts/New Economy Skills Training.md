# Defining and Describing New Economy Skills Training


_New Economy Skills Training describes practical education that connects learners with emerging workplace capabilities, mentorship, and pathways to opportunity._

In the available evidence, “NEST” is used by multiple organizations rather than as one universally defined training model. The clearest education-related example is an Atlanta nonprofit delivering business and financial education, mentorship, and seed capital to students with limited access to entrepreneurial resources.[3] Other organizations use NEST as an acronym for employment-skills programs, including Nisga’a Employment Skills & Training, which supports Indigenous citizens with training and employment services.[8] The term therefore applies most usefully to programs that combine applied skills, career readiness, technology or entrepreneurship, and access-building rather than classroom instruction alone.[3][8]

## Uses in Context

- **Entrepreneurship education:** NEST can describe programs combining business education, mentorship, and startup capital for students.[3]
- **Marketing and communication:** Its curriculum also includes marketing and public speaking, positioning skills as practical tools for launching or presenting ventures.[3]
- **Emerging technology:** The same seminars reportedly include 3D prototyping and training language-learning models.[3]
