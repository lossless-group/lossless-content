# Defining and Describing Managed Service Providers

![Remote monitoring and management dashboard showing servers, endpoints, alerts, and service tickets](https://thumbs.dreamstime.com/b/managed-services-27035129.jpg)

- _Managed service providers turn IT from a reactive repair function into a continuously monitored, recurring service._
- A **managed service provider (MSP)** is an organization that operates technology or business functions for clients under an ongoing service agreement. The model commonly includes remote monitoring, maintenance, help-desk support, cybersecurity, cloud administration, backup, compliance, and strategic technology advice. [^avyx5w] [^xesc1o]
- MSPs typically serve multiple organizations simultaneously, using standardized tools, recurring contracts, automation, and centralized expertise to deliver predictable support. The model is especially relevant to small and midsize businesses that need capabilities they cannot economically maintain internally. [^oqdm2s] [^avyx5w]
- Unlike traditional “break/fix” providers, MSPs aim to identify and resolve problems proactively through continuous monitoring and management. [^avyx5w] [^xesc1o]

## Uses in Context

- **Outsourced IT operations:** A business may retain an MSP to manage networks, computers, servers, cloud services, and user support instead of employing a full internal IT department. [^oqdm2s] [^xesc1o]
- **Proactive infrastructure management:** The term describes services that monitor systems remotely, apply patches, troubleshoot faults, and automate remediation. [^oqdm2s] [^9jvf12]
- **Cybersecurity delivery:** Contemporary MSPs frequently bundle endpoint protection, identity security, email defense, security awareness, backup, and managed detection and response. [^9jvf12] [^avyx5w]
- **Compliance and resilience:** Organizations use MSPs to strengthen business continuity, backup, regulatory readiness, and security controls. [^n9o7sf] [^gmi46g]
- **Cloud transformation:** MSPs may migrate client workloads to cloud platforms and operate the resulting environments, including identity, centralized backup, monitoring, and cost management. [^bsf1wa]
- **Business-model language:** “MSP” also denotes a recurring-revenue operating model in which services are delivered under subscriptions or managed-service agreements rather than isolated consulting projects. [^l8o0fm] [^avyx5w]

# History of Use

## Origins

- The modern MSP model is generally traced to the late 1990s and early 2000s, when organizations sought predictable, proactive IT support instead of reactive break/fix work. [^avyx5w]
- The model also developed from 1990s application service providers, which hosted software remotely for clients; early MSPs focused on remote monitoring and management of servers and networks before expanding into broader services. [^xesc1o]
- The available sources describe an industry evolution rather than crediting a single inventor or academic paper. The originating practice was therefore a market response shaped by service providers and customer demand for continuous, outsourced technology operations. [^avyx5w] [^xesc1o]

## Evolution

- **1990s:** Application service providers established the precedent of delivering hosted software remotely, creating part of the commercial and technical foundation for later managed services. [^xesc1o]
- **Late 1990s–early 2000s:** Providers shifted from reactive break/fix work toward proactive monitoring and management, making recurring support and prevention central to the model. [^avyx5w]
- **2010s–2020s:** MSPs expanded into security, compliance, cloud migration, managed detection and response, centralized backup, and strategic business advice. [^n9o7sf] [^gmi46g] [^bsf1wa] [^9jvf12] [^avyx5w]

# Best Real-World Examples

- [Enitech Solutions](https://enitechsolutions.com/kaseya-features-enitech-solutions-founder-antwine-jackson/) — grew from a one-person operation and a first managed-services client producing \$1,000 in monthly recurring revenue into a broader provider of managed IT, cybersecurity, continuity, and compliance services. [^n9o7sf]
- [BE Connected](https://www.netgear.com/ae/hub/business/case-studies/it-case-studies/how-be-connected-helps-local-businesses-keep-running-smoothly-anywhere/) — uses cloud-managed networking to monitor, maintain, update, and troubleshoot local-business networks remotely. [^oqdm2s]
- [Omega Systems](https://omegasystemscorp.com/insights/case-studies/how-a-ny-single-family-office-modernized-its-technology/) — illustrates the MSP role in combining cloud migration, cybersecurity, and round-the-clock support for a financial-services client. [^gqm2wf]
- [PGH Networks](https://pghnetworks.com/pittsburgh-small-manufacturer-msp-2/) — applies managed infrastructure, ERP support, operational technology assessment, and CMMC readiness to a small manufacturer. [^gmi46g]
- [Real Solutions](https://www.kaseya.com/case-studies/real-solutions-case-study) — evolved from an ERP provider’s internal response to customer IT requests into a separate MSP operation managing 1,300 endpoints. [^l8o0fm]
- [Innovative Solutions and Aerify](https://innovativesol.com/case-study/aerify/) — demonstrate an MSP using AWS organizational controls, single sign-on, service catalog design, centralized backup, monitoring, and cost improvement to scale customer cloud delivery. [^bsf1wa]
- [Step Fwd IT](https://www.kaseya.com/case-studies/step-fwd-it/) — represents a security-centric MSP that standardizes endpoint, identity, email, backup, awareness training, and detection-and-response controls for every customer. [^9jvf12]

# Case Studies

**Enitech Solutions: moving from founder-led support to a broader managed-services practice.** Enitech began as a one-person operation, and its first managed-services client generated \$1,000 per month in recurring revenue. [^n9o7sf] The company later expanded its offering across managed IT services, cybersecurity, business continuity, and compliance while serving organizations across North Carolina and elsewhere in the United States. [^n9o7sf] The case illustrates how an MSP can begin with a narrow recurring-support relationship and grow by adding adjacent operational and risk-management services.

**BE Connected: remote management as the practical core of the MSP model.** Founded in 2017, BE Connected was established to help small and midsize businesses manage internet access, networks, computers, and related technology. [^oqdm2s] Its cloud-managed networking environment allows the provider to monitor, maintain, update, and troubleshoot customer networks regardless of where the provider or customer is located. [^oqdm2s] The example shows why remote visibility and centralized tools make it possible for a small team to support multiple geographically distributed clients.

**Real Solutions: an internal service function becoming a standalone MSP.** Real Solutions emerged from Clients First Business Solutions, an ERP company whose customers began asking for IT support; after an external partner failed to meet the company’s standards, Clients First built a two-technician capability internally. [^l8o0fm] The division initially was not intended to be a profit center and was subsidized by its parent company. [^l8o0fm] After adopting a consolidated endpoint and security subscription, the operation became profitable as its own company, managed 1,300 endpoints, and reported tripled margins. [^l8o0fm] The case demonstrates how customer demand can transform a complementary support function into a specialized recurring-services business.
