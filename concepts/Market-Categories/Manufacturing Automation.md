# Manufacturing Automation

Manufacturing automation has evolved from a narrow focus on programmable logic controllers and robotic arms into a broad, data-driven systems category that integrates industrial control, smart manufacturing software, and AI-augmented workflows across global factories. [^muaz27] [^x3240s] [^nqi6g0] It now sits at the intersection of industrial automation, smart manufacturing, and supply-chain automation, with its own emerging market sizing, dedicated vendor ecosystem, and accelerating capital flows that justify treating it as a distinct analytic category. [^puf6kf] [^sjd0g0] [^f1yupa]  

## Snapshot

_Manufacturing automation refers to the technology stack that automates and optimizes production processes inside factories, spanning control hardware, robotics, execution software, IIoT connectivity, and increasingly AI-driven decision layers. [^muaz27] [^3t1m49] [^nqi6g0] It is the operational core of “advanced manufacturing,” translating Industry 4.0 rhetoric into concrete reductions in cycle time, scrap, energy use, and labor intensity on the factory floor. [^5w3ksa] [^3t1m49] [^nqi6g0]_

> “The global manufacturing automation market size was valued at USD 15.4 billion in 2025 and is projected to grow from USD 17.3 billion in 2026 to USD 32.3 billion by 2033, at a CAGR of 9.3% from 2026 to 2033.” [^puf6kf]

This profile treats manufacturing automation as the subset of industrial automation and smart manufacturing that directly targets production equipment, lines, and in-plant workflows, rather than the wider industrial or enterprise software stack. [^muaz27] [^29ouo8] [^x3240s] The analytical timeframe is roughly 2024–2034, anchored on market data that sizes the manufacturing automation market itself, plus overlapping segments in industrial automation, smart manufacturing, and supply-chain automation. [^puf6kf] [^sjd0g0] [^f1yupa] [^5w3ksa] [^3t1m49] It is worth a dedicated reference card now because market reports have started to quantify manufacturing automation separately, capital is concentrating in AI-native factory software and modular robotics platforms, and incumbent automation giants are repositioning their portfolios around software-defined, AI-enabled factories. [^puf6kf] [^sjd0g0] [^29ouo8] [^x3240s] [^05x164] [^a6sny9] [^nyr7zr] [^aprpe7]  

## What is this Market Category?

Manufacturing automation as a market category can be defined as the technologies and systems that automate, control, and optimize discrete and process manufacturing operations inside factories, with the explicit goal of reducing manual intervention in production, improving quality, lowering costs, and increasing flexibility. [^puf6kf] [^muaz27] [^x3240s] [^05x164] At its core, this includes industrial control and factory automation platforms such as programmable logic controllers (PLCs), distributed control systems (DCS), supervisory control and data acquisition ([[concepts/Market-Categories/SCADA]]), industrial robotics, and motion control hardware, usually integrated into a plant-level architecture. [^muaz27] [^29ouo8] [^x3240s] It also encompasses manufacturing execution systems (MES), industrial IoT connectivity, AI and machine-learning analytics, digital twins, and quality/predictive maintenance applications that interpret production data and orchestrate automated actions in real time. [^f1yupa] [^3t1m49] [^x3240s] [^nqi6g0] In practical terms, manufacturing automation is sold primarily to operations, engineering, and IT/OT leaders in manufacturing enterprises across sectors such as automotive, electronics, chemicals, food and beverage, energy-related manufacturing, and pharmaceuticals, often through multi-year projects delivered by global automation vendors and systems integrators. [^29ouo8] [^x3240s] [^7hue26] [^f14x4j]  

To draw a meaningful boundary, manufacturing automation excludes the broader industrial automation activities that focus on non-manufacturing verticals such as utilities, oil and gas production in the field, building automation, and grid-level energy management, even though the same vendors often cover these sectors. [^5w3ksa] [^muaz27] [^29ouo8] [^x3240s] It also typically excludes pure enterprise software used for planning and finance, such as ERP systems, HR platforms, or generic CRM tools, even when these systems integrate with factory data. [^2h4bap] [^nqi6g0] Within smart manufacturing, the category narrows in on technologies whose primary locus of value is the production line, work cells, and directly adjacent in-plant logistics, rather than upstream product design or downstream customer analytics. [^f1yupa] [^3t1m49] [^nqi6g0] Likewise, supply-chain automation that focuses mainly on outbound logistics, e-commerce fulfillment, or third-party transportation management sits at the edge of the category, included only when those functions are tightly coupled to in-plant production scheduling and material flow. [^2h4bap]  

The category’s customers usually frame problems in terms of OEE (overall equipment effectiveness), scrap rate, labor availability, downtime, energy use, and responsiveness to volatile demand, and they buy manufacturing automation solutions to address issues like predictive maintenance, in-line quality inspection, automated assembly or packaging, and real-time production planning. [^f1yupa] [^3t1m49] [^nqi6g0] AI in manufacturing, for example, is described as reading the operational signals a plant already produces—equipment, materials, quality, and demand—and turning them into earlier warnings and faster decisions, directly enabling predictive maintenance, quality inspection, production scheduling, supply-chain planning, and workforce support. [^nqi6g0] Industrial automation market reports similarly define their scope as the use of control systems such as computers, robots, and information technologies to handle industrial processes and machinery with minimal human intervention, which is the functional backbone of manufacturing automation inside plants. [^muaz27] [^b2lhf7]  

The boundary is fuzzy and disputed in several places, particularly around whether to include plant-adjacent supply-chain automation (warehouse management, inbound logistics automation) and broader smart manufacturing platforms that cover surveillance and safety, inventory and warehouse management, and quality management alongside production automation. [^f1yupa] [^2h4bap] [^3t1m49] Some analysts fold these elements into “smart manufacturing,” which they size at over USD 118.7 billion in 2024 and project to USD 391.2 billion by 2034, [^f1yupa] while others carve out a narrower manufacturing automation segment with a 2025 baseline of USD 15–16 billion and a sub-10% CAGR. [^puf6kf] [^sjd0g0] Operators also disagree on whether AI-native factory analytics platforms that do not directly control equipment should be considered manufacturing automation vendors or merely adjacent industrial analytics providers, and this dispute shows up in how category reports and capital allocators label companies like Daedalus, EthonAI, and Oden Technologies. [^a6sny9]  

## Why Now?

The manufacturing automation category is cohering and expanding now because several distinct forces have aligned across technology, economics, regulation, and capital formation, moving automation from a long-standing industrial theme into a sharper, software-defined and AI-augmented market.

One powerful force is the maturation and democratization of smart manufacturing technologies, including industrial IoT, AI and machine learning, digital twins, and robotics, which have crossed cost, scalability, and usability thresholds that make them deployable in both large enterprises and mid-market manufacturers. [^f1yupa] [^3t1m49] [^nqi6g0] Smart manufacturing market analyses describe a broad spectrum of solutions—industrial automation, MES, IIoT, AI and ML, digital twin and simulation, robotics, cybersecurity, AR/VR, energy management, and smart maintenance platforms—all integrated to optimize production efficiency, reduce operational costs, and enhance product quality. [^f1yupa] Meticulous Research notes that the smart manufacturing market was valued at USD 133.1 billion in 2023 and is expected to reach USD 733.4 billion by 2031, growing at a CAGR of 24.6%, with industrial IoT, robotics and automation, and AI-driven analytics taking significant share. [^3t1m49] This rapid expansion of enabling technologies provides the substrate on which manufacturing automation vendors can build integrated solutions that are economically attractive and technically feasible.

A second force is the steady rise of industrial automation adoption in Asia-Pacific and other manufacturing-heavy regions, which is driving global demand for factory automation solutions and setting new competitive benchmarks. [^5w3ksa] [^uespb0] [^3t1m49] ResearchAndMarkets highlights that the global industrial automation market attained USD 192.74 billion in 2024 and is expected to reach USD 439.82 billion by 2034, with Asia-Pacific anticipated to lead market growth at a CAGR of 10.6%. [^5w3ksa] MarketDataForecast similarly estimates the industrial automation market at USD 183.54 billion in 2025, growing to USD 395.02 billion by 2034 at a CAGR of 8.89%. [^uespb0] Smart manufacturing data reinforce the regional tilt, with Asia-Pacific expected to account for over 37.3% of the smart manufacturing market in 2024. [^3t1m49] As manufacturers in these regions aggressively automate to compete on quality and speed rather than low labor costs alone, global supply chains feel pressure to match their capabilities, which in turn raises demand for manufacturing automation technologies worldwide.

A third enabling condition is the integration of AI into manufacturing workflows, which has changed automation from static rule-based control to dynamic, data-driven optimization that many operators now view as indispensable. [^nqi6g0] [^a6sny9] Infor’s discussion of AI in manufacturing emphasizes that AI systems can interpret production data and act on it across the full operational lifecycle—from product design and demand planning to scheduling, production, quality, maintenance, and workforce management—detecting anomalies, forecasting what is likely to happen next, and automating routine work. [^nqi6g0] This allows manufacturers to prevent unplanned downtime, catch quality problems early, and absorb supply-chain disruption without stopping production. [^nqi6g0] Sifted’s 2024 industrial tech briefing further underscores this force, noting that “machine learning smarts are arriving on the factory floor” for quality control, equipment maintenance, training, and automation of paperwork, and that startups like Daedalus, Robovision, EthonAI, and Oden Technologies have raised significant rounds specifically to bring AI-controlled factories, computer vision intelligence, and predictive maintenance into mainstream manufacturing. [^a6sny9]

A fourth force is the structural need to digitize and automate supply chains tied to manufacturing, which binds supply-chain automation and manufacturing automation together in practice. [^2h4bap] [^nqi6g0] The supply-chain automation market, sized at USD 14.51 billion in 2025 and projected to reach USD 37.11 billion by 2033 at a CAGR of 12.5%, explicitly includes manufacturing as a major end-user, with manufacturers leveraging automation to enhance production planning, inventory control, and supplier coordination, thereby reducing lead times and improving product quality. [^2h4bap] As disruptions—from pandemics to geopolitical tensions—have exposed the fragility of manual, spreadsheet-driven supply-chain processes, manufacturers have accelerated investment in automation that spans both factory floors and adjacent logistics, increasing the salience of manufacturing automation as an integrated category. [^2h4bap] [^nqi6g0]

A fifth enabling condition is the redirection of capital toward industrial tech, evidenced by sizeable funding rounds in AI-native manufacturing platforms and modular automation providers, which signal investor belief that the category is ready for scale. [^a6sny9] [^nyr7zr] [^aprpe7] Bright Machines, for example, raised USD 126 million in a Series C round in June 2024, bringing its total funding to more than USD 400 million to push “software-defined manufacturing” and expand its intelligent assembly platforms. [^nyr7zr] Vention, a provider of plug-and-play robot automation solutions and a “Physical AI” platform for global manufacturing, raised USD 110 million backed by institutional investors, on top of earlier funding that brought total capital raised to approximately USD 155 million. [^aprpe7] Sifted’s briefing documents multiple mid-sized rounds—USD 21 million for Daedalus, USD 42 million for Robovision, USD 16.5 million Series A for EthonAI, and USD 28.5 million Series B for Oden Technologies—indicative of a broader wave of venture appetite for data-driven factory automation. [^a6sny9]  

## What's Happening?

### CAGR and TAM: The Emerging Quantitative Picture

The most direct lens on manufacturing automation’s current momentum comes from market-sizing reports that explicitly name the category. Grand View Research estimates that the global manufacturing automation market was valued at USD 15.4 billion in 2025 and will grow from USD 17.3 billion in 2026 to USD 32.3 billion by 2033, corresponding to a compound annual growth rate of 9.3% during 2026–2033. [^puf6kf] Precedence Research produces a similar but not identical sizing, calculating the global manufacturing automation market size at USD 16.30 billion in 2026 and projecting it to surpass USD 34.28 billion by 2034, expanding at a CAGR of 9.74% from 2026 to 2034. [^sjd0g0] This slight disagreement—roughly a 6% difference in 2026 baseline and a 3–4% divergence in 2033–2034 projections—illustrates both the relative nascency of the category and the sensitivity of TAM estimates to scope and methodology. [^puf6kf] [^sjd0g0]  

Regionally, Mordor Intelligence estimates that the North America manufacturing automation market will grow from USD 3.94 billion in 2025 to USD 5.72 billion by 2030, implying a CAGR of 7.71%. [^eyizp6] Precedence Research notes that Europe’s manufacturing automation market size is evaluated at USD 5.71 billion in 2026 and projected to reach around USD 12.17 billion by 2034, growing at a CAGR of 9.89%, slightly higher than global averages and pointing to strong European momentum. [^sjd0g0] These regional snapshots suggest that while manufacturing automation is smaller than the broader industrial automation or smart manufacturing markets, it is growing at a high single-digit to low double-digit pace, with particular strength in Europe and Asia-Pacific. [^sjd0g0] [^eyizp6] [^5w3ksa] [^3t1m49]  

When set against overlapping categories, the scale and velocity of manufacturing automation become clearer. The global industrial automation market, which includes manufacturing but also other sectors, is valued at USD 183.54–192.74 billion around 2024–2025 and projected to reach roughly USD 395–439.82 billion by 2034, with CAGRs ranging from 8.60% to 8.89% depending on the report. [^5w3ksa] [^uespb0] Smart manufacturing, defined more broadly to encompass automation, AI, IIoT, MES and related technologies, is sized at approximately USD 118.7 billion in 2024 by Emergen Research and projected to reach nearly USD 391.2 billion by 2034, reflecting a robust CAGR of 12.7%. [^f1yupa] Meticulous Research offers an even more aggressive forecast for smart manufacturing, projecting growth from USD 157 billion in 2024 to USD 733.4 billion by 2031 at a 24.6% CAGR, although this likely reflects a broader technology inclusion and different base-year assumptions. [^3t1m49] Supply-chain automation, adjacent but partially overlapping, is sized at USD 14.51 billion in 2025 and projected to reach USD 37.11 billion by 2033 at a 12.5% CAGR. [^2h4bap] Together, these figures show manufacturing automation as a mid-sized, high-growth subcategory within a much larger structural shift toward automated, data-driven industrial operations.

### Category Creation Events: Naming and Recognition

Manufacturing automation as a labeled category has crystallized through a combination of analyst framing, vendor repositioning, and defining product and funding events. On the analyst side, both Grand View Research and Precedence Research have issued dedicated “Manufacturing Automation Market” reports, explicitly segmenting this subset from the wider industrial automation space and publishing independent TAM and CAGR figures, which is a standard marker of category recognition in the market-research ecosystem. [^puf6kf] [^sjd0g0] Mordor Intelligence’s focus on the “North America Manufacturing Automation Market” with separate size and share analysis reinforces this signal by treating manufacturing automation as a discrete lens on industrial automation trends. [^eyizp6]  

The broader “Industrial Control & Factory Automation” framing in ResearchAndMarkets and the “Industrial Automation and Control Systems” coverage in Grand View and Precedence show incumbents and analysts converging on a taxonomy where factory automation—including manufacturing automation—is distinct from other industrial control domains. [^29ouo8] [^x3240s] [^05x164] In these reports, companies like Siemens, Schneider Electric, ABB, Rockwell Automation, and Emerson are profiled explicitly as leaders in industrial automation and control systems, with special emphasis on their manufacturing-focused offerings such as PLCs, DCSs, industrial software, and smart factory solutions. [^29ouo8] [^x3240s] [^05x164] For example, the industrial control and factory automation insights highlight Siemens and Schneider Electric as “primary orchestrators,” both having transitioned from hardware suppliers to digital platform companies, with Siemens Xcelerator and Schneider’s EcoStruxure platforms providing integrated automation and software stacks for factories. [^29ouo8]  

On the vendor side, product and positioning moves have played a defining role in shaping the category’s narrative. Siemens has built its Xcelerator open digital business platform around DCS/PLC services, digital twin simulation, and industrial IoT analytics, explicitly positioning itself as delivering the “industry’s most complete automation services ecosystem” across 190+ countries. [^7hue26] Rockwell Automation has articulated a “Connected Enterprise” vision and deepened its focus on smart manufacturing through its FactoryTalk software ecosystem, autonomous operations capabilities, and investments in software-defined automation and digital twin technologies. [^x3240s] [^7hue26] In October 2025, Rockwell launched the ControlLogix 5590 controller as a next-generation industrial control product, an event highlighted in Precedence Research as ushering in “a new era in industrial control,” which signals both technological maturation and marketing emphasis around advanced manufacturing automation. [^05x164]  

In the startup ecosystem, Bright Machines’ self-description as an innovator in “intelligent, software-defined manufacturing” and its substantial Series C funding round in 2024 anchor a narrative where manufacturing becomes programmable and composable via software rather than solely via fixed hardware. [^nyr7zr] Vention’s emphasis on “Physical AI” and plug-and-play automation solutions for industrial SMBs, with pre-engineered automation applications delivered through a software platform, extends this narrative into mid-market and smaller manufacturers. [^aprpe7] Sifted’s coverage of AI manufacturing startups such as Daedalus, Robovision, EthonAI, Oden Technologies, and DarwinAI, along with budding predictive-maintenance and safety startups like Ai:omatic Solutions, Tomorrow Things, and Buddewise, frames “AI-controlled factories” and “computer vision intelligence for industrial machinery” as the latest wave in manufacturing automation. [^a6sny9]

### Capital Concentration: Where the Money Is Flowing

Capital in manufacturing automation is currently concentrating in three visible bands: incumbent-led software and services expansions, mid-stage AI-native manufacturing platforms, and early-stage predictive maintenance and quality startups. On the incumbent side, industrial automation services represent a growing revenue and investment focus for companies like Siemens, ABB, Schneider Electric, Rockwell Automation, Honeywell, Emerson, Yokogawa, Mitsubishi Electric, Johnson Controls, and Bosch Rexroth. [^7hue26] [^f14x4j] [^4vzo7q] MarketResearchFuture notes that Siemens leads the industrial automation services market with its Xcelerator platform integrating DCS/PLC services, digital twin simulation, and industrial IoT analytics. [^7hue26] Fortune Business Insights describes Siemens, ABB, Schneider Electric, Rockwell Automation, Honeywell, Emerson, Yokogawa, Mitsubishi Electric, Johnson Controls, and Bosch Rexroth as major companies operating in the industrial automation services market, reporting that competitive positioning is now shaped by capabilities in industrial system integration, robotics implementation, predictive maintenance services, and smart manufacturing transformation. [^f14x4j] Mordor Intelligence adds that ABB, Siemens, and Schneider Electric leverage installed base depth and global service centers to defend share, while second-tier players like Yokogawa, Emerson, and Rockwell Automation diversify into software and analytics platforms, such as ABB Ability, Siemens Industrial Edge, Schneider’s EcoStruxure Service Bureau, and Rockwell’s MES and diagnostic suites. [^4vzo7q]  

In the challenger band, Bright Machines’ USD 126 million Series C round in June 2024 stands out as a landmark funding event, led by BlackRock with participation from NVIDIA, Microsoft, Eclipse, Jabil, and Shinhan Securities, plus USD 20 million in venture debt from J.P. Morgan, bringing total funding to over USD 400 million. [^nyr7zr] This round is notable both for its size and its investor mix, which combines institutional capital, strategic investors from both cloud (Microsoft) and chip (NVIDIA) ecosystems, and manufacturing service providers like Jabil, signaling a strong belief in software-defined manufacturing platforms. [^nyr7zr] Vention’s USD 110 million financing to accelerate “Physical AI deployment across global manufacturing” similarly indicates capital concentration in flexible, pre-engineered automation platforms that can be configured and deployed quickly, particularly for robotics automation in industrial SMBs. [^aprpe7] With total funding of roughly USD 155 million and annual revenue around USD 60 million, Vention sits in a category of well-funded scale-ups that have both capital and traction in the manufacturing automation space. [^aprpe7]  

Sifted’s industrial tech briefing shows a parallel wave of mid-sized rounds across European and transatlantic AI manufacturing startups. Daedalus raised USD 21 million in February 2024 to advance AI-controlled factories. [^a6sny9] Robovision secured USD 42 million in March to bring computer vision intelligence to industrial machinery. [^a6sny9] EthonAI raised USD 16.5 million in a Series A round in May for software that predicts maintenance needs and catches quality issues early. [^a6sny9] Oden Technologies attracted USD 28.5 million in a Series B round in April for its factory analytics software. [^a6sny9] Ai:omatic Solutions raised EUR 2 million in January for predictive maintenance systems, Tomorrow Things raised EUR 1.5 million in March for similar products, and Buddewise raised EUR 3.5 million in March for safety tools that log urgent events such as forklifts entering restricted areas. [^a6sny9] Taken together, these rounds show that while the absolute dollar amounts are smaller than incumbents’ capex, the venture capital ecosystem is heavily investing in AI-centric manufacturing automation plays, particularly in Europe, which can reshape the competitive dynamics as these companies mature.

## Frontier and Open Questions

The frontier of manufacturing automation is defined by unresolved questions about how deeply AI and software can penetrate production control, how far category boundaries should stretch into adjacent domains, and how value will be captured between incumbents and emerging players.

One pressing question is how far AI-driven decision-making will displace traditional deterministic control in factory operations, and whether “AI-controlled factories” like those envisioned by Daedalus will become mainstream or remain niche. Innovators such as Daedalus, EthonAI, Ai:omatic Solutions, and Tomorrow Things are experimenting with machine-learning models that learn from production data and suggest or even implement control actions, but incumbents like Siemens, ABB, Schneider Electric, and Rockwell Automation must decide how aggressively to incorporate such AI agents into their PLCs, DCSs, and industrial software without compromising safety and reliability. [^29ouo8] [^x3240s] [^7hue26] [^4vzo7q] [^nqi6g0] [^a6sny9]  

A second open question concerns the optimal division of labor between edge computing and cloud-based analytics in manufacturing automation. Industrial IoT and smart manufacturing reports describe architectures where data is collected at the edge but analyzed centrally, yet AI in manufacturing increasingly relies on time-series forecasting and computer vision that may require low-latency edge execution. [^f1yupa] [^3t1m49] [^nqi6g0] Incumbents with industrial edge platforms, like Siemens Industrial Edge and ABB Ability, and challengers such as Bright Machines, Vention, and Oden Technologies are likely to shape this balance by demonstrating where edge AI provides tangible benefits versus where cloud analytics suffice. [^4vzo7q] [^nyr7zr] [^aprpe7] [^a6sny9]  

A third frontier question is how manufacturing automation will integrate with or reshape supply-chain automation. Verified Market Reports highlights manufacturing as a key end-user of supply-chain automation, using it for production planning, inventory control, and supplier coordination, which suggests that factory-specific automation and broader supply-chain automation may merge into unified “manufacturing and supply-chain operations” platforms. [^2h4bap] Incumbents like Schneider Electric and Honeywell, with strengths in energy and building management, and challengers like Bright Machines and Vention, with flexible automation platforms, will influence whether the category boundary moves to explicitly include warehouse automation, inbound logistics, and real-time supplier collaboration under the manufacturing automation umbrella. [^29ouo8] [^x3240s] [^f14x4j] [^nyr7zr] [^aprpe7]  

A fourth question involves workforce transformation: to what extent will manufacturing automation augment human workers versus replace them, and how will safety and training tools evolve. Infor notes that AI in manufacturing can provide workforce support, such as digital assistants and generative AI that turn engineering changes into work instructions, while startups like Buddewise focus on safety monitoring and urgent event logging. [^nqi6g0] [^a6sny9] Innovators and challengers working on workforce-facing tools, along with incumbents that integrate AR/VR and training modules into their platforms, will determine whether manufacturing automation becomes a driver of better jobs and safer workplaces or primarily a labor-reduction instrument. [^f1yupa] [^3t1m49] [^nqi6g0] [^a6sny9]  

A fifth unresolved issue is the degree to which manufacturing automation should be defined as a hardware-centric category versus a software-centric one. Traditional industrial automation markets are anchored on control hardware, robots, and instrumentation, whereas smart manufacturing and AI manufacturing emphasize software platforms, analytics, and digital twins. ## Adjacent Concepts and Categories

Manufacturing automation sits at the nexus of several adjacent concepts and market categories that operators should consider when building mental models of the space. Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) is a foundational concept underpinning manufacturing automation, as it provides connectivity and data collection from equipment, sensors, and production lines, enabling analytics and AI-driven control. [^29ouo8] [^x3240s] [^7hue26] [^nqi6g0] Robotics and automation, especially industrial robots and collaborative robots, are adjacent hardware categories that are increasingly orchestrated by software from manufacturing automation vendors and challengers such as ABB, FANUC, Bright Machines, and Vention.
