# Snapshot

_**Physical AI** is the emerging category of AI systems that sense, reason about, and act in the physical world through robots, machines, and embodied devices. The category is coalescing around the convergence of foundation models, robotics, simulation, edge computing, and real-world telemetry._

> “Physical AI is a $40B+ market by 2029.” — IDC, based on its 2025 robotics and operations research. [^3kmmxx]

This profile captures the category as of **October 2026**, when “physical AI,” “embodied AI,” and “robot foundation models” are increasingly being used to describe overlapping markets. The category is worth tracking because major technology companies, robotics manufacturers, and heavily funded startups are converging on a common thesis: general-purpose AI becomes substantially more valuable when it can perceive and manipulate physical environments. [^3kmmxx]

## What is this Market Category?

Physical AI comprises software, models, infrastructure, and machines that integrate artificial intelligence into physical systems so they can perceive environments, interpret tasks, make decisions, and execute actions in the real world. [^3kmmxx] Products are sold primarily to manufacturers, logistics operators, warehouses, healthcare providers, retailers, laboratories, and—in some cases—consumers. The category includes humanoid robots, collaborative robots, autonomous mobile robots, delivery and cleaning robots, robot-control software, simulation environments, edge-compute stacks, perception systems, and general-purpose “robot brains.” [^3kmmxx]

The category solves problems that conventional automation handles poorly: unstructured environments, variable objects and tasks, natural-language instructions, long-tail edge cases, and workflows that change faster than fixed industrial programming can accommodate. Its central commercial promise is to make physical labor more flexible, general-purpose, and economically deployable. IDC describes the category as spanning **platforms and software, infrastructure, services, and devices**, rather than only robot hardware. [^3kmmxx]

Physical AI excludes ordinary software agents that have no physical sensing or actuation, conventional industrial robots executing tightly scripted movements, and generic computer-vision systems that only observe without controlling a physical process. It also excludes most autonomous vehicles and drones if they are treated as separate vertical markets, although their underlying perception, planning, simulation, and control technologies are materially adjacent.

The boundary is disputed: some analysts use “physical AI” broadly for any AI embedded in machines, while others reserve it for systems with embodied, adaptive, and increasingly general-purpose behavior; IDC’s broader framing includes humanoids, cobots, autonomous mobile robots, and delivery and cleaning robots. [^3kmmxx]

## Why Now?

- **Foundation models are moving from language and vision into action.** Physical AI systems increasingly combine visual perception, reasoning, and control rather than relying exclusively on hand-coded task programs; IDC defines embodied-AI robots as systems capable of autonomous perception, learning, decision-making, and action in physical environments. [^n2ec18]

- **The economics of robotics software are expanding beyond single-purpose automation.** Transformer- and diffusion-based models are being applied to robotic perception and action, supporting a software market that MarketIntelo estimates reached $4.5 billion in 2025 and is growing at 38.0% CAGR. [^23t2po]

- **Edge computing and improved visual systems are lowering deployment friction.** IDC says breakthrough advances in AI models, visual systems, and edge computing could triple the number of robot applications, with manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and services as major deployment areas. [^n2ec18]

- **Large technology companies are entering the embodiment race.** Meta established a division within Reality Labs to develop AI-powered humanoid robots and physical-world AI, joining Nvidia-backed [[Tooling/Hardware/Robotics/Figure AI|Figure AI]] and [[organizations/Tesla|Tesla]] in the race to commercialize humanoid systems. [^2qznwy]

- **Labor shortages and repetitive or hazardous work create a clear initial wedge.** Apptronik is targeting warehouse and manufacturing tasks with Apollo, while Figure and other humanoid developers are positioning robots for logistics, manufacturing, and household work. [^j79yha] [^f3x34y]

- **Capital markets have established credible funding precedents.** Figure’s 2025 financing valued the company at $39 billion, while [[Physical Intelligence]] reportedly raised $600 million at a $5.6 billion valuation and Apptronik raised $350 million to scale production. [^f3x34y] [^7cmsdl] [^j79yha]

- **Fortune Business Insights, “Physical AI Market Size & Forecast Report, 2034,” 2026:** Fortune Business Insights estimates **$33.42 billion in 2025**, growing to **$294.07 billion by 2034** at a **27.5% CAGR**; it reports robotics as the largest segment in 2025, with a 46.9% share. [^2fg0mt]

### Category creation events

- **IDC formally positioned Physical AI as a distinct market category.** IDC’s 2026 research page describes Physical AI as AI embedded directly into robots and machines so they can sense surroundings, decide, and act, and organizes the market across platforms, infrastructure, services, and devices. [^3kmmxx]

- **Meta’s entry validated the category beyond traditional robotics.** Reuters reported that Meta was establishing an AI-humanoid-robotics division within Reality Labs, with plans involving robot hardware as well as AI, sensors, and software that could power robots made by other companies. [^2qznwy]

- **Figure’s $39 billion financing crystallized humanoid robotics as a frontier-AI market.** Reuters reported that Figure’s Series C was led by Parkway Venture Capital and included Nvidia, Intel Capital, LG Technology Ventures, Salesforce, T-Mobile Ventures, and Qualcomm Ventures. [^f3x34y]

- **Physical Intelligence helped define the “general-purpose robot brain” thesis.** Bloomberg reported that the startup raised $600 million at a $5.6 billion valuation for software intended to help robots learn a wide range of tasks. [^7cmsdl]

### Capital concentration

- **Humanoid platforms are attracting the largest disclosed rounds.** Figure’s 2025 Series C exceeded $1 billion and valued the company at $39 billion, with participation from strategic technology and industrial investors. [^f3x34y]

- **Manufacturing and logistics are attracting scale-up capital.** Apptronik raised $350 million led by B Capital and Capital Factory, with participation from Google, to scale Apollo humanoid robots for warehouses and manufacturing plants. [^j79yha]

- **Robot-foundation-model software is becoming a separate capital pool.** Physical Intelligence reportedly raised $600 million at a $5.6 billion valuation, while Skild AI was reported to have raised $500 million at a $4.7 billion valuation and was subsequently in discussions for a potential investment exceeding $1 billion at a $14 billion valuation. [^7cmsdl] [^8sappm]

- **Strategic investors are concentrating around the infrastructure stack.** Nvidia appears repeatedly across Figure, Physical Intelligence-related financing, and Skild AI reporting, while Google invested in Apptronik and Meta is developing its own physical-AI capabilities. [^f3x34y] [^j79yha] [^7cmsdl] [^8sappm]

## Frontier and Open Questions

- **Will Physical AI become a software-platform market or remain dominated by vertically integrated robot manufacturers?** Nvidia, Physical Intelligence, and [[Skild AI]] are pursuing reusable infrastructure and model layers, while Tesla, Figure, and Apptronik are pursuing tightly integrated hardware-and-software systems. [^2qznwy] [^f3x34y] [^j79yha] [^7cmsdl] [^8sappm]

- **Can foundation models generalize across embodiments, tasks, and environments without requiring uneconomic amounts of real-world data?** Physical Intelligence and Skild AI are the clearest challengers to single-robot architectures, but the supplied sources do not yet establish durable cross-platform generalization. [^7cmsdl] [^8sappm]

- **Will humanoids be the dominant form factor, or will specialized mobile and industrial robots capture most early revenue?** IDC’s category includes humanoids, cobots, autonomous mobile robots, and delivery and cleaning robots, while Fortune Business Insights reports that robotics broadly—not humanoids alone—held the largest 2025 share. [^3kmmxx] [^2fg0mt]

- **Does the category include autonomous vehicles, drones, and industrial control systems?** A broad definition includes any AI embedded in a machine that senses and acts, while a narrower definition limits Physical AI to general-purpose embodied agents; IDC’s broad four-layer framing favors inclusion, but market reports vary materially in scope. [^3kmmxx] [^n2ec18] [^gqx4r4]

- **Can enterprise customers achieve reliable unit economics before humanoid hardware reaches mass production?** Apptronik is targeting warehouses and manufacturing, Figure is targeting logistics, manufacturing, and household tasks, and the outcome will depend on uptime, safety, integration cost, and labor substitution rather than model capability alone. [^f3x34y] [^j79yha]

- **Will incumbents capture the value through compute, cloud, industrial distribution, and installed bases?** Nvidia, Google, Meta, Tesla, Amazon, Siemens, and ABB can distribute physical-AI capabilities through existing platforms, while challengers must prove that their model or robot advantage is strong enough to overcome incumbent channels. [^2qznwy] [^f3x34y] [^j79yha] [^8sappm]

## Adjacent Concepts and Categories

- **Embodied AI** — the broader technical concept covering AI systems that learn and act through physical bodies and environments.
- **Robot Foundation Models** — reusable models intended to control multiple robot types and perform many tasks.
- **[[concepts/Market-Categories/Humanoid Robots|Humanoid Robotics]]** — the most visible physical-AI form factor and a major destination for current capital.
- **[[concepts/Market-Categories/Industrial Automations|Industrial Automation]]** — the incumbent market where physical AI must prove reliability, safety, and return on investment.
- **Autonomous Mobile Robots** — adjacent warehouse, factory, hospital, delivery, and cleaning systems already operating in commercial environments.
- **Simulation-to-Real Transfer** — the technique of training or validating robotic behavior in simulated environments before deployment in the physical world.
- **Real-World Telemetry** — sensor, interaction, and operational data generated by physical systems and used to improve models.
- **[[concepts/Explainers for AI/Edge AI|Edge AI]]** — the compute and inference layer that allows robots and machines to respond locally under latency, connectivity, privacy, and safety constraints.
