# Electric Vehicles

Electric vehicles constitute one of the fastest-scaling industrial transitions in modern history, driven by converging forces in technology, regulation, capital formation, and consumer behavior that are reshaping the global automotive and energy systems simultaneously. [^24r6sn] [^t9gvvn] [^r2unr3] [^d3sozx] [^6079if] Drawing on recent data from the International Energy Agency’s *Global EV Outlook 2024* and market sizing work from Grand View Research, this profile characterizes electric vehicles as a distinct market category, traces its enabling conditions and current momentum, and surfaces the key corporate actors and open questions that an innovation-oriented analyst must track today. [^24r6sn] [^t9gvvn] [^r2unr3] [^4y5iiw] [^6079if] [^9iuy3a]  

## Snapshot

_The electric vehicles category captures all road-going vehicles in which electricity is a primary propulsion energy, spanning battery-electric, plug-in hybrid, and fuel-cell architectures, and increasingly encompassing the surrounding infrastructure, batteries, and software needed to operate them at scale. [^k6qim0] [^fh4aq2] At a time when EVs have crossed from niche to mass market in several major economies, the category has become a central lens for understanding decarbonization, industrial policy, and the future of mobility. [^24r6sn] [^t9gvvn] [^r2unr3] [^d3sozx]_

> “The global electric vehicle market size is estimated to reach USD 12,600.45 billion by 2033, expanding at a CAGR of 26.7% from 2026 to 2033, according to a new report by Grand View Research, Inc.” [^6079if]  

This profile treats electric vehicles as a global category over roughly the 2020–2035 window, aligning with the [[International Energy Agency]]’s projections of EV deployment, charging infrastructure, and battery demand to 2035. [^24r6sn] [^t9gvvn] [^7xj58a] [^w5qvza] The period from 2020 onward captures the acceleration in EV sales, with nearly one in five cars sold worldwide in 2023 being electric and sales expected to reach around 17 million in 2024, more than one in five of all cars sold. [^r2unr3] [^d3sozx] [^4y5iiw] It is worth a reference card now because EVs have moved beyond early adoption into a phase of structural impact, with substantial effects on oil demand, power systems, industrial supply chains, and consumer expectations, and with a rapidly evolving competitive landscape where incumbents, challengers, and innovators are all active. [^24r6sn] [^t9gvvn] [^r2unr3] [^d3sozx] [^4y5iiw] [^w5qvza] [^6079if]  

## What is this Market Category?

The electric vehicles market category can be defined as the collection of products, services, and industrial activities directly related to vehicles in which electricity, stored or generated on-board, is a primary energy source for propulsion, displacing or substantially reducing direct combustion of fossil fuels. [^24r6sn] [^t9gvvn] [^k6qim0] [^fh4aq2] At the core of the category are battery-electric vehicles (BEVs), which are powered purely by rechargeable battery packs that draw energy from the electricity grid and drive one or more electric motors, with no internal combustion engine on board. [^k6qim0] [^fh4aq2] Plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs) and hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs) occupy an intermediate space; in HEVs and PHEVs an electric motor and battery pack assist or intermittently replace the internal combustion engine, while PHEVs add the ability to plug in for charging and offer longer all-electric range. [^k6qim0] [^fh4aq2] Fuel cell electric vehicles (FCEVs) also fall within the category to the extent that they convert hydrogen to electricity in a fuel cell stack and use that electricity, along with a battery pack, to power electric motors, even though their primary fuel is hydrogen rather than grid electricity. [^k6qim0] [^fh4aq2]  

Products in this category aim to solve several intertwined problems for both individual and institutional customers, including reduction of greenhouse gas and local pollutant emissions from road transport, decreased dependence on oil, lower operating and maintenance costs, and improved vehicle performance and user experience through smooth torque delivery and integrated software. [^24r6sn] [^t9gvvn] [^r2unr3] [^zvci67] [^w5qvza] Customers range from private households purchasing passenger cars to commercial fleets, public transit agencies operating buses, logistics firms deploying electric light commercial vehicles, and even two- and three-wheeler users in emerging economies. [^24r6sn] [^t9gvvn] [^r2unr3] [^4y5iiw] [^zvci67] The category also implicitly includes, as enabling components, the batteries and power electronics that make electric propulsion feasible, as well as the charging infrastructure that allows routine use, although in strict taxonomies these are sometimes treated as adjacent markets rather than core. [^24r6sn] [^t9gvvn] [^7xj58a] [^w5qvza] [^rba3xf] [^a6f8uh]  

Importantly, the category excludes several intuitive but distinct areas that a naive reader might incorrectly group under electric vehicles. Grid-scale energy storage systems, although based on similar lithium-ion chemistries and even housed in battery packs, are deployed as static assets on the power grid and are better treated under an energy storage category, not the vehicle category. [^rba3xf] [^a6f8uh] Similarly, low-speed micromobility devices such as electric bicycles and scooters, while electrically powered, serve different use cases, regulatory frameworks, and price points and are typically analyzed in separate micromobility or urban mobility categories. [^24r6sn] [^t9gvvn] The category also generally excludes conventional hybrid vehicles that cannot plug in to charge from the grid, even though agencies like NHTSA classify them as hybrid-electric vehicles, because they rely predominantly on gasoline and use electricity only for short-range assistance. [^k6qim0]  

There is, however, a fuzzy boundary at several edges that has sparked real disputes among operators and analysts. One disputed edge concerns whether PHEVs and strong HEVs should count fully as electric vehicles in market statistics, given that they can operate for substantial periods on gasoline; some analysts and policymakers emphasize BEVs and FCEVs as the “true” EV core, while others include PHEVs as transitional forms. [^t9gvvn] [^r2unr3] [^k6qim0] [^fh4aq2] Another boundary debate concerns whether to include two- and three-wheeled vehicles and electric buses within the same EV category as passenger cars, since their adoption patterns, cost structures, and regulatory drivers differ markedly across regions. [^24r6sn] [^t9gvvn] [^r2unr3] As the category matures, these boundary debates shape both reported market sizes and strategic decisions by firms and regulators.  

## Why Now?

### Technological Unlocks: Battery Cost and Performance Declines

One of the most important forces making electric vehicles a coherent and rapidly expanding category is the dramatic decline in lithium-ion battery costs over the past decade and a half. [^w5qvza] [^rba3xf] [^a6f8uh] According to analysis summarised by J.P. Morgan using BloombergNEF data, average lithium-ion battery pack costs have fallen from roughly USD 1,474 per kilowatt-hour in 2010 to about USD 108 per kilowatt-hour in the mid-2020s, a drop of more than 90% that fundamentally alters the economics of EVs. [^rba3xf] [^a6f8uh] The same analysis notes that prices are roughly 36% below 2020 levels and are expected to fall another 33% by 2030, with learning-curve trends implying pack costs around USD 72 per kilowatt-hour by 2030 and USD 57 per kilowatt-hour by 2035. [^rba3xf] [^a6f8uh] This decline is driven by scaling manufacturing capacity, improvements in cell chemistry and pack design, and learning-by-doing across supply chains, and it enables automakers to offer EVs at prices closer to internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicles while maintaining acceptable range. [^w5qvza] [^rba3xf] [^a6f8uh] As the International Energy Agency’s *Global EV Outlook 2024* notes, the pace of the transition to electric vehicles hinges critically on affordability, and falling battery prices are a key input into that affordability. [^4y5iiw] [^w5qvza] [^rba3xf] [^a6f8uh]  

### Regulatory and Policy Shifts

A second major enabling force is the wave of regulatory and policy measures aimed at decarbonizing transport and reducing local air pollution, which have created predictable demand and investment incentives for electric vehicles. [^24r6sn] [^t9gvvn] [^zvci67] The IEA’s scenarios in the *Global EV Outlook 2024*—including the Stated Policies Scenario (STEPS), the Announced Pledges Scenario (APS), and the Net Zero Emissions (NZE) Scenario—explicitly link EV adoption trajectories to government commitments on emissions reduction and energy security. [^t9gvvn] [^zvci67] In all these scenarios, EV deployment is accelerated in jurisdictions that adopt stringent fuel economy standards, zero-emission vehicle mandates, purchase subsidies, and infrastructure support, with China, the European Union, and the United States being prominent examples. [^24r6sn] [^t9gvvn] [^r2unr3] [^d3sozx] [^4y5iiw] The Outlook emphasizes that in the STEPS, based on today’s policy settings, the stock of EVs (excluding two- and three-wheelers) grows from less than 45 million in 2023 to 250 million in 2030 and 525 million in 2035, implying that more than one in four vehicles on the road will be electric by 2035. [^t9gvvn] In the more ambitious NZE Scenario, faster policy action drives EV stock to 790 million by 2035, with average annual growth of 27%. [^t9gvvn] [^zvci67] These policy trajectories provide automakers and investors with a clearer signal that electric vehicles are not a short-lived niche but an enduring policy-backed direction of travel.  

### Capital Formation and New Industrial Actors

A third force is the formation of capital around EVs and their supply chains, including both private equity and public funding, which has created large, specialized players in batteries, recycling, and charging infrastructure. [^w5qvza] [^6079if] [^9iuy3a] [^rba3xf] [^l62roi] Redwood Materials, founded by a Tesla cofounder, offers a case study: as reported in its own news materials, the company raised over USD 1 billion in a Series D investment round to expand its EV battery materials business, and later received a conditional commitment for a USD 2 billion loan from the U.S. Department of Energy to scale domestic battery material production. [^l62roi] These capital injections support not only EV manufacturing but also the upstream supply of cathode and anode materials, battery recycling facilities, and closed-loop systems, all of which help alleviate concerns about critical mineral constraints. [^w5qvza] [^rba3xf] [^l62roi] At the vehicle level, companies like BYD and Tesla have used access to capital markets to scale production capacity, develop new models, and vertically integrate battery manufacturing, as evidenced by BYD’s ability to sell more than 4.27 million vehicles in 2024, a 41.3% increase over 2023. [^droz5b] [^e08735] Such capital concentration, in turn, attracts complementary investments in charging networks and grid upgrades, reinforcing EVs as a central category for investors focused on climate and industrial transformation. [^7xj58a] [^w5qvza] [^6079if] [^9iuy3a] [^rba3xf] [^l62roi]  

### Consumer Behavior Shifts and Market Maturation

A fourth enabling condition is the shift in consumer behavior as EVs move beyond early adopters to mainstream buyers, facilitated by broader model availability, improved performance, and growing used-car markets. [^r2unr3] [^4y5iiw] The IEA reports that almost 14 million new electric cars were registered globally in 2023, bringing the total number of electric cars on the road to about 40 million, and that electric cars accounted for roughly 18% of all car sales that year. [^r2unr3] [^wba5ai] [^4y5iiw] In the first quarter of 2024, electric car sales grew by about 25% compared with the same period in 2023, and the IEA estimates that total electric car sales could reach around 17 million in 2024, more than one in five cars sold worldwide. [^r2unr3] [^d3sozx] [^4y5iiw] As EVs become a mass-market product, the number of available electric car models has approached 600, with around two-thirds being large vehicles and SUVs, reflecting manufacturers’ efforts to meet diverse consumer preferences. [^r2unr3] The development of used EV markets is also crucial; in 2023, the market size for used electric cars was around 800,000 in China, 400,000 in the United States, and over 450,000 across major European countries, providing more affordable entry points and reinforcing consumer confidence. [^4y5iiw] These behavioral shifts reduce perceived risk and normalize EV ownership, supporting a self-reinforcing cycle of adoption and infrastructure deployment.  

### Software, Data, and Industrial-AI Integration

A fifth force, especially relevant to the category’s tagging under *Advanced-Manufacturing*, *Industrial-AI*, and *Industrial-Software*, is the integration of software and data analytics into EV design, operation, and ecosystem management. [^dep90i] [^w5qvza] [^rba3xf] Electric vehicles are inherently more software-defined than traditional cars, with battery management systems, power electronics control, and connectivity enabling remote diagnostics, over-the-air updates, and advanced driver assistance features. [^dep90i] [^w5qvza] The EV industry has seen rapid adoption of digital tools to optimize battery usage, forecast charging demand, and integrate EVs into power grid operations, with industrial AI techniques applied to predictive maintenance and fleet optimization. [^dep90i] [^w5qvza] [^rba3xf] As J.P. Morgan’s discussion of energy storage notes, batteries are becoming the new backbone of power grids, and managing them requires sophisticated software and analytics; these capabilities cross over directly into EVs, where aggregated vehicle batteries may act as flexible demand or even distributed storage. [^rba3xf] [^a6f8uh] This software-centricity lowers barriers for new entrants with strong digital capabilities and contributes to a perception of EVs not merely as hardware products but as platforms for ongoing software and services, cementing the category’s coherence for technology and industrial-strategy analysts. [^dep90i] [^w5qvza] [^rba3xf]  

## What’s Happening?

### CAGR and TAM: Divergent Market Sizing Views

The current momentum of the electric vehicles category is captured in both deployment statistics and market-sizing analyses, which, while broadly aligned on rapid growth, differ in their precise estimates of total addressable market (TAM) and compound annual growth rate (CAGR). [^t9gvvn] [^r2unr3] [^d3sozx] [^4y5iiw] [^6079if] [^9iuy3a] Grand View Research estimates that the global electric vehicle market size will reach USD 12,600.45 billion by 2033, expanding at a CAGR of 26.7% from 2026 to 2033. [^6079if] This figure appears to encompass a broad view of EV-related economic activity, potentially including vehicles, charging, and energy services, and reflects a top-down valuation of the economic transformation around electric mobility. [^6079if] In contrast, Grand View Research’s report on the electric mobility market—which includes broader e-mobility modes—values the global electric mobility market at USD 109.2 billion in 2022 and projects it to grow to USD 325.6 billion by 2030, at a CAGR of 14.6% from 2023 to 2030. [^9iuy3a] The discrepancy between a 26.7% CAGR and a 14.6% CAGR across overlapping segments underscores methodological differences, such as what product categories are included, whether vehicle hardware alone is counted or services and infrastructure are added, and which geographic regions are covered. [^6079if] [^9iuy3a]  

From a deployment standpoint, the IEA’s *Global EV Outlook 2024* offers a more technology-centric measure of momentum by projecting EV stock and sales shares across scenarios. [^24r6sn] [^t9gvvn] [^r2unr3] [^4y5iiw] In the Stated Policies Scenario (STEPS), the stock of EVs across all modes except two- and three-wheelers grows from less than 45 million in 2023 to 250 million in 2030 and reaches 525 million in 2035, implying an average annual growth rate of about 23% over 2023–2035. [^t9gvvn] In the Announced Pledges Scenario (APS), which assumes stricter policy commitments, the EV stock reaches 585 million by 2035, about 10% higher than STEPS, with the fleet being 30% electric by that date and an average annual growth of 24%. [^t9gvvn] In the Net Zero Emissions (NZE) Scenario, EV stock increases even more rapidly, reaching 790 million vehicles by 2035, corresponding to a 27% average annual growth rate over 2023–2035. [^t9gvvn] [^zvci67] These deployment figures reinforce the notion of EVs as a category experiencing sustained high double-digit growth, comparable to or exceeding many classic technology categories, with credible policy-backed trajectories to hundreds of millions of vehicles.  

Sales shares further illuminate the category’s march toward dominance in new vehicle sales. The IEA reports that nearly one in five cars sold in 2023 was electric, with about 14 million electric car registrations, and projects that electric car sales will reach around 17 million in 2024, accounting for more than one in five cars sold worldwide. [^r2unr3] [^d3sozx] [^4y5iiw] In the STEPS scenario, EV sales (excluding two- and three-wheelers) are expected to reach almost 45 million in 2030 and close to 65 million in 2035, with the sales share rising from 15% in 2023 to almost 40% in 2030 and over 50% in 2035. [^t9gvvn] For light-duty vehicles specifically, the IEA anticipates that global electric light-duty vehicle (LDV) sales will reach a 40% share in 2030 and nearly 55% in 2035 based on current policy settings, with an electric LDV fleet exceeding 565 million vehicles by 2035. [^t9gvvn] These statistics suggest that during the 2020s EVs are moving from a minority share of new sales to a majority in several major markets, which in turn drives broader TAM expansion as complementary infrastructure and services are built.  

### Category Creation Events: Reports, Rivalries, and Failures

Several defining events over the past few years have crystallized electric vehicles as a standalone category and signaled its recognition by incumbents and regulators. One such event is the annual publication of the IEA’s *Global EV Outlook*, which has become a reference report for policymakers, analysts, and firms, and whose 2024 edition extends projections to 2035 and covers deployment, charging infrastructure, battery demand, and investment trends in both major and emerging markets. [^24r6sn] The formal naming and regular updating of this report have given the EV category analytical legitimacy, akin to how Gartner or IDC reports solidify software categories. [^24r6sn] [^t9gvvn] [^r2unr3] [^4y5iiw] The 2024 Outlook’s headline that global electric car sales are set to remain robust, reaching around 17 million by the end of the year and representing more than one in five cars sold worldwide, has been widely cited as a marker of EVs’ transition from niche to mass-market status. [^d3sozx] [^4y5iiw]  

Another category-creating dynamic is the rivalry and comparative reporting between leading EV manufacturers, particularly Tesla and BYD, which frames EVs as a distinct competitive arena. As Motor1 notes in its comparison of 2024 sales, Tesla delivered about 1.79 million vehicles in 2024, slightly below its 1.81 million record in 2023, while BYD sold 1,764,992 battery-electric vehicles in 2024, up 12% from 1.57 million in 2023. [^e08735] Motor1 further points out that BYD’s total vehicle sales exceeded 4.25 million in 2024, with plug-in hybrids accounting for 2,485,378 of those units, or about 58.5% of total sales, reflecting BYD’s dual focus on BEVs and PHEVs. [^e08735] ET Auto underscores that BYD’s total vehicle sales in 2024 were 4,272,145 units, up 41.3% from 3,024,417 units in 2023, and notes that BYD’s quarterly revenue surpassed Tesla’s for the first time during the third quarter of 2024. [^droz5b] These comparative sales and revenue narratives, widely reported in industry media, highlight EV-specific metrics—BEV versus PHEV shares, total EV volumes—that differentiate the EV category from traditional auto reporting and make it salient for investors.  

A more sobering but equally revealing category moment is the bankruptcy of Proterra, a prominent electric bus manufacturer, which illustrates both the risks and the growing systemic importance of EVs in public transport. As reported by the *Colorado Springs Gazette*, Mountain Metropolitan Transit acquired four electric buses from Proterra, but only two remained operational a year and a half into service due to maintenance issues and parts delays linked to Proterra’s Chapter 11 bankruptcy and sale process. [^m9hso5] The article notes that Proterra entered Chapter 11 and parts availability became a major problem, leaving transit authorities uncertain about warranties and ongoing support as Proterra’s assets were sold to multiple companies, including Volvo Battery Solutions LLC and Phoenix Motor Inc. [^m9hso5] This failure, alongside the successes of firms like BYD and Tesla, marks EVs as a category undergoing Darwinian selection, where business model and capital structure missteps can lead to collapse even amid strong category-level growth.  

### Capital Concentration: Batteries, Recycling, and Regional Demand

Capital concentration within the EV category is visible not only in vehicle manufacturing but in associated supply chains, especially batteries and recycling. Redwood Materials stands out as a flagship example in battery materials and recycling; its news page summarizes that the company raised over USD 1 billion in a Series D investment round to boost its EV battery materials business, and that it later secured a conditional commitment for a USD 2 billion Department of Energy loan to expand battery material production. [^l62roi] Redwood has also announced partnerships with major automakers, including BMW of North America and Volkswagen, to recycle lithium-ion batteries from EVs and consumer electronics and feed recycled materials back into new battery production, illustrating how capital in this category is clustering around closed-loop systems. [^l62roi]  

On the demand side, capital is implicitly concentrated in regions with strong EV uptake, shaping where vehicles, infrastructure, and supply chains must scale. The IEA notes that in China, electric car sales are expected to reach around 10 million in 2024, representing almost one in three cars on the road by 2030 being electric, while in the United States and European Union, almost one in five cars on the road is expected to be electric by 2030 under current policy settings. [^d3sozx] Additionally, outside the major EV markets, electric car sales are anticipated to surpass 1 million units in 2024, a more than 40% increase over 2023, indicating capital and industrial activity are starting to spread to emerging economies. [^r2unr3] [^d3sozx] The development of used EV markets—with hundreds of thousands of used EV sales in China, the United States, and major European economies—also reflects growing capital turnover within the category, as assets retire, are resold, and feed into recycling loops. [^4y5iiw] [^l62roi] Collectively, these patterns show that while funding data points like Redwood’s Series D and DOE loan are emblematic, a broader capital concentration is occurring in EV-related assets across vehicles, infrastructure, and battery materials.  

## Market Incumbents

In the electric vehicles category, incumbents are large, established automakers or technology companies—often public, sometimes late-stage private—that possess legacy manufacturing capacity, deep dealer or customer networks, and significant existing contracts with fleet buyers and governments. They dominate the production of vehicles, have credibility with regulators, and increasingly offer EVs alongside or instead of ICE vehicles. Within this tier, Tesla and BYD are paradigmatic EV-focused incumbents, while traditional OEMs such as Volkswagen, General Motors, Ford, Hyundai, Nissan, and Stellantis are legacy players transitioning their portfolios.  

[Tesla](https://www.tesla.com) — A public, U.S.-based automaker that pioneered mass-market battery-electric vehicles and now delivers nearly 1.8 million vehicles per year, positioning itself as a global benchmark for EV performance, software integration, and direct-to-consumer sales. [^e08735]  

[BYD](https://www.byd.com) — A Chinese automaker that has rapidly grown into one of the world’s largest EV manufacturers, selling over 4.27 million vehicles in 2024 with a mix of BEVs and PHEVs and surpassing Tesla’s quarterly revenue in 2024, reflecting its broad footprint in both passenger cars and buses. [^droz5b] [^e08735]  

[Volkswagen Group](https://www.vw.com) — A major European automaker that has invested heavily in electrification across its brands, scaling production of battery-electric models in response to EV sales reaching 18% of global car sales in 2023 and projected to surpass 20% in 2024. [^r2unr3] [^4y5iiw]  

[General Motors](https://www.gm.com) — A leading U.S. automaker transitioning its portfolio with a public commitment to an all-electric future, leveraging its incumbent dealer network and manufacturing capacity to introduce EVs alongside ICE models in a market where EV sales are projected to grow by 20% and add half a million more units in 2024 relative to 2023. [^r2unr3]  

[Ford Motor Company](https://www.ford.com) — Another major U.S. automaker with significant EV offerings, including electric pickups and SUVs, operating in the same policy and market environment where EV stock and sales are forecast to grow steadily through 2035. [^t9gvvn] [^r2unr3]  

[Hyundai Motor Group](https://www.hyundai.com) — A Korean automaker that has expanded its EV lineup internationally, benefiting from global trends toward larger EVs and SUVs, which now make up about two-thirds of available electric car models. [^r2unr3]  

[Nissan](https://www.nissan-global.com) — An early mover in the EV space with models that helped seed consumer familiarity, now competing in a more crowded market where EVs represent a rapidly growing share of total car sales. [^r2unr3] [^4y5iiw]  

[Stellantis](https://www.stellantis.com) — A multinational automaker formed from the merger of FCA and PSA, investing heavily in EVs across multiple brands and geographies, particularly in Europe where EV adoption has been strong and policy-driven. [^24r6sn] [^t9gvvn] [^r2unr3]

[^d3sozx]  

**Why they’re in this category**: BYD is in this category as a vertically integrated battery and vehicle manufacturer that has become a leading producer of both BEVs and PHEVs, leveraging its battery expertise to compete head-to-head with Tesla and to supply electric buses and other vehicles globally. #### [Volkswagen Group](https://www.vw.com)

## Market Innovators

Market innovators are early-stage startups, typically pre-Seed through Series B, that experiment with novel concepts in the EV ecosystem—ranging from charging technologies and battery chemistries to fleet management software and new vehicle formats. These companies are often founder-led, have fewer than 100 employees, and articulate contrarian theses about what the EV category should encompass, such as vehicle-to-grid integration or radically modular vehicle platforms. Given the constraint of provided sources, innovators in this profile are described more generically than by name, but they remain central to the category’s future evolution.  

One cluster of innovators focuses on next-generation batteries, seeking alternatives or complements to conventional lithium-ion chemistries, such as solid-state batteries, with the aim of further reducing costs and improving safety beyond the trends highlighted by BloombergNEF and J.P. Morgan. [^w5qvza] [^rba3xf] [^a6f8uh] These firms typically position themselves as enablers of EVs with longer range, faster charging, and lower weight, addressing concerns that even with costs at USD 108 per kilowatt-hour today and projections of USD 72 per kilowatt-hour by 2030, EVs must continue to improve to attract late adopters. [^rba3xf] [^a6f8uh]  

Another set of innovators works on charging solutions, including ultra-fast chargers, smart home chargers, and software platforms for fleet charging optimization, building on the IEA’s projection that home charger stocks will grow more than tenfold by 2035 to over 270 million in STEPS and about 300 million in APS. [^7xj58a] These companies treat charging not just as hardware but as an intelligent system, often integrating industrial AI to predict demand, manage load on local grids, and optimize charging schedules across fleets, a direction aligned with broader energy storage and smart grid trends. [^7xj58a] [^rba3xf] [^a6f8uh]  

A third group of innovators focuses on software-defined fleets and mobility services, developing platforms that manage EV deployments in logistics, ride-hailing, and public transport. They use data from vehicles and chargers to offer route optimization, maintenance scheduling, and energy cost management, tapping into the growing pool of EVs on the road—projected by the IEA to reach hundreds of millions by 2035—and the need for operational intelligence around them. [^t9gvvn] [^r2unr3] [^4y5iiw] [^dep90i] These companies often emphasize that EVs, unlike traditional vehicles, are more easily instrumented and connected, making them ideal nodes in industrial-software ecosystems.  

Finally, some innovators explore business models around battery recycling and second-life applications, complementary to large players like Redwood Materials. They target niches such as localized recycling in specific cities or second-life deployment of EV batteries in stationary storage, piggybacking on trends highlighted by the IEA’s focus on batteries and Redwood’s large capital raises. [^w5qvza] [^rba3xf] [^l62roi] Their contrarian thesis is that value in EVs extends beyond the first vehicle life, requiring software and logistics to manage battery flows across use cases.

## Frontier and Open Questions

As EVs transition into a mass-market category, several frontier questions remain open and will shape the next decade of innovation and competition.  

One central question is whether the projected high growth rates—23% annual average EV stock growth in STEPS, 24% in APS, and 27% in NZE—can be sustained given constraints in charging infrastructure, grid capacity, and critical mineral supply. [^t9gvvn] [^7xj58a] [^w5qvza] [^rba3xf] [^a6f8uh] Incumbents and challengers alike will be involved in resolving this, with incumbents like Tesla and BYD scaling production and infrastructure partnerships, and innovators in batteries and charging working to alleviate bottlenecks. [^t9gvvn] [^r2unr3] [^7xj58a] [^w5qvza] [^rba3xf] [^a6f8uh] [^droz5b] [^e08735] [^l62roi]  

A second question concerns affordability: will declining battery costs and emerging used EV markets be sufficient to make EVs accessible to lower-income households and emerging-market consumers, or will price remains a barrier? [^4y5iiw] [^w5qvza] [^rba3xf] [^a6f8uh] The IEA notes that the pace of EV transition hinges on affordability and highlights growing used EV markets in China, the United States, and Europe, but it remains uncertain how far this trend can go without additional policy support or manufacturing innovations. [^4y5iiw] Innovators in low-cost EV designs and incumbents scaling economy models will be critical here.  

Third, there is an open question about category boundaries and technology mix: will PHEVs and strong HEVs remain significant parts of the EV category through 2035, or will BEVs and FCEVs dominate, especially under stricter emissions regimes? [^t9gvvn] [^r2unr3] [^zvci67] [^k6qim0] [^fh4aq2] [^e08735] Operators disagree about whether PHEVs should be treated as full EVs; challengers like BYD, which have large PHEV portfolios, and incumbents with mixed powertrains have strong incentives to keep PHEVs in the category, while some policymakers and climate advocates prefer focusing on zero-tailpipe-emission BEVs and FCEVs. [^droz5b] [^e08735] [^zvci67] [^k6qim0] [^fh4aq2]  

A fourth question revolves around the integration of EVs with power grids and energy storage: to what extent will EVs be used as flexible grid resources through vehicle-to-grid (V2G) schemes, and how will this affect battery degradation and user behavior? [^7xj58a] [^zvci67] [^rba3xf] [^a6f8uh] Innovators in charging software and fleet management are pushing V2G concepts, while incumbents and utilities must address technical and regulatory challenges. The J.P. Morgan analysis of batteries as the backbone of power grids suggests a future where EVs and stationary storage are tightly coupled, but the specifics are still emerging. [^rba3xf] [^a6f8uh]  

Fifth, there is uncertainty about the durability and reliability of new EV technologies and business models, as illustrated by Proterra’s bankruptcy and its impact on transit agencies. [^m9hso5] This raises questions about long-term support, warranties, and risk allocation between [[Original Equipment Manufacturers|OEMs]] and public-sector buyers. Incumbents and challengers developing buses and heavy-duty EVs must learn from such failures to design more resilient financial and operational structures. [^m9hso5]  

Finally, a frontier question concerns data governance and software control in EVs: as vehicles become more software-defined and connected, who will control data flows, updates, and interoperability among vehicles, chargers, and grids? [^dep90i] [^w5qvza] [^rba3xf] Industrial-AI and software companies may become central players here, competing or partnering with automakers, and regulators may need to intervene to ensure safety, privacy, and competition. Innovators in open platforms and incumbents with proprietary ecosystems will be key actors in resolving these issues.
