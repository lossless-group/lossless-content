# Snapshot

_**Personal AI** is the market for software and devices that build an enduring model of one person— their preferences, history, relationships, files, communications, and goals—then use that context to answer, remember, recommend, and increasingly act on their behalf. The category is moving from chatbot-style assistance toward persistent, multimodal, agentic “personal intelligence.”_ [^caq1g4]

> “The personal artificial intelligence assistant market” is forecast to grow from **$3.4 billion in 2025 to $19.63 billion in 2030**, a **41.9% CAGR**. [^t9cb07]

This profile captures the category as of **October 30, 2026**, when personal memory, cross-application context, autonomous task execution, and AI-native wearables are converging. The category merits a reference card because the same strategic question is now being pursued simultaneously by hyperscalers, foundation-model companies, search companies, consumer-device makers, and privacy-first startups: who owns the durable relationship between a person and their AI?

# What is this Market Category?

Personal AI comprises consumer-facing assistants, memory systems, AI-native interfaces, and software agents that are personalized to an individual rather than optimized primarily for a department or enterprise workflow. Products typically solve four problems: retrieving personal information, maintaining continuity across conversations, reducing routine cognitive and administrative work, and taking bounded actions such as drafting messages, booking services, shopping, managing calendars, or operating browser and productivity tools. [^299elp]

The customer is usually an individual consumer, household, professional, creator, student, or executive, although many products monetize through employer-paid productivity subscriptions. The defining asset is not merely access to a powerful model; it is a persistent personal context layer containing user-approved memories, preferences, files, communications, behavioral patterns, and permissions. Google describes emerging agent systems as having session, semantic, procedural, and episodic memory. [^uf2vx9]

The category excludes generic chatbots with no durable personalization, conventional voice assistants that mainly execute fixed commands, enterprise-only copilots whose data model is a company or tenant rather than an individual, and ordinary recommendation engines that personalize content without acting as a general-purpose assistant. It also excludes most robots and smart-home devices unless the device is an embodied interface to a persistent personal intelligence system. [^yqp8w8]

The boundary is disputed at both edges: some operators include AI search and browser agents such as Perplexity’s Comet because they act on a user’s behalf, while others reserve “Personal AI” for systems with long-term memory and a personal knowledge graph; similarly, some include wearables such as Humane’s Ai Pin and Limitless, while others treat them as hardware interfaces rather than a market category in their own right. [^ne5yon] [^6sbju5]

# Why Now?

- **Foundation models crossed the usefulness threshold for personal context.** Large models can now summarize long conversations, search heterogeneous personal data, maintain multi-step tasks, and use tools, making persistent memory more valuable than a single-turn answer. Google’s emerging agent architecture explicitly separates session, semantic, procedural, and episodic memory. [^uf2vx9]

- **Memory became a product primitive rather than a novelty.** Google, Microsoft, and other vendors have moved toward default or broadly available memory that retains preferences, working style, recurring topics, and prior interactions across product surfaces. [^mjrb2f] [^2y29m2]

- **Distribution shifted from standalone apps to incumbent ecosystems.** Microsoft can place a personal agent inside Outlook, OneDrive, Teams, and Microsoft 365; Google can connect Gemini to Search, Gmail, Photos, YouTube, Android, and Workspace; Meta can distribute through WhatsApp, Instagram, Messenger, and Facebook. [^05elaf] [^28nv71]

- **Agentic interfaces are expanding the action surface.** Perplexity’s Comet is an AI browser that can perform tasks on a user’s behalf, while newer personal-agent products describe themselves in terms of errands, bills, travel, calls, shopping, and inbox management rather than only question answering. [^ne5yon] [^299elp]

- **Capital has concentrated around model and distribution owners.** Anthropic raised $3.5 billion at a $61.5 billion post-money valuation in March 2025 and later announced a $13 billion Series F at a $183 billion valuation; OpenAI reached approximately $12 billion in annualized revenue and around 700 million weekly active users in 2025. [^h9t6js] [^qvi9fv] [^qfo9m9]

# What's Happening?

## CAGR and TAM

- **Research and Markets, _Personal Artificial Intelligence Assistant Market Report 2026_** estimates the market at **$3.4 billion in 2025**, **$4.84 billion in 2026**, and **$19.63 billion by 2030**, implying a **41.9% CAGR from 2026–2030**. The framing is a dedicated personal-AI-assistant market rather than the entire consumer-AI or robotics economy. [^t9cb07]

- **Technavio, _Personal AI Assistant Market 2026–2030_** forecasts **$12.36 billion of incremental market growth** between 2025 and 2030 at a **34.8% CAGR**. Its vendor-and-forecast framing is broader than a narrow memory-app definition and includes approximately 25 vendors. [^0xcfwr]

- The estimates are not directly comparable: broader definitions that include personal robots, smart-home assistance, and autonomous agents generate materially larger numbers, while narrow assistant definitions produce smaller TAMs. One broader Research and Markets study forecasts the personal-robot market at **$63.8 billion by 2030** and personal-assistance robots at **$7.7 billion**, demonstrating how much the headline changes with category scope. [^yqp8w8]

## Category creation events

- **Persistent memory became the reference implementation.** Google’s “personal intelligence” direction and Microsoft’s Copilot Memory shifted competition from “which chatbot is smartest?” toward “which assistant knows the user best and can use that context safely?” [^mjrb2f] [^2y29m2]

- **Personal agents became a named product category in 2026.** Coverage increasingly grouped products such as Google Gemini Spark, Microsoft Scout/Autopilot, and Meta Muse under the “personal agent” label, with the category defined by initiative, bounded autonomy, and access to personal context. [^sg2f0b] [^r2c83m]

- **The wearable route was repriced by failure and acquisition.** Humane shut down its Ai Pin business and sold relevant assets to HP for $116 million after raising roughly $241 million; the episode established that an AI-native device must solve a substantially better personal-assistance problem than a smartphone. [^6sbju5]

- **Meta’s acquisition of Limitless validated personal memory as a strategic capability.** Limitless, formerly Rewind, built software and wearable products that captured and searched conversations and on-screen activity; Meta acquired it in December 2025 to strengthen its AI-wearables and “personal superintelligence” ambitions. [^dha1fr] [^0s7nl5]

## Capital concentration

- The largest capital pools are flowing to **foundation-model challengers** that can become personal-AI platforms: Anthropic’s $13 billion Series F was led by ICONIQ, following its $3.5 billion Series E at a $61.5 billion valuation. [^qvi9fv] [^h9t6js]

- **OpenAI** remains the highest-distribution challenger because ChatGPT combines a large consumer base with multimodal, memory, and agent ambitions; Reuters reported approximately $12 billion in annualized revenue and approximately 700 million weekly active users in 2025. [^qfo9m9]

- **Perplexity** is concentrating capital around AI-native search and browser agency. It reportedly secured $200 million at a $20 billion valuation in September 2025, while its Comet browser can perform certain tasks on a user’s behalf. [^ne5yon]

- **Hardware capital has been more fragile than software capital.** Humane raised roughly $241 million but ultimately sold assets for $116 million, while Limitless’s wearable strategy ended in acquisition by Meta. [^6sbju5] [^dha1fr]

# Frontier and Open Questions

- **Will personal AI be a memory layer or an action layer?** Limitless and Rewind demonstrate the memory-first thesis, while Perplexity, Manus, and the major model companies are pushing toward autonomous action; the resolution will likely come from challengers that can prove repeatable task completion rather than merely better recall. [^299elp] [^ne5yon]

- **Who will own the personal context graph?** Google, Microsoft, Meta, and Apple possess the richest identity and application graphs, while innovators argue that users should control an independent vault that can move between models; privacy-first innovators and platform incumbents will determine whether portability becomes standard. [^k3pqnl] [^28nv71]

- **Can a standalone wearable beat the smartphone?** Humane’s $116 million asset sale after approximately $241 million of funding is a negative precedent, while Meta’s acquisition of Limitless suggests that wearables may succeed as extensions of an ecosystem rather than as independent platforms. [^6sbju5] [^dha1fr]

- **Will users accept always-on capture?** Ambient transcription and personal memory require consent, deletion, access controls, and clear boundaries around bystanders; the category may contract toward explicitly user-initiated capture if privacy and legal risk overwhelm convenience. [^k3pqnl] [^0s7nl5]

- **Does “Personal AI” include AI search and browsers?** Perplexity’s Comet suggests yes because it acts within the user’s browsing context, but a narrower definition would exclude search and include only persistent personal memory and identity. [^ne5yon]

- **Will foundation-model companies or distribution incumbents capture the economics?** OpenAI and Anthropic are concentrating capital in models, while Microsoft, Google, Meta, Apple, and Amazon control operating systems, applications, devices, and daily touchpoints; the decisive variable is whether model intelligence or proprietary personal context is harder to replicate. [^5v8rtd] [^qfo9m9] [^05elaf]
