# Defining and Describing Growth Loops

- _Growth loops turn customer activity into the next wave of growth._
- A **growth loop** is a closed system in which an input passes through a process, creates an output, and reinvests part of that output as the next input. [^9v61iu] Unlike a linear funnel, which describes movement through stages, a loop models compounding relationships among acquisition, activation, retention, monetization, and referral. [^9v61iu] [^gzp0yh]
- The concept applies when ordinary product or marketing activity generates a measurable input for another cycle—for example, a user invitation creates a new signup, or published content attracts visitors who create more content. [^hx3eck]
- Growth loops matter because they connect growth to the product’s operating system rather than treating acquisition as a sequence of one-time campaigns. [^9v61iu] [^gzp0yh]

# Uses in Context

- **Product strategy:** Teams use “growth loop” to identify product behaviors—such as invitations, sharing, or collaboration—that can create additional users. [^9v61iu] [^hx3eck]
- **Marketing:** Practitioners distinguish viral, content, and paid loops according to the mechanism that generates the next cycle. [^wlsl18] [^hx3eck]
- **Growth modeling:** The term describes a system in which outputs are reinvested into acquisition or usage rather than discarded after one conversion. [^9v61iu] [^gzp0yh]
- **Product-led growth:** A product event can trigger an invitation, referral, expansion cue, or sales handoff that changes the product or commercial state and enables another trigger. [^upx9k3]
- **Strategic critique of funnels:** Growth loops are invoked as an alternative to treating acquisition, activation, retention, and monetization as a strictly one-way sequence. [^p66p95] [^wlsl18]

# History of Use

## Origins

- The modern growth-loops framework is generally attributed to Brian Balfour, Casey Winters, Kevin Kwok, and Andrew Chen at Reforge, whose 2018 essay **“Growth Loops are the New Funnels”** framed loops as an alternative to linear funnel thinking. [^wlsl18]
- The framework developed in the context of growth-practitioner debates about the limits of the AARRR funnel model for products whose users, content, or revenue create additional growth. [^p66p95] [^wlsl18]
- Its central formulation is that an input passes through a process, produces an output, and allows that output to be reinvested into the input. [^9v61iu] [^hx3eck]

## Evolution

- **2018 — Funnel replacement:** Reforge’s essay positioned growth loops as a replacement or complement to the one-way funnel, emphasizing that fast-growing products often rely on one or two major loops. [^wlsl18]
- **Late 2010s–2020s — Loop taxonomy:** Practitioners increasingly classified loops as viral, content, and paid systems, each defined by a different reinvestment mechanism. [^wlsl18] [^hx3eck]
- **2020s — Product and commercial instrumentation:** The concept expanded from marketing theory into event-based product analysis, where triggers, transfers, feedback, and next-entry conditions are used to test whether a claimed loop is operationally real. [^upx9k3]

# Best Real-World Examples

- [Dropbox](https://www.dropbox.com/) — A referral loop in which existing users invite others and successful referrals contribute to further user acquisition. [^hx3eck]
- [Notion](https://www.notion.so/) — A collaboration and sharing loop in which documents, workspaces, and templates expose additional people to the product.
- [Canva](https://www.canva.com/) — A content-sharing loop in which publicly shared designs can attract viewers who become creators or users.
- [Figma](https://www.figma.com/) — A collaboration loop in which inviting teammates is part of using the product, expanding adoption within organizations.
- [GitHub](https://github.com/) — An open-source collaboration loop in which repositories, contributions, and sharing create discovery and additional participation.
- [HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/) — A content loop in which published educational material attracts search traffic, users, and further content demand. [^hx3eck]
- Paid acquisition systems — A paid loop occurs when advertising generates customers whose retained revenue funds additional advertising. [^hx3eck]

# Case Studies

[[Tooling/Enterprise Jobs-to-be-Done/Dropbox|Dropbox]] illustrates a referral loop because the product’s existing users can invite other people, and those invitations create new signups and potential referrers. [^hx3eck] The mechanism differs from a conventional campaign: the product experience itself supplies the distribution event. The example shows that a referral loop is strongest when inviting another person is useful to the original user, rather than being an external promotional task.

Content-driven businesses demonstrate a second pattern: [[Vocabulary/Search Engine Optimization|SEO]], [[concepts/Explainers for AI/Generative Answer Engine Optimization|GEO]], [[Vocabulary/Static Site Generators|Static Site Generators]], [[concepts/Explainers for Tooling/Content Management Systems|Content Management Systems]]. A content loop begins with content creation, earns discovery through search or distribution, acquires users, and increases the population capable of creating more content. [^hx3eck] This is structurally different from a viral invitation loop: the reinvestment asset is searchable or shareable content rather than a direct interpersonal invitation. 

Paid acquisition demonstrates a third pattern. In the model described by growth practitioners, advertising acquires customers, retained customers generate revenue, and enough revenue is reinvested into subsequent advertising. [^hx3eck] The case shows why retention and unit economics are part of the loop: if customers do not generate sufficient durable value, the paid system cannot fund its next cycle and remains a linear spending program rather than a self-reinforcing loop.
