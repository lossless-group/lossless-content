# Habana Labs

![Habana Labs / Intel Gaudi accelerator card product photo or official logo](https://us1.discourse-cdn.com/grafana/optimized/3X/9/c/9cb83680e59aaf6ab3dc82fb0fd0318735259b50_2_690x223.png)

_*Habana Labs is an Israel-based AI chip company, acquired by Intel, that designs specialized processors and software stacks to make large‑scale deep learning training and inference more efficient and cost-effective for data centers and cloud providers.*_[^nof3g7] [^1srb2g] [^7vx0xk]

Habana Labs is a **for-profit semiconductor company** that develops high-performance AI accelerators, notably the Intel Gaudi and Goya processor families, for deep learning training and inference workloads in data centers and cloud environments. [^nof3g7] [^1srb2g] [^rtnhj5] Founded in **2016** and headquartered in **Tel Aviv, Israel**, the company focuses on custom AI processors and the SynapseAI software stack, and since **2019** has operated as an Intel subsidiary while retaining a distinct product line within Intel’s data-center portfolio. [^1srb2g] [^7vx0xk] [^db0lbb] Consultants track Habana because its Gaudi platform is one of the main non‑GPU options for large-scale AI training, used in offerings like Amazon EC2 DL1 instances to reduce AI training cost. [^qnw5d4] [^rpzmf3]

## Mission and Identity

- Habana Labs is consistently described as serving **enterprises and cloud providers** that need faster and more cost-effective AI compute for training, fine-tuning, and inference of generative AI and other deep learning models. [^1srb2g] [^qnw5d4] It positions itself around unlocking “order of magnitude improvements in processing performance, cost and power consumption” for AI workloads by designing processors from the ground up for training and inference. [^61jiu6] [^1srb2g] Its identity today is tightly integrated with Intel’s data-center AI strategy while retaining the Habana Gaudi brand as a specialized accelerator line. [^db0lbb] [^2rsn74]

- **Stated values / principles:**  
  - External descriptions emphasize **performance, efficiency, and scalability** for AI workloads, with messaging around “high-performance, high-efficiency AI accelerators” and optimization “for the specific needs of training deep neural networks and for inference deployment in production environments,” but there is no explicit standalone values list published by the company. ## What They Do

Habana Labs designs and delivers **AI accelerator hardware and software platforms** for data centers and cloud providers, focusing on training and inference of deep neural networks and generative AI models. [^nof3g7] [^1srb2g] It generates value by selling Gaudi-based accelerator cards and systems (now branded Intel Gaudi) plus the SynapseAI software stack that enables developers and infrastructure teams to train and deploy AI models more efficiently than on general-purpose GPUs in some workloads, often via cloud instances like AWS EC2 DL1. [^g85rft] [^qnw5d4] [^rtnhj5]

- **Intel Gaudi training accelerators:** Second-generation **Gaudi2** and related Gaudi family devices are deep learning accelerators “developed by Habana Labs (now part of Intel)” and “designed from the ground up for large-scale AI training,” featuring memory-centric architecture and high-bandwidth Ethernet-based scale-out. [^g85rft] [^2rsn74]  
- **Goya inference accelerators:** **Goya** processors are described as “optimized for inference,” providing specialized hardware for production deployment of deep learning models in data centers. [^rtnhj5] [^lrbfe5]  
- **SynapseAI software stack:** [[SynapseAI]] is described as Habana’s “comprehensive software ecosystem for Gaudi processors, providing everything needed to program, optimize, and run machine learning workloads efficiently.”[^g85rft]  
- **Cloud instances (AWS EC2 DL1):** Amazon EC2 **DL1** instances are “powered by Gaudi accelerators from Habana Labs (an Intel company)” and are marketed as delivering low cost-to-train deep learning models for NLP, object detection, and image recognition. [^qnw5d4] [^rpzmf3]  
- **On-prem and HPC integrations:** Gaudi2 nodes are deployed in HPC environments (e.g., a Gaudi2 node for researchers on a Future Technology Platform), where users can evaluate distributed AI training on Habana Processing Units (HPUs). [^g85rft]  
- **AI infrastructure for science and engineering:** Academic platforms like SDSC’s Voyager system “based on Intel’s Habana Lab AI processors” use Gaudi (training) and Goya (inference) processors with high-speed networking to explore AI in science and engineering. [^rtnhj5]  
- **Enterprise and cloud AI solutions:** StartupHub.ai characterizes Habana as operating in computing, artificial intelligence, data center, training, inference, and AI infrastructure segments, serving enterprises and cloud providers needing scalable AI compute. [^1srb2g] [^nof3g7]

## History and Origin Story

Habana Labs was founded in **2016** in Israel to build AI and deep learning processors for data centers and cloud environments, targeting more efficient training and inference than general-purpose hardware. [^1srb2g] [^lrbfe5] The company launched its first inference and training products in **2018** and **2019**, respectively, and was acquired by Intel in **2019** (deal closing reported in early 2020 in some summaries), after which it has operated as an autonomous Intel subsidiary focused on the Gaudi and Goya accelerator lines. [^7vx0xk] [^on61d7] [^db0lbb]

- **2016** — Habana Labs founded in Israel to develop disruptive AI and deep learning computing solutions for data center cloud and on-premise. [^lrbfe5] [^1srb2g]  
- **2018** — Launch of Habana products for AI inference, marking its first commercial hardware for production deployment. [^lrbfe5]  
- **2019** — Launch of Habana products for AI training, expanding from inference into full deep learning training accelerators. [^lrbfe5]  
- **December 2019** — Intel announces acquisition of Habana Labs; coverage notes “In December 2019 Intel bought Habana Labs,” and Intel later positions the deal as central to its AI accelerator portfolio. [^on61d7] [^db0lbb]  
- **2020** — Job and profile descriptions cite that Habana “was acquired by Intel in 2020,” and began operating as an autonomous subsidiary within Intel’s Data Products Group. [^lrbfe5] [^7vx0xk]  
- **2020s** — Gaudi-based accelerators gain adoption in cloud and HPC, e.g., powering AWS EC2 DL1 instances and academic systems like Voyager for AI training and inference at scale. [^qnw5d4] [^rtnhj5]

## Milestones and Signature Output

- **Gaudi (first-generation training accelerator)** — ~2019 — Initial Gaudi processors introduced as deep learning accelerators designed from the ground up for large-scale AI training, marking Habana’s entry into the training hardware market. [^g85rft] [^2rsn74] [^lrbfe5]  
- **Goya inference accelerator** — ~2018–2019 — Goya processors launched as AI inference accelerators optimized for production deployment, distinguishing Habana’s product line between training and inference. [^rtnhj5] [^lrbfe5]  
- **Intel acquisition** — 2019–2020 — Intel’s acquisition of Habana Labs integrated its Gaudi and Goya accelerators into Intel’s data-center AI portfolio and led Intel to discontinue its prior Nervana NNP‑T line in favor of Habana technology. [^on61d7] [^db0lbb]  
- **Gaudi2 launch** — early–mid 2020s — Gaudi2 described as Intel’s second-generation deep learning accelerator “developed by Habana Labs (now part of Intel),” with 96 GB of high-bandwidth memory and 24×100 Gbps Ethernet interfaces, enabling efficient training of large and complex models. [^g85rft] [^2rsn74]  
- **AWS EC2 DL1 general availability** — 2020s — Amazon introduces EC2 DL1 instances “powered by Gaudi accelerators from Habana Labs (an Intel company),” providing a cloud-based, low cost-to-train option for deep learning workloads. [^qnw5d4] [^rpzmf3]  
- **Adoption in HPC / scientific AI** — 2020s — Systems like SDSC’s Voyager and GWDG’s Future Technology Platform integrate Habana Gaudi and Goya processors to support AI in science and engineering, with high-speed networking and scale-out capabilities. [^rtnhj5] [^g85rft]

## Impact

- **Impact on society:**  
  - By enabling lower-cost AI training via cloud instances like AWS EC2 DL1, powered by Habana’s Gaudi accelerators, Habana contributes to broader access to deep learning for organizations that might otherwise be constrained by GPU costs, particularly in areas like NLP, object detection, and image recognition. [^qnw5d4] [^rpzmf3]  
  - Habana-backed systems like SDSC’s Voyager and GWDG’s Gaudi2 node provide researchers and students with access to AI-focused hardware for science and engineering applications, impacting academic and scientific communities. [^rtnhj5] [^g85rft]

- **Impact on innovation:**  
  - Habana Labs helped **pioneer Ethernet-connected, memory-centric AI training accelerators** (Gaudi and Gaudi2) with standard 100 Gbps Ethernet scale-out, offering an alternative architecture to traditional GPU-based solutions for large-scale AI training. [^g85rft] [^2rsn74] [^rtnhj5]  
  - Intel’s decision to discontinue its Nervana NNP‑T in favor of Habana technology underscores Habana’s influence on Intel’s AI hardware roadmap and, by extension, on the broader AI accelerator market. [^on61d7] [^fth876]

- **Impact on its industry or domain:**  
  - Habana’s Gaudi accelerators, via offerings like EC2 DL1, have become one of the main non‑GPU options for large-scale AI training, pushing competitors and cloud providers to consider heterogeneous accelerator portfolios beyond dominant GPU vendors. [^qnw5d4] [^2rsn74] [^dpi5pv]  
  - Industry and investment analyses frequently cite the acquisition of Habana Labs as a landmark AI chip deal, using it as a benchmark for later valuations and strategic moves in the AI accelerator sector. [^xv79zu] [^db0lbb]

- **Historical significance:**  
  - The 2019 Intel–Habana deal is routinely referenced as a key moment in the AI chip “arms race,” illustrating the shift of major CPU vendors into specialized AI accelerators and shaping how subsequent AI hardware startups and acquisitions are evaluated. [^on61d7] [^db0lbb] [^xv79zu]

- **Criticisms and controversies:**  
  - No substantive public controversies or regulatory findings specifically targeting Habana Labs were identified in the search results; most external commentary treats the company positively as a strategic AI hardware acquisition by Intel. [^on61d7] [^fth876] [^db0lbb]
