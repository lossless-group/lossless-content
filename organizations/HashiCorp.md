# HashiCorp

![HashiCorp logo and hero banner from hashicorp.com highlighting “The Infrastructure Cloud” and core products](https://media.licdn.com/dms/image/v2/D4E10AQGRRc1TjBgvyA/image-shrink_800/B4EZ_75eFLHYAw-/0/1786637575652?e=2147483647&v=beta&t=I_ottAGA5kddj0ZAGwBVtLDjKgjtoafDyCysR7tK5Sc)

_An IBM-owned infrastructure software company that helps enterprises “do cloud right” by turning infrastructure and security lifecycle management into code across hybrid and multi‑cloud environments._ [^891yr8] [^0y9795]

HashiCorp is a **for-profit developer-tools company** that builds infrastructure and security automation software, originally founded in 2012 by [[Sources/People/Mitchell Hashimoto|Mitchell Hashimoto]] and Armon Dadgar and now operating as an IBM company. [^cl2uk2] [^d35hhy] [^891yr8] It offers **The Infrastructure Cloud™** via the HashiCorp Cloud Platform (HCP) and self-managed products, spanning Infrastructure Lifecycle Management ([[Tooling/Software Development/Developer Experience/DevOps/Terraform|Terraform]], Packer, Nomad, Waypoint) and Security Lifecycle Management (Vault, Boundary, Consul). [^d35hhy] [^0y9795] [^7e05ri] The company was founded in San Francisco, California and its tools are adopted by thousands of enterprises worldwide, making it a global player in multi-cloud infrastructure automation. [^9w1wzj] [^s87nkl] [^k8grf3] Consultants track HashiCorp because its stack—especially Terraform and Vault—acts as a reference platform for modern infrastructure-as-code, zero-trust security, and hybrid cloud operations. [^k8grf3] [^e6v96f] [^7pri6s]

# What They Do

HashiCorp builds and operates **The Infrastructure Cloud**, an integrated platform of tools and SaaS services that turn infrastructure and security lifecycle management into code for hybrid and multi‑cloud environments. [^0y9795] [^891yr8] [^1f44x3] Day-to-day, it develops core tools (Terraform, Vault, Consul, Nomad, Packer, Waypoint, Boundary, Vagrant), delivers them as both community source‑available projects and enterprise/HCP offerings, and provides solutions for provisioning, security, networking, workload orchestration, and developer experience. [^4gxd9x] [^7e05ri] [^d35hhy]

- **Main offerings / products:**
  - **[[Tooling/Software Development/Developer Experience/DevOps/Terraform|Terraform]]** — Infrastructure as Code tool to provision cloud and other infrastructure using a declarative workflow (“plan/apply”), state management, and thousands of providers; available as OSS, Terraform Cloud, Terraform Enterprise, and HCP Terraform. [^k8grf3] [^y0kosm] [^0y9795] [^mcw4bu]
  - **Vault** — Identity-based secrets management and encryption system for centrally storing, accessing, and distributing dynamic secrets (tokens, passwords, certificates, keys) across public and private clouds; offered as OSS, Vault Enterprise, and HCP Vault. [^7pri6s] [^7e05ri] [^k8grf3] [^t05s76]
  - **Consul** — Service networking and service mesh platform providing service discovery, secure communication, and network automation across multiple cloud and runtime environments. [^mzyvh4] [^7e05ri] [^k8grf3]
  - **Nomad** — Lightweight workload orchestrator and scheduler for containers, VMs, and non‑containerized applications across on‑prem and clouds. [^k8grf3] [^0y9795] [^7e05ri]
  - **Packer** — [[Images (for Containers like Docker)|Image Management]] tool to build and manage machine images “as code” for multiple platforms. [^4gxd9x] [^0y9795] [^7e05ri]
  - **Waypoint** — Internal developer platform to standardize build‑deploy‑release workflows for applications. [^7e05ri] [^0y9795] [^b6tfbi]
  - **Boundary** — Secure remote access product that provides identity-based access to systems for humans and, in newer messaging, non-human and AI agents. [^7e05ri] [^tbp1zk] [^7rpt2h]
  - **[[Vagrant]]** — Tool for reproducible development environments, one of HashiCorp’s earliest open source projects. [^4gxd9x] [^hh3vrg] [^7rpt2h]
  - **HashiCorp Cloud Platform (HCP)** — Unified SaaS platform delivering managed Terraform, Vault, Consul, Boundary, Nomad, Waypoint and related services as “The Infrastructure Cloud,” including governance, collaboration, and operations automation. [^0y9795] [^d35hhy] [^k8grf3] [^7jl8id]
  - **Infragraph** — Event-driven knowledge graph that provides a single-pane-of-glass view of hybrid estates, offering infrastructure insights for operations and automated remediation. [^0y9795]

# History and Origin Story

HashiCorp began when Mitchell Hashimoto and Armon Dadgar, who met at the University of Washington working on research to bring emerging public cloud tech to scientists, moved to San Francisco and founded HashiCorp in 2012 to solve hard infrastructure management problems. [^m6umqn] [^cl2uk2] [^zxmn4r] Initially a small startup with zero revenue, it grew rapidly as infrastructure moved to the cloud, built a portfolio of open-source tools (starting with Vagrant and then Terraform, Vault, Consul, etc.), went public on NASDAQ in 2021, and was subsequently acquired by IBM in a multibillion-dollar deal. [^hh3vrg] [^2i12d7] [^7vrtvr] [^y0kosm]

- **2008–2010** — Hashimoto and Dadgar meet at University of Washington and collaborate on a research project to expose Amazon and Microsoft cloud technologies to scientists. [^zxmn4r] [^m6umqn]  
- **2012** — HashiCorp is founded in San Francisco by Mitchell Hashimoto, with Armon Dadgar joining as co‑founder, focusing on automation tools for multi-cloud infrastructure. [^cl2uk2] [^zxmn4r] [^s87nkl]  
- **2014** — Terraform v0.1 is released, introducing HashiCorp Configuration Language (HCL) and a plan/apply workflow that becomes a de facto standard in Infrastructure as Code. [^y0kosm] [^k8grf3]  
- **2018** — Terraform Cloud is introduced as a managed service for remote state, automated runs, and team collaboration. [^y0kosm] [^mcw4bu]  
- **December 2021** — HashiCorp completes its IPO on NASDAQ, with ticker HCP. [^y0kosm] [^7vrtvr] [^2i12d7]  
- **August 10, 2023** — HashiCorp announces license changes for its core products (including Terraform, Vault, Consul) from MPL 2.0 to Business Source License (BSL) 1.1, triggering community controversy and leading to the OpenTofu fork. [^ltqgs5] [^h85bsd] [^y0kosm] [^ibe9zl]  
- **2024–February 2025** — IBM acquires HashiCorp for approximately $6.4 billion enterprise value, integrating it into IBM’s hybrid cloud and security strategy. [^7vrtvr] [^y0kosm] [^pgmw7t] [^n9j5ub]

# Milestones and Signature Output

- **[Terraform](https://www.hashicorp.com/products/terraform)** — 2014 — Introduced as a leading open-source Infrastructure as Code tool using HCL, becoming a de facto standard for multi-cloud provisioning with thousands of providers. [^y0kosm] [^k8grf3] [^7rpt2h]  
- **[Vault](https://www.hashicorp.com/products/vault)** — mid‑2010s — Established as a widely adopted identity-based secrets management and encryption solution across public and private clouds, central to zero‑trust security implementations. [^7pri6s] [^k8grf3] [^7e05ri]  
- **[Consul](https://www.hashicorp.com/products/consul)** — mid‑2010s — Delivered service discovery and service mesh capabilities for multi‑cloud service networking, influencing modern microservices architectures. [^mzyvh4] [^k8grf3]  
- **[Nomad](https://www.hashicorp.com/products/nomad)** — mid‑2010s — Launched as a simple, flexible workload orchestrator for containers and non-container workloads across hybrid environments. [^k8grf3] [^0y9795]  
- **[HashiCorp Cloud Platform (HCP)](https://www.hashicorp.com/infrastructure-cloud)** — late 2010s–2020s — Rolled out as a unified SaaS suite for managed Terraform, Vault, Consul, Boundary, and other products, branded as “The Infrastructure Cloud.” [^0y9795] [^d35hhy] [^k8grf3] [^7jl8id]  
- **License change and OpenTofu fork** — 2023 — HashiCorp’s move from MPL 2.0 to BSL 1.1 for core products led to the community-driven OpenTofu fork under the Linux Foundation, reshaping the Terraform ecosystem. [^ltqgs5] [^h85bsd] [^y0kosm] [^ibe9zl]  
- **IBM acquisition** — 2024–2025 — Integration into IBM’s hybrid cloud and security strategy, with IBM reporting strong post‑acquisition momentum and record bookings for HashiCorp offerings. [^n9j5ub] [^pgmw7t] [^7vrtvr] [^y0kosm]  
- **Vault Kubernetes Key Management public beta** — 2026 — Release of a KMS v2-compatible plugin (`vault-kube-kms`) letting Kubernetes clusters use Vault Enterprise as their encryption-at-rest KMS provider. [^1jjco7]

# Impact

- **Impact on society**
  - HashiCorp’s tools underpin “the world’s most critical applications,” helping enterprises achieve efficiency, security, and scalability as they adopt hybrid and multi‑cloud architectures. [^d35hhy] [^e6v96f]  
  - Terraform’s widespread adoption (with 3,000+ providers and tens of thousands of modules) has standardized how organizations codify and manage infrastructure, impacting operational practices across thousands of enterprises and nearly every major U.S. bank. [^k8grf3] [^9w1wzj] [^hh3vrg]

- **Impact on innovation**
  - HashiCorp popularized core [[concepts/Infrastructure as Code]] and [[concepts/Zero Trust Security]] patterns through Terraform and Vault, turning infrastructure and security lifecycle management into code and policy-driven workflows that are now industry norms. [^0y9795] [^7pri6s] [^k8grf3]  
  - Its “plan/apply” workflow, HCL language, and unified Infrastructure Cloud platform created reference models for multi‑cloud provisioning, secrets automation, and service networking adopted and emulated by competitors. [^y0kosm] [^k8grf3] [^1f44x3]

- **Impact on its industry or domain**
  - HashiCorp reshaped the DevOps and cloud infrastructure tooling market, with Terraform becoming the de facto multi‑cloud IaC standard and Vault widely cited as a leading enterprise secrets management solution. [^k8grf3] [^rtv9hq]  
  - The 2023 BSL license change forced infrastructure vendors and platform providers to reassess their reliance on Terraform, driving market fragmentation and new offerings (e.g., OpenTofu, Pulumi) in Infrastructure as Code. [^ltqgs5] [^h85bsd] [^ibe9zl]  
  - IBM’s acquisition and subsequent strong bookings for HashiCorp products reinforced hybrid cloud and security as strategic segments within large enterprise IT. [^n9j5ub] [^pgmw7t]

- **Historical significance**
  - HashiCorp is likely to be seen as a pivotal actor in the first generation of cloud-native tooling, bridging the transition from manual configuration to programmable infrastructure and security at global scale. [^zxmn4r] [^k8grf3] [^0y9795]  
  - Its licensing decision and the resulting OpenTofu fork are a watershed moment in the history of commercial open-source models and infrastructure tooling governance. [^ltqgs5] [^h85bsd] [^y0kosm] [^ibe9zl]

- **Criticisms and controversies**
  - The August 2023 switch from MPL 2.0 to BSL 1.1 for core products generated significant controversy, with critics highlighting loss of open-source guarantees, ambiguity in “competitive use” restrictions, and the risk to platforms built atop Terraform; this prompted the OpenTofu fork under the Linux Foundation. [^ltqgs5] [^h85bsd] [^y0kosm] [^ibe9zl]  
  - Analysts note that the license change forced many infrastructure teams and vendors to confront unexpected compliance and governance decisions, turning a technical tool choice into a legal and business constraint. [^ibe9zl] [^h85bsd]
