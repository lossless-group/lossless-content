# National Aeronautics and Space Administration

![NASA insignia or official NASA headquarters image](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/qGveHpp3tQp4CrVaXyqnAY.jpg)

- _NASA is the U.S. government’s civil space and aeronautics agency, combining large-scale exploration programs with scientific research and technology development._
- NASA is a **government body** that explores air and space, conducts scientific research, develops aerospace technologies, and communicates discoveries to the public.[^261e6n] It was established in 1958, although the supplied search results do not provide a reliable founding source. Its headquarters are in Washington, D.C., with major facilities and laboratories across the United States, including Johnson Space Center in Houston and the Jet Propulsion Laboratory in Pasadena.[^cabp9d][^ty7n4i] Consultants track NASA because its missions, procurement, research, and public-private partnerships shape aerospace technology and national innovation priorities.

- **Type:** This organization is a **government body**.[^261e6n]

- **Headquarters and presence:** NASA Headquarters is in Washington, D.C.[^cabp9d] Its operational footprint is national, including centers and laboratories such as Johnson Space Center and JPL.[^ty7n4i][^1ac1s0]

## Mission and Identity

> “NASA explores the unknown in air and space, innovates for the benefit of humanity, and inspires the world through discovery.”[^261e6n]

NASA presents itself as an exploration and discovery organization whose work serves humanity through science, innovation, and inspiration.[^261e6n] Its current emphasis includes crewed lunar exploration, open scientific data, aeronautics, and preparation for future human missions beyond the Moon.[^tae1bf][^15petq]

- **Stated operating principles:** NASA foregrounds “transparency, collaboration, and accessibility” in its Artemis science and data-sharing approach.[^15petq]

## What They Do

NASA operates spacecraft, observatories, laboratories, aircraft, and human-spaceflight programs; funds and conducts research; partners with commercial aerospace companies and other governments; and publishes scientific data for researchers and the public.[^tae1bf][^1ac1s0][^7dz5pj][^15petq]

- **Human spaceflight:** Artemis lunar exploration and International Space Station operations.[^tae1bf][^1ac1s0]
- **Space science:** Robotic and crewed investigations of the Moon, planets, stars, and universe.[^tae1bf][^4v3bq1]
- **Earth science:** Space-based observation and analysis of Earth systems; no detailed current program list was returned.
- **Aeronautics research:** Research and innovation in aviation and aerospace technologies.[^261e6n]
- **Commercial partnerships:** Cooperation with companies including SpaceX and Boeing for crew transportation and spacecraft development.[^1ac1s0][^7dz5pj]
- **Open science and international coordination:** Artemis data sharing and the Artemis Accords, which had 71 signatory countries in the cited September 2026 result.[^15petq]
- **Workforce development:** The State Hubs for Skilled Technical Workforce initiative launched in September 2026.[^m540ya]

## History and Origin Story

NASA was created by the United States to lead civil space exploration, scientific discovery, and aeronautics research; the supplied search results do not provide a reliable founding-history source or identify its original founders and founding location. Its current trajectory is organized around Artemis lunar exploration, sustained low-Earth-orbit operations, scientific observatories, and international and commercial partnerships.[^tae1bf][^1ac1s0][^7dz5pj][^15petq]

- **1958** — The agency’s commonly recognized founding year; no returned source substantiates this date.
- **2020** — NASA and the U.S. State Department established the Artemis Accords with seven other founding nations, according to NASA.[^15petq]
- **September 2026** — NASA launched the State Hubs for Skilled Technical Workforce initiative.[^m540ya]
- **September 2026** — NASA began work on the United States Space Academy after an executive order and formed a presidential commission to develop recommendations.[^e5p7rf]
- **October 2026** — NASA’s SpaceX Crew-13 reached orbit.[^1ac1s0]

## Milestones and Signature Output

- **Artemis Accords — 2020** — Established principles for safe and transparent civil space exploration and expanded to 71 signatory countries by September 2026.[^15petq]
- **Artemis II lunar science record — 2026** — Produced crew observations, images, audio, and annotations from a crewed journey around the Moon, including observations relevant to lunar impacts and geology.[^tae1bf]
- **International Space Station crew transportation — ongoing** — NASA’s commercial-crew relationship with SpaceX supports crewed transportation to and from the station.[^1ac1s0]
- **Boeing Starliner development — ongoing** — NASA and Boeing continued planning for regular crew flights to and from the International Space Station.[^7dz5pj]
- **Hubble scientific legacy — ongoing** — NASA’s Hubble materials describe the observatory’s contribution to astronomical knowledge and detailed observations across the solar system.[^4v3bq1][^uh6mds]

## Ecosystem and Relationships

- **U.S. federal government:** NASA operates within the U.S. government and works with the State Department on the Artemis Accords.[^15petq]
- **Commercial space providers:** SpaceX is a crew-transportation partner, while Boeing is developing Starliner for NASA missions.[^1ac1s0][^7dz5pj]
- **Jet Propulsion Laboratory:** JPL is a NASA-associated laboratory in Pasadena overseen through NASA’s Office of JPL Management and Oversight.[^ty7n4i]
- **International partners:** The Artemis Accords connect NASA with 71 signatory countries.[^15petq]
- **Scientific community:** NASA makes Artemis data, tools, and results available to the global scientific community.[^15petq]

## Impact

### Impact on society

- NASA’s crewed and robotic missions produce publicly available scientific records and datasets; the Artemis II record was explicitly made available to the science community and public for further study.[^tae1bf]
- NASA’s workforce initiative seeks to strengthen the technical aerospace workforce through state-based hubs.[^m540ya]
- No reliable external quantitative estimate of NASA’s total public reach or societal beneficiaries was returned.

### Impact on innovation

- NASA’s Artemis program is generating lunar datasets and open tools intended to accelerate research by scientists and partner countries.[^tae1bf][^15petq]
- Its commercial-crew model has connected a federal space agency with private spacecraft providers for crew transportation to low Earth orbit.[^1ac1s0][^7dz5pj]
- NASA’s aeronautics mandate extends its innovation role beyond spaceflight into aviation research.[^261e6n]

### Impact on its industry or domain

- NASA’s Artemis Accords established a shared framework for safe and transparent civil lunar exploration and had 71 signatory countries by September 2026.[^15petq]
- NASA’s continuing collaboration with SpaceX and Boeing makes commercial crew transportation a central part of U.S. human-spaceflight operations.[^1ac1s0][^7dz5pj]

### Historical significance

- NASA’s institutional role spans civil space exploration, scientific discovery, and aeronautics research, making it a central public-sector institution in the history of U.S. aerospace.[^261e6n]
- Its current Artemis and international-data-sharing efforts are shaping the governance and scientific infrastructure of renewed lunar exploration.[^tae1bf][^15petq]

### Criticisms and controversies

- NASA’s Inspector General reported that the agency’s fiscal-year 2026 information-security program was rated Level 3 and was not considered effective.[^sllpi9]
- A NASA Inspector General audit identified a projected $9.3 million overrun against a $628 million project-managed development-cost estimate for an upcoming twin-satellite mission.[^1yrc26]
