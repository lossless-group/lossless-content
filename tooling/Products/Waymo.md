## Value Proposition & Features

Waymo develops the **Waymo Driver**, a fully autonomous driving system, and operates autonomous ride-hailing services intended to make urban travel safer, more accessible, and convenient. Its service is a [[concepts/Market-Categories/Mobility-as-a-Service|Mobility-as-a-Service]] offering: riders request, take, and pay for driverless trips through an app. Waymo describes the system as designed to operate without a human behind the wheel and to reduce collision risk. [^E1] [^3v0rd0]

- **Multimodal perception:** [[Vocabulary/LiDAR|LiDAR]], cameras, and radar provide overlapping, 360-degree environmental sensing, reportedly reaching up to 300 metres. [^3v0rd0]
- **Prediction and planning:** AI anticipates movements by pedestrians, cyclists, and vehicles to navigate complex traffic situations. [^3v0rd0]
- **Autonomous ride-hailing:** Riders can request fully autonomous trips through Waymo’s service in supported markets. [^E1]
- **Safety validation:** New capabilities are evaluated through a peer-reviewed safety framework before public deployment. [^cx7riv]
- **Weather adaptation:** The sixth-generation system uses sensor heaters and micro-wipers, and can reduce or pause service when conditions exceed validated limits. [^cx7riv]
- **Simulation and road testing:** Waymo combines hundreds of millions of public-road kilometres with billions of simulated kilometres. [^3v0rd0]
- **Vehicle partnerships:** Toyota and Waymo are collaborating on autonomous Toyota bZ4X fleet testing, with initial Japanese operations planned for 2027. [^n4cpdg]
## Product Roadmap / Announcements

As of November 14, 2026,

- **October 30, 2026:** Waymo and Toyota announced plans to test an autonomous bZ4X fleet in Japan in 2027, followed by potential deployment in the United Kingdom and other Waymo-serviced markets. [^n4cpdg]
- **October 16, 2026:** Waymo announced preparations for a Toronto ride-hailing service, targeting first riders in 2028 after mapping and supervised testing. [^op6b6p]
- **November 5, 2026:** Waymo detailed severe-weather capabilities for its sixth-generation Driver, including heated sensors, micro-wipers, and weather-triggered service adjustments. [^cx7riv]

## Recent Developments

- Waymo reported that its Driver had completed more than 270 million fully autonomous miles through June 2026 and was associated with 82% fewer injury-causing crashes than human-driver benchmarks across five U.S. metropolitan areas. [^jbhf9u]
- Waymo said its Toronto service would eventually support hundreds of thousands of weekly trips across major markets and emphasized continued international expansion. [^3v0rd0]
- The Toyota collaboration is positioned as a pathway to scale Level 4 autonomous vehicles into additional global markets. [^n4cpdg]

# History and Origin Story

Waymo was founded in 2009 as [[organizations/Google|Google]]’s self-driving-car project and later became a distinct Alphabet-backed company focused on autonomous-driving technology and services. [^E1] Its central inflection point was the transition from research vehicle development to commercial autonomous ride-hailing, with the Waymo Driver becoming the basis for a consumer mobility service. [^E1]

# Competitive Landscape

## Who it's for, who it's not for

Waymo is for riders in supported service areas who want app-based, fully autonomous urban transportation, as well as transportation and vehicle-manufacturing partners seeking autonomous-driving deployment. [^E1] [^n4cpdg]

It is not currently for people outside Waymo’s operating or testing geographies, or for customers requiring conventional human-driven service; expansion remains phased and market-specific. [^op6b6p]

## Viable Alternatives

- **[[organizations/Uber|Uber]]:** Conventional app-based ride-hailing with broad geographic coverage; it is a substitute for the mobility service, not necessarily for fully autonomous operation.
- **[[organizations/Lyft]]:** Ride-hailing alternative for users prioritizing conventional driver-based trips.
- **[[organizations/Tesla|Tesla]]:** Autonomous-driving technology competitor pursuing consumer-vehicle deployment rather than Waymo’s same operating model.
- **[[Cruise]]:** Autonomous-vehicle technology and ride-hailing competitor, where service availability permits.
- **Traditional taxis and public transit:** Lower-technology substitutes for urban point-to-point mobility.
