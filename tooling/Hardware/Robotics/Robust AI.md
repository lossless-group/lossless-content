## Value Proposition & Features

Robust AI develops AI-powered collaborative mobile robots for dynamic warehouse environments, with Carter designed to work alongside people across picking, putaway, transport, sorting, and value-added services. [^E1] Carter is positioned as “drop-in” automation that operates without fixed-infrastructure changes, helping warehouses improve throughput while reducing worker strain. [^E1] [^asvny6]

- **Multifunctional Carter platform:** One robot supports picking, point-to-point transport, putaway, conveyor loops, and mobile sorting, reducing the need for separate robot fleets. [^e8yrc6] [^asvny6]
- **360-degree perception:** Four stereo-camera sets provide visual awareness in shared aisles, while visual SLAM maps facilities and supports localization accuracy of up to 5 cm, according to the product description. [^asvny6]
- **Holonomic mobility:** Carter can move in any direction while rotating, allowing navigation through tight aisles and congested warehouse spaces. [^asvny6]
- **Human handoff:** A force-sensitive handlebar lets associates manually steer Carter when needed. [^asvny6]
- **Infrastructure-free deployment:** Carter is designed to operate without facility rebuilds or additional fixed automation hardware. [^ucbi9p] [^asvny6]
- **Collaborative operation:** The robot is intended to work alongside warehouse associates rather than isolate them from the workflow. [^asvny6]
- **Flexible task allocation:** Carter can be moved between tasks, zones, and shifts as operating priorities change. [^asvny6]
- **Robot-as-a-Service option:** Robust AI’s deployment model is described as performance-based RaaS, enabling flexible scaling for logistics operators. [^e8yrc6]

# History and Origin Story

Robust AI was founded in 2019 and is headquartered in San Carlos, California. [^E1] Its public product strategy centers on Carter, a collaborative warehouse robot developed around human-centered operation, adaptable workflows, and deployment without major infrastructure changes. [^E1] [^asvny6] The company’s positioning fits the broader [[concepts/Market-Categories/Physical AI|Physical AI]] category because its software directly controls perception, mobility, and task execution in physical environments.

# Competitive Landscape

## Who it's for, who it's not for

Robust AI is aimed at warehouses, fulfillment operators, retailers, manufacturers, and 3PLs that need flexible material handling in facilities where fixed infrastructure changes are undesirable. [^asvny6] Carter’s described deployment at Saddle Creek involved tote delivery for a beauty-products fulfillment operation without fixed infrastructure changes. [^ucbi9p]

It is less suited to buyers seeking humanoid robots, heavy fixed automation, or a fully automated facility that removes human associates from the workflow; Carter is explicitly designed as a collaborative mobile robot that works alongside people. [^asvny6]
