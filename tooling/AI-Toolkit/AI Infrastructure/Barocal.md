Barocal develops solid-state cooling and heating systems using novel caloric materials, or solid refrigerants, that it says can deliver **2–3× higher energy efficiency** while eliminating harmful refrigerant gases. [^E1] The provided company metadata lists its headquarters at Newmarket Road, Cambridge, England, a workforce of 16, and funding rounds including a $10.0 million seed round dated May 1, 2026. [^E1]

## Value Proposition & Features

Barocal is a [[University of Cambridge]] spin-out commercializing solid barocaloric refrigerants for cooling and heating. [^3xrw4h] Its proposition is to replace gas-based vapor-compression refrigerants with inexpensive, non-toxic organic solids that absorb and release heat when pressure changes their volume. [^3xrw4h]

The technology targets air conditioners, refrigerators, heat pumps, and potentially high-density computing environments. [^3xrw4h] Barocal’s second-generation prototypes reportedly perform comparably to market vapor-compression systems, while a third generation is in development. [^3xrw4h]

- **[[Solid-State Refrigerants]]** that avoid harmful refrigerant gases. [^E1]
- **Barocaloric operation** using pressure-driven volume and phase changes to transfer heat. [^3xrw4h]
- **Higher efficiency**, with the company claiming 2–3× improvement over conventional approaches. [^E1]
- **Non-toxic organic materials** described as inexpensive and safe. [^3xrw4h]
- **Cooling and heating** in a single materials platform. [^E1]
- **Modular, scalable systems** intended for multiple cooling applications. [^E1]
- **Potential data-center relevance** as an alternative to conventional air- and liquid-cooling infrastructure. [^3xrw4h]

The technology could pair with [[content-areas/AI-Factories-Datacenters/Concepts/Liquid Cooling Systems|Liquid Cooling Systems]] where operators need more efficient thermal management for dense computing loads.

# History and Origin Story

Barocal was founded in 2019 by Xavier Moya, a materials physicist and University of Cambridge professor, as a Cambridge spin-out commercializing solid barocaloric refrigerants. [^3xrw4h] Moya’s research focuses on functional materials whose thermal, structural, magnetic, and electrical properties are coupled. [^3xrw4h]

## Competitive Landscape

### Who it's for, who it's not for

Barocal is aimed at HVAC, refrigeration, heat-pump, and other cooling-system developers seeking efficient alternatives to vapor-compression systems, as well as operators facing energy and thermal constraints in dense computing environments. [^3xrw4h] Its reported prototype performance and solid-refrigerant approach suggest a focus on industrial and commercial applications rather than consumer software or standalone hardware purchases. [^3xrw4h]

It is not currently positioned as a general-purpose software product, a conventional data-center liquid-cooling vendor, or a publicly priced off-the-shelf appliance. The available material describes prototypes and technology development rather than a broadly available commercial product. [^3xrw4h]

### Viable Alternatives

- **Vapor-compression HVAC and refrigeration:** The incumbent technology against which Barocal’s prototypes are reportedly benchmarked. [^3xrw4h]
- **Thermoelectric cooling:** Another solid-state cooling category included in broader solid-state cooling-market coverage. [^xg5609]
- **Magnetocaloric cooling:** A competing caloric approach based on magnetic rather than pressure-driven material changes. [^xg5609]
- **Direct-to-chip liquid cooling:** A data-center alternative for removing heat from high-density AI and computing hardware. [^er96o4]
- **Immersion cooling:** A data-center approach competing for high-density thermal-management deployments. [^xg5609]
