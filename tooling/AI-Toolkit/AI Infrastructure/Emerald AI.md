# Emerald AI


## Retrieved sources
Emerald AI — https://emeraldai.co/

Emerald AI was founded in 2024 and is headquartered in Washington, D.C. [^E1] It develops software that turns energy-intensive AI data centers into flexible assets for the electricity grid. This fits into [[content-areas/AI-Factories-Datacenters/Concepts/Smart Grids]] [^E1]


# Value Proposition & Features

Emerald AI’s value proposition is to help AI data centers obtain or use grid capacity without treating their electricity demand as fixed. Its Emerald Conductor platform adjusts computing demand in response to grid conditions while maintaining required compute performance. [^E1] [13]

Core features include real-time coordination between data-center workloads and electricity-grid signals. Lower-priority work can be slowed or rescheduled while higher-priority inference and other critical services continue operating. [13]

The platform also coordinates workloads across data centers and can incorporate onsite energy resources, allowing facilities to reduce or shift power draw during grid stress. [^E1] [6]

- **Grid-responsive workload orchestration** for AI data centers. [^E1] [13]
- **Workload prioritization** that protects critical inference and other high-priority services. [13]
- **Dynamic power reduction** during periods of grid constraint. [^E1] [13]
- **Scheduling and shifting of flexible compute** across time or locations. [2] [10]
- **Coordination across multiple data centers** as a distributed compute network. [^E1]
- **Integration with onsite energy resources** to manage facility power demand. [6]
- **Utility-facing grid flexibility**, including demonstrations with Silicon Valley Power. [13]

The approach fits naturally within the [[content-areas/AI-Factories-Datacenters/Concepts/AI Factories|AI Factories]] concept because it treats the data center as both a compute facility and a controllable energy asset.

# History and Origin Story

Emerald AI was founded in 2024 by Varun Sivaram, who serves as founder and CEO, and Ayse Coskun, a professor identified in company coverage as a co-founder. [2] [9] Its central premise is that software can make AI workloads responsive to changing electricity-system conditions, converting data centers from inflexible loads into grid-supporting resources. [^E1] [13]

# Competitive Landscape

## Who it's for, who it's not for

Emerald AI is aimed at large AI data-center operators, utilities, global infrastructure providers, and AI laboratories that need to manage multi-megawatt computing loads against changing grid conditions. Its reported customer and partner profile includes leading AI labs, major utilities, and global data-center operators. [^E1]

It is not primarily designed for small businesses, ordinary cloud-software buyers, or organizations without large controllable compute loads and meaningful electricity constraints.

## Viable Alternatives

- **NVIDIA DSX Flex:** Provides a related approach to flexible AI-factory power management and is described by NVIDIA as a platform category that Conductor helps demonstrate. [13]
- **Traditional data-center demand-response systems:** Existing utility programs can reduce facility load, but generally lack Emerald AI’s workload-level orchestration focus.
- **Battery and onsite-generation systems:** These manage grid exposure through physical energy assets rather than primarily shifting AI computation.
- **Cloud-provider workload schedulers:** These can move or defer compute jobs, but typically do not specialize in coordinating AI workloads with real-time regional grid signals.
