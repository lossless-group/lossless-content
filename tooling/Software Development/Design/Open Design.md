# Value Proposition & Features

**Value proposition (2–3 sentences)**  
OpenDesign is an **open-source, local-first AI design workspace** that turns the coding agents you already use — such as Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini CLI, OpenCode, or Qwen — into a design engine for prototypes, websites, dashboards, slides, images, and HTML video, all on your own machine.[94][97] It positions itself as the **“best open source Claude Design alternative,”** offering a vibe design workspace driven by composable skills and portable DESIGN.md systems instead of a hosted, single-vendor model.[91][94] OpenDesign is Apache-2.0 licensed, free to run, and keeps artifacts as real local files (HTML, PDF, PPTX, MP4) that teams can own and ship.[92][95][101]

**Core product features (2–3 sentences each)**  

- **Local-first desktop and web workspace**  
  OpenDesign is a desktop app for macOS and Windows, with Docker and Vercel deployment options, built as a local-first design workbench that runs entirely on the user’s machine and repositories rather than a hosted SaaS.[58][97][101] The workspace orchestrates design workflows around your project directory, writing exports directly as HTML, PDF, PPTX, and MP4 files into your repo or local folders.[58][101]

- **Agent-driven design engine (BYOK coding agents)**  
  The product does not bundle its own AI model; instead it detects and connects to existing coding-agent CLIs like Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Qwen, Copilot, DeepSeek Harness, or any OpenAI-compatible BYOK endpoint, turning them into a structured design engine.[58][92][119] Design is executed through SKILL.md skills and DESIGN.md systems that define workflows and visual systems, letting multiple agents collaborate on consistent artifacts.[94][97][119]

- **Multi-artifact generation (prototypes, web, slides, dashboards, media)**  
  OpenDesign can generate web prototypes, desktop and mobile UI, landing pages, dashboards, slides, images, and HTML video, and export them in production-oriented formats including HTML, PDF, PPTX, and MP4.[58][97][101] The workflow covers requirement definition, visual direction, artifact generation, and review, making each deliverable an interactive file rather than a static mockup.[58][122]

- **Brand-aware design systems and skills**  
  The workspace ships with roughly **151 bundled design systems** styled after products like Linear, Stripe, Vercel, Airbnb, Apple, and Notion, and more than **100 design skills** that encapsulate repeatable design tasks.[58][114][117] Users can extract brands from screenshots or Figma exports into DESIGN.md files; once saved under `design-systems/<brand>/`, every skill renders in that brand without re-prompting.[94][130][132]

- **Screenshots-to-prototype and Figma-to-code workflows**  
  OpenDesign includes plugins that take a screenshot or image reference and reconstruct it as an editable prototype with sensible components, layout, and responsive behavior.[127][131] It can also convert designs, screenshots, and Figma files into code, helping teams move from visual artifacts to implementation.[131][97]

- **Cloud and harness integrations (DeepSeek, Ox Alpha, etc.)**  
  Beyond local agents, OpenDesign integrates with hosted runtimes such as DeepSeek Harness and stealth models like Ox Alpha via Open Code, remaining model-agnostic while exposing structured design workflows and plugin ecosystems.[18][114][117] These integrations enable structured thinking, tool calls, and high-efficiency generations for UI tasks at lower model cost.[18][83]

- **Team workflows and shared canvases**  
  OpenDesign supports multiplayer AI design, allowing teams and their agents to design together on one shared live canvas inside coding environments such as Codex.[99][46] Brand memory compounds over sessions as screenshots, fonts, and palettes accumulate, letting the system learn the team’s brand with continued use.[132][58]

**Feature bullets (5–8, priority order)**  

- **Local-first, open-source AI design workspace (Apache-2.0) for macOS, Windows, Docker, and Vercel.**[58][97]  
- **BYOK agent model: turns existing coding agents (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini CLI, OpenCode, Qwen, etc.) into a structured design engine.**[94][92][119]  
- **Multi-artifact generation: prototypes, landing pages, dashboards, slides, images, and HTML video, exported as HTML, PDF, PPTX, and MP4.**[58][97][101]  
- **~151 bundled design systems and 100+ skills, driven by SKILL.md and DESIGN.md for brand-consistent workflows.**[58][114][117]  
- **Screenshots/Figma-to-prototype and Figma-to-code pipelines, reconstructing visual references into editable, responsive designs.**[127][131]  
- **Model-agnostic integrations with DeepSeek Harness, Ox Alpha, and other hosted agents for structured, cost-efficient UI generation.**[18][114][83]  
- **Multiplayer design: shared canvases where teams and their agents collaborate live, with accumulating brand memory.**[99][46][132]  
- **Exports are real local files in your repo, emphasizing production-ready artifacts over static mockups.**[58][122][101]

# History and Origin Story

OpenDesign originates from the **nexu-io/open-design** GitHub project, created on April 28, 2026, as an Apache-2.0 licensed, local-first alternative to the hosted Claude Design product, and quickly grew to tens of thousands of stars within its first months.[19][58][101] It evolved from an earlier internal name “Open Claude Design” before being refactored repo-wide to “OpenDesign,” and is framed by sources as part of Nexu Labs’ push to open up design workflows by turning existing coding agents into design engines.[19][58][112] By mid-2026, the project had over 3,600 commits, more than 90,000 stars, and a fast release cadence around versions 0.19–0.21, marking its emergence as a prominent open-source AI design tool.[19][52][46]

# Competitive Landscape

## Who it’s for, who it’s not for

OpenDesign is for **developers, designers, and teams already working with coding agents** who want a local-first, model-agnostic design workspace that produces real, shippable files (HTML, PDF, PPTX, MP4) and can be integrated into existing repos and automation.[58][92][101] It particularly suits self-hosters, tinkerers, and teams with established design systems that want AI to operate as a structured design director within multi-agent workflows rather than as a single hosted design product.[4][58][102]

OpenDesign is **not ideal** for users who need a purely hosted, no-install, turnkey design SaaS with its own models and collaboration stack, or for those whose workflows are tightly bound to proprietary ecosystems like Claude Design’s hosted environment or Canva’s marketing-centric platform.[6][7][12] It is also less suited to teams unwilling to manage local agents, BYOK credentials, or developer tooling such as CLIs, Docker, and repos, since much of its power depends on those components.[58][97][119]

## Viable Alternatives

- **Claude Design** – Hosted AI design tool that produces interactive prototypes and decks with Claude-based models, suited for fast concepting but closed and vendor-locked.[6][13]  
- **Figma Make** – AI generation inside Figma for product designers who already rely on Figma components and libraries for collaborative prototyping.[2][7]  
- **Google Stitch** – Real-time UI exploration and web handoff tool that generates app or website interfaces, more tightly integrated with Google’s ecosystem.[2][4]  
- **Moda** – Agent-led canvas for slides and visual collateral where work is not an app prototype, better for non-product visual workflows.[2]  
- **Vision (VSON)** – AI app-building platform that starts with interactive prototypes like Claude Design but continues to working software in a real codebase, focused on shipping internal tools and product pages.[3]
