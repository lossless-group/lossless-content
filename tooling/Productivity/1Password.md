## Value Proposition & Features

1Password is a password manager and unified access platform for protecting passwords, secrets, passkeys, and sensitive business information. Its newer positioning extends access governance to humans, machines, and AI agents, with scoped, task-specific permissions and activity auditing. [^E1] [^iny6o4]

- **Password and secret vaults:** Encrypted storage for credentials, secrets, passkeys, and other sensitive information across devices and applications. [^E1] [^3u4f7t]
- **Business access management:** Provisioning, credential sharing, auditing, and integrations with identity tools such as Azure AD, [[Tooling/Enterprise Jobs-to-be-Done/Okta|Okta]], OneLogin, and Slack. [^E1]
- **Developer secrets management:** Developer-facing APIs, SDKs, [[Tooling/Software Development/Developer Experience/DevOps/Terraform|Terraform]] support, and Kubernetes tooling support programmatic access to stored secrets. [^w76ump]
- **AI-agent security:** 1Password’s agent platform lets users approve an agent’s credential request while keeping the underlying credential hidden from the agent. [^y4zccp]
- **Privileged access:** The company describes its Privileged Access product as enforcing just-in-time, just-enough access and zero standing privilege. [^iny6o4]
- **Credential brokering:** The Credential Broker verifies agents or machine workloads and provides only authorized credentials at runtime. [^iny6o4]
- **SaaS access management:** SaaS Manager discovers and secures application access while helping organizations monitor AI-related SaaS spending. [^iny6o4]
- **Passkeys and passwordless authentication:** Passage by 1Password provides passkeys, biometric authentication, and phishing-resistant login capabilities. [^E2]

1Password is particularly relevant to [[concepts/Secrets Management|Secrets Management]] because its product scope spans employee credentials, developer secrets, machine identities, and AI-agent access.

## History and Origin Story

1Password was founded in 2005 and is headquartered in Toronto, Canada. [^E1] Accel’s 2019 investment was described as a $200 million minority investment, marking a major institutional-financing inflection point. [^F3] The company later expanded from password management into unified access, secrets management, privileged access, SaaS management, and AI-agent security. [^E1] [^iny6o4]

## Competitive Landscape

### Who it's for, who it's not for

1Password is designed for individuals, families, growing companies, enterprises, developers, and security teams that need centralized management of passwords, secrets, passkeys, application access, and machine or AI-agent credentials. [^E1] [^iny6o4]

It is less suited to users seeking only a basic, ecosystem-specific password vault or organizations that require a narrowly focused secrets-management or privileged-access product without end-user password-management features.

### Viable Alternatives

- **Bitwarden:** A password-management alternative for individuals, families, and organizations seeking a competing vault product. [^ft7c7c]
- **Dashlane:** A consumer and business password-management alternative with overlapping credential-storage and sharing capabilities. [^ft7c7c]
- **Keeper:** A business-oriented password-management competitor with overlapping enterprise use cases. [^ft7c7c]
- **HashiCorp Vault:** A specialized alternative for infrastructure and application secrets management rather than primarily end-user password management. [^6k4pj1]
- **Doppler:** A developer-focused secrets-management alternative for application and infrastructure credentials. [^6k4pj1]
