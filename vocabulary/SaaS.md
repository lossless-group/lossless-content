# Defining and Describing SaaS

![Diagram showing a SaaS application, provider-managed cloud infrastructure, browser/API access, subscriptions, and multiple customer organizations](https://www.investopedia.com/thmb/FmHi2FYg-2nDq1JneTHLWNT6Z1w=/1500x0/filters:no_upscale():max_bytes(150000):strip_icc()/Software-as-a-service-saas_final-5caef210a00b48f5ac5de4964f72a016.png)
- _SaaS (software as a service) is a way of delivering software over the internet as an ongoing service rather than as a one-time, customer-installed product. [^eioe0v] [^wwa9xh]_
- The term applies when a provider hosts and operates the application while customers access it remotely, commonly through a browser or API. [^eioe0v] [^wwa9xh] In startup and innovation work, SaaS also commonly describes the business built around that delivery model: recurring subscriptions, continuous product improvement, and provider-managed operations. [^b8s04h] It does not automatically mean that every cloud-hosted product, subscription product, or web application is SaaS; the provider’s responsibility for running and updating the application is central. [^eioe0v] [^wwa9xh] Consultants care because the model changes product architecture, pricing, customer adoption, retention metrics, implementation work, and the economics of scaling a software company. [^b8s04h]

# Disambiguation

## Primary sense — the innovation-consulting sense

**SaaS is a software-product and business-delivery model in which a provider centrally operates an application and sells customers continuing access to it, usually through the internet and recurring payments. [^eioe0v] [^wwa9xh] [^b8s04h]**

- In common startup usage, a SaaS company delivers software online instead of selling a perpetual license that the customer installs and maintains; monthly or annual subscriptions are typical, although usage-based pricing is also used. [^b8s04h]
- The customer generally outsources infrastructure, application operations, maintenance, and updates to the provider, accessing the product through a browser or API. [^eioe0v] [^wwa9xh]
- SaaS is not synonymous with **subscription software**: a traditionally installed application can be sold by subscription without being provider-hosted SaaS. [^eioe0v] [^b8s04h]
- SaaS is not synonymous with **cloud computing** as a whole. SaaS is the application layer; infrastructure as a service (IaaS) and platform as a service (PaaS) are separate cloud-service categories. [^y9g68n] [^83owt3]

## Other senses

### 1. SaaS as a startup category

A **SaaS startup** is a company whose principal product is internet-delivered software sold through recurring, tiered, or usage-linked access. [^b8s04h]

- Founders commonly choose SaaS to replace large upfront licenses with recurring revenue and to serve many customers from a centrally operated product. [^b8s04h]
- Typical pricing structures include flat-rate, tiered, and usage-based plans; tiering allows a company to address different customer segments and willingness to pay. [^b8s04h]
- From an innovation-consulting perspective, the relevant questions are not merely whether the product is online, but whether acquisition, activation, retention, expansion, support, and infrastructure costs produce a repeatable growth model.

### 2. SaaS as an organizational adoption model

SaaS can also refer to an organization’s shift from owning and operating software locally to consuming it as an externally managed service. [^eioe0v] [^wwa9xh]

- This shift can reduce customers’ need for upfront hardware and software infrastructure, while increasing dependence on vendor availability, security, integration, data portability, and pricing decisions. [^eioe0v] [^e70ijw]
- Adoption therefore involves organizational change as well as procurement: workflows, permissions, data governance, training, and vendor-management practices may all change.
- A hosted application supplied through an older application-service-provider arrangement may resemble SaaS, but modern SaaS generally implies a more scalable, centrally maintained service architecture. [^33s5pj] [^4y7xsj]

- Also used in cloud-architecture discussions as one member of the IaaS–PaaS–SaaS service-model taxonomy; this technical sense remains relevant to innovation work because it clarifies which layer a company builds, buys, or operates. [^y9g68n] [^83owt3]

# Etymology and Origin

- **SaaS** is an abbreviation of “software as a service,” a phrase describing software delivered over a network for on-demand use rather than installed and owned outright. [^eioe0v] [^1t382x]
- The acronym is commonly associated with *Software as a Service: Strategic Backgrounder*, an internally published industry document attributed to the Software & Information Industry Association in February 2001. [^eioe0v] [^1t382x]
- The model’s predecessors include mainframe timesharing and 1990s application service providers (ASPs), which hosted applications for customers before “SaaS” became the dominant category name. [^33s5pj] [^wwa9xh]
- Salesforce, founded by Marc Benioff in 1999, helped define and popularize the modern web-delivered SaaS category, while later cloud-infrastructure developments made the model easier to scale. [^33s5pj] [^4y7xsj]

# Adjacent Vocabulary

- **Synonyms**:
  - **Software as a service**: the unabbreviated form; identical meaning.
  - **On-demand software**: emphasizes access when needed rather than local installation. [^eioe0v]
  - **Cloud software**: a broader, less precise term that may include SaaS but can also refer to software with different operating or licensing arrangements. [^wwa9xh]
  - **Hosted software**: emphasizes provider operation, but may describe older single-customer or ASP arrangements that are not modern multi-tenant SaaS. [^33s5pj] [^4y7xsj]

- **Antonyms**:
  - **On-premises software**: software installed and operated on the customer’s own infrastructure. [^4y7xsj]
  - **Perpetual-license software**: software purchased through a one-time license rather than continuing access fees.
  - **Self-hosted software**: software for which the customer operates the deployment, even if the software is accessed through a network.

- **Adjacent terms**: [[Subscription business model]], [[Cloud computing]], [[Platform as a service]], [[Infrastructure as a service]], [[Application service provider]], [[Product-led growth]]

# Usage in Practice

- The category is commonly described as “a software delivery model in which software and its associated data are hosted centrally and accessed using a thin-client, usually a web browser over the internet.” [^eioe0v]
- Founder-oriented descriptions frame a SaaS startup as “a company that delivers software over the internet on a subscription basis.” [^b8s04h]
- The startup distinction is often expressed as replacing a one-time license with “monthly or annual fees for ongoing access to their product.” [^b8s04h]
- SaaS pricing may be “flat-rate,” “tiered,” or “usage-based,” reflecting different ways of matching revenue to customer segments or consumption. [^b8s04h]
- In cloud architecture, SaaS is classified alongside “infrastructure as a service (IaaS)” and “platform as a service (PaaS).” [^y9g68n]
- The historical transition is often described as moving from software delivered through application service providers toward “truly delivering software not as a product, but as a service.” [^4y7xsj]

# Common Misuses

- Calling any **subscription software** SaaS. Better term: **subscription software** when customers install and operate the application themselves; recurring billing alone does not establish SaaS. [^eioe0v] [^b8s04h]
- Calling any web application SaaS. Better term: **web application** when the description says nothing about provider-managed operations, updates, or service delivery.
- Calling a cloud provider or hosting layer SaaS. Better term: **IaaS** or **PaaS** when the company supplies infrastructure or developer tooling rather than a finished end-user application. [^y9g68n] [^83owt3]
- Calling a one-customer hosted deployment SaaS without qualification. Better term: **hosted software** or **application service provider** when the architecture and operating model resemble older ASP arrangements rather than a centrally maintained, scalable SaaS product. [^33s5pj] [^4y7xsj]
