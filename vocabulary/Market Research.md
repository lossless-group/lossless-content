# Defining and Describing Market Research

![Founder and researcher reviewing customer-interview notes, competitor data, and market-sizing charts](https://qubit.capital/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ChatGPT-Image-Nov-19-2025-01_34_55-PM_11zon.webp)

- _In a startup or innovation-consulting context, **market research** is the systematic collection, analysis, and interpretation of information about customers, competitors, market conditions, and industry dynamics to guide business decisions._ [^7an5af] [^cub9f7]
- The term applies when a team is reducing uncertainty about a market opportunity, customer segment, product concept, pricing decision, competitive position, or route to market. [^7an5af] [^cub9f7] It includes both firsthand investigation and analysis of existing sources. [^7an5af] [^cub9f7] An innovation consultant cares because research can expose unmet needs, test assumptions, and inform whether to enter, adapt, fund, or abandon an opportunity. [^ad8sen] [^umv41o] It does not mean merely collecting favorable opinions or presenting a large market-size figure without evidence of customer behavior or willingness to act. [^ad8sen] [^oayb3m]

# Disambiguation

## Primary sense — the innovation-consulting sense

**Market research** is decision-oriented investigation of a market and its participants, used to improve choices about what to build, for whom, how to position it, and how to commercialize it. [^7an5af] [^cub9f7]

- **Primary research** gathers original evidence through interviews, surveys, focus groups, observation, ethnography, or user testing; **secondary research** analyzes existing reports, public statistics, academic studies, reviews, competitor information, and internal data. [^7an5af] [^cub9f7]
- In early-stage innovation, market research often overlaps with **customer discovery**: speaking with people who recently experienced a problem, examining their workarounds and decision criteria, and testing whether they will take a concrete next step. [^ad8sen] [^oayb3m]
- It is not the same as asking whether people “like” an idea. Stronger research investigates recent behavior, problem frequency, consequences, purchasing processes, and commitments such as pilots, waitlist enrollment, introductions to buyers, access to a workflow, or budget discussions. [^ad8sen] [^oayb3m]
- It is also not synonymous with **market intelligence** or **business analytics**: those broader practices may continuously monitor competitors, markets, and organizational performance, whereas market research is usually organized around a defined decision or question. [^7an5af] [^cub9f7]

## Other senses

### 1. Marketing research

**Marketing research** is the broader professional function that connects consumers, customers, and the public with marketers by specifying information needs, designing collection methods, managing data collection, analyzing results, and communicating implications. [^xyu1uj]

- It commonly supports decisions about [[concepts/Positioning Theory|Positioning]], communications, segmentation, pricing, and campaign effectiveness, in addition to product and market decisions. [^xyu1uj]
- In innovation work, it is useful when a new offering already has a target market and the question concerns messaging, adoption barriers, or commercial execution rather than whether the underlying problem is important. [^cub9f7] [^xyu1uj]
- “Marketing research” and “market research” are often used interchangeably, but “marketing research” can emphasize the full marketing function while “market research” more directly emphasizes the market, customers, competitors, and industry context. [^7an5af] [^xyu1uj]

### 2. Customer discovery

**[[concepts/Customer Discovery|Customer Discovery]]** is an early-stage form of inquiry focused on understanding real customer problems, behaviors, workarounds, and commitments before or during product development. [^ad8sen] [^oayb3m]

- It favors conversations about specific past experiences over hypothetical reactions to a proposed solution. [^ad8sen] [^4zkikw]
- Its purpose is to challenge assumptions rather than confirm them; useful findings may overturn the founder’s original framing of the problem or target customer. [^oayb3m]
- It is narrower than market research when it excludes broad industry sizing, competitor analysis, macroeconomic conditions, or representative measurement across a large population. [^7an5af] [^cub9f7]

- Also used in historical and sociological research to mean codified knowledge about consumers and firms produced by specialized research organizations for firms, media, and administrations; this institutional-history sense is not central to innovation practice. [^aye4th]

# Etymology and Origin

- The expression combines **market**, derived from the Latin *mercatus* for trade or commerce, with **research**, derived from Old French *recerche*, meaning to seek or search again. [^x5ra95]
- Market research became a formalized discipline in the early twentieth century alongside the growth of consumer-goods industries. [^x5ra95]
- Its early development was connected to advertising and audience measurement; later accounts identify 1920s work by Daniel Starch and formal practice during the 1930s radio-advertising era. [^l0few7]
- The field subsequently expanded from consumer opinion gathering into structured qualitative and quantitative investigation, including surveys, interviews, observation, focus groups, behavioral data, and analysis of secondary sources. [^x5ra95] [^l0few7]

# Adjacent Vocabulary

- **Synonyms**:
  - **Marketing research**: often broader, covering the marketing function as well as market conditions. [^xyu1uj]
  - **Customer research**: emphasizes users’ needs, behaviors, experiences, and decision processes rather than the whole market. [^cub9f7]
  - **Market intelligence**: usually suggests ongoing monitoring and synthesis of market, competitor, and industry information. [^7an5af]
  - **Customer discovery**: an early-stage, problem-centered subset especially relevant to startups. [^ad8sen] [^oayb3m]

- **Antonyms**:
  - **Guesswork**: making a market or product decision without systematic evidence.
  - **Confirmation bias**: selectively collecting or interpreting evidence to support an existing belief; customer discovery is intended to challenge assumptions instead. [^oayb3m]
  - **Build-first decision-making**: committing to a solution before establishing that a consequential customer problem exists.

- **Adjacent terms**: [[Customer Discovery]], [[Problem-Solution Fit]], [[Product-Market Fit]], [[Market Segmentation]], [[Competitive Intelligence]], [[Market Sizing]]

# Usage in Practice

- A startup-oriented guide recommends: “Pick one narrow segment with a clear workflow,” rather than treating “SMBs” or “busy professionals” as sufficiently precise customer definitions. [^ad8sen]
- The same guide advises founders to conduct interviews focused on “recent behavior, failed workarounds, and decision criteria.” [^ad8sen]
- A founder-validation guide describes customer discovery as “talking to real people about their real problems,” not confirming assumptions. [^oayb3m]
- That guide warns founders not to ask only whether people like an idea, but to investigate use frequency, willingness to pay, and deal-breaking requirements. [^oayb3m]
- A market-research guide defines primary research as information collected firsthand for “a specific question,” designed around the organization’s objectives, audience, and decision. [^cub9f7]
- A professional definition describes market research as information gathering that helps organizations understand what people “need, want, think, and do—and why.” [^cub9f7]
- A startup research checklist recommends asking about “the last time” someone experienced the problem because past behavior is more reliable than hypothetical future behavior. [^4zkikw]

# Common Misuses

- **“People liked our concept, so we have demand.”** Better term: **concept feedback**. Positive reactions are not evidence of purchase intent, repeated use, or willingness to make a concrete commitment. [^ad8sen] [^oayb3m]
- **“We interviewed five friends, so we completed market research.”** Better term: **informal exploratory conversations**. Research should define a target segment, use a deliberate method, and synthesize patterns rather than rely on convenience respondents. [^ad8sen] [^trfrl8]
- **“The total addressable market is huge, so the opportunity is validated.”** Better term: **market sizing**. A large theoretical market does not establish an urgent problem, reachable buyers, competitive differentiation, or adoption. [^7an5af] [^umv41o]
- **“Our survey proves customers want this product.”** Better term: **stated-preference research**. Surveys can measure reported attitudes and preferences, but they do not automatically predict behavior; stronger validation tests actions such as pilots, budget discussions, or workflow access. [^ad8sen] [^cub9f7]
