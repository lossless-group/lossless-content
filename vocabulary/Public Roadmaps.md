# Defining and Describing Public Roadmaps

- ![Public product roadmap showing Exploring, In Progress, and Shipped columns with customer feedback links](https://img2.storyblok.com/filters:format(webp)/f/84825/1024x768/053236234f/79c6bf33-cec9-4f5f-8971-3c2cc32ef32a.png)
- _A public roadmap is an externally visible, customer-facing view of a product’s intended direction, current work, and shipped outcomes._
- In startup and innovation-consulting contexts, the term applies when a company deliberately exposes selected product priorities to customers, prospects, partners, investors, or the broader public. It is not simply an internal project plan copied onto a website: public roadmaps typically omit sensitive details, speculative commitments, internal owners, dependencies, and unsupported dates.[1][2][3]
- Consultants care about public roadmaps because they turn product strategy into a market-facing artifact: they can clarify positioning, test demand, create a feedback loop, and reveal how a company makes prioritization decisions.[2][4][7]

# Disambiguation

## Primary sense — the innovation-consulting sense

A **public roadmap** is a curated, externally accessible communication and discovery mechanism showing the product outcomes, themes, initiatives, or releases a company is considering, building, or has shipped.

- The audience may include customers, prospects, partners, investors, and competitors; the defining feature is visibility outside the organization, not a particular software format.[2][6][9]
- Common formats use status categories such as **Exploring**, **Planned**, **In progress**, and **Shipped**, or organize work by strategic horizon rather than promising exact delivery dates.[3][4]
- A useful roadmap connects product direction with customer evidence: users may submit requests, vote, comment, or follow updates, allowing the team to compare market interest with its own strategic priorities.[7][8][12]
- It is **not** an internal delivery plan, backlog, sprint board, contract commitment, or feature catalog. Internal plans may contain estimates, owners, dependencies, security findings, customer-specific obligations, and unresolved debate that should not be exposed publicly.[1][3][4]
- It is also not a guarantee that every listed feature will ship. Public-roadmap guidance emphasizes communicating direction while acknowledging that priorities and timelines can change.[2]

## Other senses

### 1. Building-in-public roadmap

A **building-in-public roadmap** is a component of a broader practice in which a founder or startup shares its journey, decisions, experiments, metrics, failures, and product direction in real time.

- In this sense, the roadmap is part of an ongoing audience relationship rather than a standalone product-management page.[14]
- The startup may invite users to influence prioritization, making the roadmap both a communication channel and a lightweight market-validation mechanism.[14]
- This approach can expose more strategic reasoning than a conventional public roadmap, but it also increases the risk of revealing competitive information, premature commitments, or sensitive business data.[1][14]

### 2. Public-sector or ecosystem roadmap

A **public-sector or ecosystem roadmap** is a publicly shared sequence of policy, infrastructure, standards, technology-adoption, or organizational-change milestones affecting multiple stakeholders.

- In innovation work, this sense is relevant when consultants coordinate government agencies, industry partners, investors, or platform participants around a shared transition.
- Unlike a product roadmap, it may describe outcomes that no single organization fully controls; its value lies in aligning dependencies, expectations, and investment timing.
- The retrieved sources primarily document public roadmaps in product and SaaS settings rather than public-sector planning, so this broader usage should be treated as an adjacent application rather than the term’s dominant startup meaning.

# Etymology and Origin

The phrase is compositional rather than a clearly attributable coined term: **roadmap** supplies the metaphor of a route toward a destination, while **public** specifies that the plan is shared outside the organization. The available sources do not establish a single founder, paper, or startup as the originator of the specific phrase **public roadmap**.

- In contemporary product practice, the term has settled into a contrast with the **internal roadmap**: the public version communicates high-level direction, while the internal version contains operational planning detail.[3][4]
- Its modern SaaS usage is associated with customer-facing roadmap pages that show planned, active, and shipped work, often connected to feedback, voting, and update notifications.[5][7][12]
- The related phrase **building in public** broadens the practice from publishing product direction to sharing the startup journey, including decisions, metrics, experiments, and failures.[14]

# Adjacent Vocabulary

- **Synonyms**:
  - **Public product roadmap** — the most precise synonym when the roadmap concerns a software or physical product.
  - **Customer-facing roadmap** — emphasizes the audience and communication function rather than unrestricted public access.
  - **External roadmap** — emphasizes the contrast with an internal planning document.
  - **Product direction** — broader and less structured; may describe strategy without listing initiatives or statuses.

- **Antonyms**:
  - **Internal roadmap** — restricted to the organization and usually detailed enough for operational planning.
  - **Private roadmap** — not shared publicly, though it may be selectively shared with customers or partners.
  - **Undisclosed roadmap** — withholds future product direction rather than communicating it externally.

# Usage in Practice

- “A public roadmap reduces repeated status questions and gives customers a place to follow work.”[1]
- A public roadmap is “a curated, externally visible view of your product plans — not your internal planning board.”[4]
- Public roadmaps are described as “a shared view of what your product team plans to build, what is in progress, and what has shipped.”[5]
- “A public roadmap is any product roadmap that’s visible to people outside your organization.”[6]
- “A public product roadmap also supports the entire feedback cycle,” allowing users to submit feedback, vote on requests, and participate in prioritization.[8]
- Building in public includes sharing “the wins, the failures, the metrics, the decisions, the roadmap” with an audience in real time.[14]
- In practice, teams often use public roadmaps to “build trust, manage expectations, and generate excitement about upcoming features,” while stating that priorities and timelines may change.[2]

# Common Misuses

- **Publishing the internal backlog as a public roadmap.** A backlog is a changing inventory of tasks and requests; the better term is **customer-facing roadmap** when the material has been curated for external audiences.[3][4]
- **Treating every roadmap item as a delivery promise.** A directional roadmap or **product direction** is more accurate when dates, scope, and commitment level remain uncertain.[2][4]
- **Using feature votes as a substitute for product strategy.** A feedback board captures demand signals; it is not the same as **feature prioritization**, which must also consider strategy, feasibility, impact, and dependencies.[7][10]
- **Calling a changelog a roadmap.** A changelog records completed releases, whereas a public roadmap communicates future direction as well as current and shipped work.[5][7][12]
- **Exposing sensitive internal information in the name of transparency.** Public communication should omit items such as internal debate, named customer contracts, security findings, and unsupported release dates; the precise term for the safer practice is a **curated public roadmap**.[1]
