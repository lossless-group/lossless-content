# Defining and Describing Crowdsourcing

![Diagram showing an open innovation challenge flowing from a startup to a distributed online crowd, with ideas, solutions, and evaluations flowing back](https://thumbs.dreamstime.com/b/concept-crowdsourcing-group-people-participating-generating-new-ideas-solutions-69009454.jpg)

- _Crowdsourcing is an innovation and operating model in which an organization uses an open call to obtain ideas, work, data, or solutions from a large, often undefined group outside its formal boundaries._ [^kssss9] [^sa1m9z]
- In startup and consulting contexts, the term applies when a founder deliberately exposes a task or problem to a broad external population rather than assigning it only to employees or a named contractor. [^fed44t] [^kssss9] It can support product discovery, design, R&D, data creation, customer insight, and community-led problem-solving. [^kssss9] [^2eygn4] An innovation consultant cares because crowdsourcing can expand the solution space and lower the cost or time of experimentation, but it also creates challenges around incentives, quality control, intellectual property, and governance. [^2eygn4] [^sa1m9z]

# Disambiguation

## Primary sense — the innovation-consulting sense

Crowdsourcing is the deliberate use of an open invitation to mobilize an undefined or large external group for work traditionally performed by an employee, contractor, or internal innovation team. [^fed44t] [^kssss9]

- The defining mechanism is an **open call**: participation is solicited from a broad network rather than from a preselected supplier or fixed partner. [^fed44t] [^mr41zk]
- Typical outputs include ideas, product concepts, creative assets, data, problem solutions, and even corporate R&D. [^kssss9] [^2eygn4]
- Crowdsourcing is not simply customer research: a survey that collects opinions without asking participants to perform or contribute to a defined task is better described as market research or feedback collection.
- It is not ordinary outsourcing: outsourcing transfers work to a known external provider, whereas crowdsourcing addresses an undefined or generally large group through an open invitation. [^fed44t] [^2eygn4]

## Other senses

### 1. Crowdsourced innovation and co-creation

Crowdsourced innovation uses a crowd to generate, develop, evaluate, or refine products, services, or business-model ideas. [^kssss9] [^q6pgk2]

- The crowd may contribute independently or collaborate toward a shared outcome; academic literature associates the practice with innovation, problem-solving, efficiency, co-creation, and user innovation. [^kssss9] [^q6pgk2]
- For founders, this sense is most useful when internal teams lack domain knowledge, need diverse concepts, or want evidence of demand before committing scarce capital.
- “Co-creation” is broader: it can involve a small number of named partners or customers, while crowdsourcing normally emphasizes scale, openness, and an undefined participant population. [^mr41zk] [^q6pgk2]

### 2. Crowdsourcing as a digital business model

Crowdsourcing can describe a business model that organizes an online crowd’s labor, creativity, data, or assets into a repeatable marketplace or production system. [^pl0beo] [^sa1m9z]

- The model often relies on information technology to coordinate an open call among human or non-human contributors. [^sa1m9z]
- iStockphoto illustrates the model: a marketplace for amateur photographers helped undercut a conventional stock-image business by more than 99 percent, according to *Wired*’s account. [^2eygn4]
- A platform that merely hosts transactions between identified buyers and sellers is not automatically crowdsourcing; the crowdsourcing element is the deliberate mobilization of a broad external population for a task or resource.

# Etymology and Origin

- Jeff Howe coined **crowdsourcing** in a June 2006 *Wired* article, *The Rise of Crowdsourcing*. [^0ygwj7] [^fed44t]
- The term combines “crowd” and “outsourcing” and named a pattern in which businesses used the internet to draw work and ideas from large groups of people. [^0ygwj7] [^j48jod]
- Howe’s original formulation described taking “a job traditionally performed by an employee or contractor” and assigning it to an “undefined, generally large group of people” through an open call. [^fed44t]
- Editor Mark Robinson participated in the naming conversation with Howe; later accounts credit Howe and Robinson jointly with the coinage, while Howe’s 2006 article popularized it in business and technology discourse. [^0ygwj7] [^j48jod]
- The term migrated into innovation vocabulary as researchers and practitioners applied it to open innovation, user innovation, peer production, and business models built around distributed contributors. [^kssss9] [^pl0beo] [^q6pgk2]

# Adjacent Vocabulary

- **Synonyms**
  - **Open innovation**: broader than crowdsourcing; includes inbound ideas, external technologies, licensing, partnerships, and other boundary-spanning practices.
  - **Open call**: the invitation mechanism that commonly enables crowdsourcing, but not the whole operating model.
  - **Peer production**: collaborative production by distributed participants, often without conventional managerial control; it may occur within crowdsourcing but is not identical to it.
  - **User innovation**: innovation led or materially shaped by users, whether or not an open call or large crowd is involved.

- **Antonyms**
  - **Closed innovation**: relies primarily on internal employees, proprietary R&D, and controlled organizational boundaries.
  - **Insourcing**: keeps a task within the organization rather than assigning it to an external crowd.
  - **Top-down product development**: concentrates problem definition and solution selection within a central team.

# Usage in Practice

- Jeff Howe described crowdsourcing as “the act of taking a job traditionally performed by an employee or contractor” and giving it to an undefined, generally large group through an open call. [^fed44t]
- Howe’s *Wired* article framed the opportunity as “the new pool of cheap labor,” including people using spare time to create content, solve problems, and perform corporate R&D. [^2eygn4]
- *Wired* contrasted the model with conventional contracting directly: “It’s not outsourcing; it’s crowdsourcing.” [^2eygn4]
- Academic researchers describe crowdsourcing as engaging “a ‘crowd’ of people to achieve a common goal,” often involving innovation, problem-solving, or efficiency. [^kssss9]
- A revised academic definition describes it as using information technology “to outsource any organizational function to a strategically defined population” through an open call. [^sa1m9z]
- Business-model research treats crowdsourcing as a form of business-model innovation in which a traditionally internal job is outsourced to a large, undefined network. [^pl0beo]
- Innovation literature also connects crowdsourcing with collaborative online activities such as co-creation and user innovation. [^q6pgk2]

# Common Misuses

- Calling any customer survey “crowdsourcing.” Use **market research**, **customer feedback**, or **voice-of-customer research** when participants only provide opinions rather than completing an open-call task.
- Calling work assigned to a named agency “crowdsourcing.” Use **outsourcing**, **contracting**, or **managed services** because the provider is selected in advance rather than drawn from an undefined crowd.
- Calling a small workshop with invited stakeholders “crowdsourcing.” Use **co-creation**, **design workshop**, or **participatory design** unless the process genuinely uses an open call to a broad population.
- Using “crowdsourcing” as a vague synonym for community enthusiasm. Use **community building**, **advocacy**, or **community-led growth** when members are promoting or supporting a product without contributing a defined task, asset, or solution.
