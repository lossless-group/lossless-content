# Defining and Describing Customer Journeys

![End-to-end customer journey map showing awareness, consideration, purchase, onboarding, use, support, renewal, and advocacy across digital and human touchpoints](https://www.slideteam.net/media/catalog/product/cache/1280x720/c/o/consumer_insights_journey_map_showing_awareness_consideration_purchase_and_retention_Slide01.jpg)

- _In innovation consulting, a customer journey is the evolving sequence of customer goals, actions, perceptions, and interactions through which a person discovers, adopts, uses, and either continues or abandons a product or service._
- The term applies when a team needs to understand an experience over time rather than optimize an isolated interface, campaign, or transaction. [^i9muyl] A journey can span multiple channels and include touchpoints before purchase, during use, and after service or renewal. [^dd8kbi] Innovation consultants use it to expose friction, unmet needs, adoption barriers, and opportunities for new products, services, processes, or business models. [^i9muyl]

# Disambiguation

## Primary sense — the innovation-consulting sense

A **customer journey** is the end-to-end, customer-perspective process of achieving a goal with or around a product, service, or organization, including the touchpoints, contexts, emotions, and operational conditions that shape the outcome. [^i9muyl] [^dd8kbi]

- A journey is broader than a sales funnel: customers may move back and forth between stages, reconsider alternatives, and interact through several channels rather than proceed linearly. [^dd8kbi]
- A journey map is the representation or artifact; the journey is the underlying experience and behavior being investigated. [^i9muyl]
- The unit of analysis is not merely a company’s touchpoints but the customer’s progress toward a goal, including interactions the organization does not control. [^i9muyl] [^v6cwot]
- In startup and innovation work, journey analysis is useful for finding “pain points,” clarifying the value proposition, prioritizing experiments, and identifying where technology adoption fails or where organizational handoffs break the experience. [^i9muyl] [^3bhyj0]
- It is not synonymous with customer relationship management, a conversion funnel, or a service blueprint: those may analyze parts of the system, whereas a journey emphasizes the customer’s unfolding experience across time. [^i6rvzk] [^464jo9]

## Other senses

### 1. Customer journey mapping

**Customer journey mapping** is the structured visualization of a customer’s experience, commonly showing stages, actions, touchpoints, goals, perceptions, emotions, pain points, and opportunities. [^i9muyl] [^v6cwot]

- Maps help teams move beyond isolated screens or transactions and examine the broader system and context surrounding an experience. [^i9muyl]
- In practice, mapping is used to align stakeholders, identify breakdowns, and connect customer evidence to design or operational priorities. [^i9muyl]
- A map based only on internal assumptions is a hypothesis, not reliable customer research; stronger maps use interviews, observation, behavioral data, and service evidence.

### 2. Customer-experience management

**[[concepts/Market-Categories/Customer Experience|Customer Experience]] management** is the organizational practice of understanding and shaping experiences across the journey, often by coordinating touchpoints, channels, people, and processes. [^5zzhv9] [^3bhyj0]

- Customer journeys provide the temporal structure through which experience is analyzed; customer experience describes the resulting perceptions and responses.
- This sense matters to innovation consultants when experience improvements require changes to incentives, workflows, technology, or organizational ownership rather than a redesigned interface alone. [^3bhyj0]
- Journey work therefore connects design research with operating-model change, service recovery, retention, and customer value creation. [^4zv178] [^3bhyj0]

- Also used in travel and tourism to mean a person’s itinerary or movement between places; that sense is not generally relevant to innovation contexts.

# Etymology and Origin

- The phrase has no single universally accepted founding source; the academic literature has used “customer journey” across several disciplines since at least the 1990s, with definitions remaining fragmented. [^4zv178]
- A major precursor was Lynn Shostack’s 1984 service-blueprinting method, which structured service processes by connecting visible customer interactions with underlying operational activity. [^i6rvzk] [^464jo9]
- Jan Carlzon’s 1987 account of “[[Moments of Truth]]” helped popularize the idea that customers experience an organization through many discrete interactions rather than through its strategy documents alone. [^i6rvzk]
- The modern visual journey-map format emerged later from service design and user-experience practice, while business literature increasingly treated journeys as nonlinear and multi-touchpoint systems during the 2000s and 2010s. [^i6rvzk] [^dd8kbi] [^v6cwot]

# Adjacent Vocabulary

- **Synonyms**:
  - **Customer experience journey** — emphasizes the perceptions and emotions produced across the sequence.
  - **[[User Journeys]]** — often used for digital products or software, with “user” broader than “customer.”
  - **End-to-end experience** — stresses the full lifecycle rather than one interaction or channel.
  - **Service journey** — useful when the experience depends heavily on people, processes, and operational delivery.

- **Antonyms**:
  - **Point solution** — addresses one narrow problem or interaction instead of the surrounding journey.
  - **Inside-out design** — begins with organizational capabilities or assumptions rather than customer behavior and context.
  - **Siloed optimization** — improves individual touchpoints while potentially worsening the overall experience.

# Usage in Practice

- “Journey maps illustrate touchpoints, pain points, emotions, and opportunities for improvement across the full experience.” — *Understanding Customer Journey Mapping* [^i9muyl]
- “Organizations can uncover friction, clarify priorities, and align stakeholders around shared insights.” — *Understanding Customer Journey Mapping* [^i9muyl]
- “Customers jump between phases, add alternatives and remove them again.” — summary of the nonlinear-journey argument associated with McKinsey’s 2009 customer-decision research. [^dd8kbi]
- Customer journeys can be understood as “the sum total of all customer interactions with a product, brand or organization across multiple channels and touchpoints.” [^v6cwot]
- A journey is a process of purchasing and experiencing products or services “seen from a customer’s perspective,” or as walking “in the customer’s shoes.” [^v6cwot]
- Journey-based marketing treats the journey as something to be “systematically analyzed and understood” so that obstacles can be removed and customer value developed. [^3bhyj0]

# Common Misuses

- **Calling a funnel a journey** — A funnel models progression toward conversion; use **customer journey** when the analysis includes looping behavior, post-purchase use, service, renewal, or advocacy. [^dd8kbi]
- **Calling a touchpoint inventory a journey map** — A list of emails, screens, and support contacts lacks customer goals, context, perceptions, and sequence; use **touchpoint inventory** unless those dimensions are researched.
- **Using “journey” for an internal process alone** — A process map describes how the organization works; use **service blueprint** when backstage systems and frontstage delivery are the primary object of analysis. [^i6rvzk] [^464jo9]
- **Treating a speculative persona storyboard as customer evidence** — If assumptions have not been tested with customers or behavioral data, call it a **journey hypothesis** or **experience concept**, not a validated customer journey. [^i9muyl]
