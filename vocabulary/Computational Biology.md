# Defining and Describing Computational Biology

![Computational biology workflow linking biological data, computational models, machine learning, and experimental validation](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/online/courses/network-analysis-of-protein-interaction-data-an-introduction/wp-content/uploads/sites/64/2020/08/new-figure-10-1024x576.png)

- _Computational biology is the use and development of computational, statistical, and
- mathematical methods to generate biological insight and guide decisions in research, healthcare, and biotechnology._ [^ompt0m] [^4y9bsf]
- In startup and innovation contexts, the term applies when computation is part of the core scientific engine—not merely administrative software or digitized laboratory record-keeping. [^ompt0m] It covers activities such as sequence analysis, molecular modeling, systems modeling, biomarker discovery, virtual screening, and interpretation of large biological datasets. [^4y9bsf] [^9vi3ar] An innovation consultant would care because computational biology can change a company’s cost structure, product-development cycle, evidence strategy, and organizational need for hybrid biology–software talent. [^4y9bsf] [^9pfk8b]

# Disambiguation

## Primary sense — the innovation-consulting sense

Computational biology is a **technology and capability domain in which computational models, algorithms, and data analysis are used to discover, predict, or optimize biological outcomes**. [^ompt0m] [^4y9bsf]

- In a startup, the term usually signals a defensible scientific workflow: biological data are converted into predictions, hypotheses, candidate molecules, targets, diagnostics, or experimental priorities. [^4y9bsf] [^9pfk8b]
- It includes both applying existing algorithms and developing new computational methods to solve biological problems. [^ompt0m]
- It is not synonymous with “using AI in healthcare”: a computational-biology product must address a biological question, data-generating process, or mechanistic model, whereas generic software may simply support laboratory operations.
- It is broader than data visualization or bioinformatics support when the work includes predictive or mechanistic modeling of biological systems; however, the boundary with bioinformatics remains disputed. [^ompt0m] [^4y9bsf]

## Other senses

### 1. Computational biology as an academic research field

Computational biology is an interdisciplinary scientific field combining biology with computer science, statistics, mathematics, and modeling. [^ompt0m] [^4y9bsf]

- Academic work may focus on DNA, RNA, proteins, molecular interactions, biological networks, evolution, or whole-system dynamics. [^4y9bsf] [^9vi3ar]
- The field can produce foundational methods and open tools rather than a commercial product; startups often commercialize only one layer of that research stack.
- Its outputs may be algorithms, databases, models, software packages, or experimentally testable biological hypotheses. [^ompt0m] [^8x5sg2]

### 2. Computational biology as a biotechnology operating capability

Computational biology can mean the in-house or outsourced capability that lets a biotechnology company make scientific and portfolio decisions using biological data. [^4y9bsf] [^9pfk8b]

- Typical commercial applications include drug-target identification, protein-structure analysis, virtual screening, biomarker discovery, and sequence analysis. [^4y9bsf] [^72gv8b] [^9pfk8b]
- The strategic question is not whether a company “has AI,” but whether computation improves a measurable bottleneck such as hit finding, experimental throughput, validation, or time to candidate selection.
- This capability may be embedded in a platform company, a drug-discovery team, a diagnostics business, or a contract research organization.

# Etymology and Origin

- The modern field grew from earlier work applying information theory, algorithms, sequence analysis, and computing to biological systems; Margaret Dayhoff’s computational protein work and sequence-alignment methods were important early foundations. [^uwth6s]
- “Bioinformatics” was coined by Paulien Hogeweg and Ben Hesper in 1970 for the study of information processes in biological systems. [^4y9bsf] [^uwth6s]
- “Computational biology” subsequently became a broader and partly overlapping label for computational research in biology, especially work involving models of biological systems rather than only management or annotation of biological data. [^ompt0m] [^4y9bsf]
- The field expanded sharply in the 1990s as sequence databases, DNA sequencing, and the Human Genome Project increased both the volume of biological data and the need for computational interpretation. [^4y9bsf]

# Adjacent Vocabulary

- **Synonyms**:
  - **Bioinformatics**: Often emphasizes management, processing, and interpretation of biological data; its boundary with computational biology is contested. [^ompt0m] [^4y9bsf]
  - **In silico biology**: Highlights computer-based experimentation or simulation rather than laboratory experimentation.
  - **Quantitative biology**: Emphasizes measurement, mathematical analysis, and predictive models across biological scales.
  - **Systems biology**: Focuses on integrated, multiscale, and often mechanistic models of biological systems. [^9vi3ar]

- **Antonyms**:
  - **Wet-lab biology**: Experimental biology conducted primarily through physical laboratory procedures.
  - **Empirical trial-and-error discovery**: Discovery driven mainly by experiments without substantial computational prioritization or modeling.

# Usage in Practice

- “Computational Biology is a field that uses methods in computer science, statistics and modeling to solve biological problems.” — Larracuente Lab. [^ompt0m]
- “The term computational biology can refer to building and using models of biological systems.” — summary of the field’s disputed terminology. [^4y9bsf]
- “All biology is computational biology.” — Ming “Tommy” Tang, arguing that modern biological questions increasingly require computation at scale. [^3ytmdc]
- “The modular LEGO principle of building blocks for protein function.” — Peer Bork, describing a computationally informed view of protein domains and function. [^8x5sg2]
- “I believe that the progress of the gut microbiome research towards improving human health can be paralleled by global microbiomics towards planetary health.” — Peer Bork, connecting computational microbiome research to broader health and planetary applications. [^8x5sg2]
- “Life is three-dimensional, and it begins with molecules.” — Phil Bourne, framing structural computation as a foundation for understanding biology across scales. [^72gv8b]
- “Instead of relying only on lab experiments,” companies can use computation to predict drug–target interactions, perform virtual screening, and identify drug targets. [^9pfk8b]

# Common Misuses

- Calling any laboratory software “computational biology.” **Better term:** laboratory information management system, research software, or digital lab operations.
- Calling a dashboard that reports biological measurements “computational biology” when it performs no biological inference or modeling. **Better term:** bio-data analytics or scientific reporting.
- Using “AI-powered computational biology” as a slogan without specifying the biological task, training data, validation method, or decision improved. **Better term:** machine-learning-assisted sequence analysis, virtual screening, or predictive biomarker modeling.
- Treating bioinformatics and computational biology as interchangeable in every context. **Better term:** use **bioinformatics** for data processing and annotation, and **computational biology** for broader modeling, prediction, and biological explanation—while acknowledging that real-world usage overlaps.
