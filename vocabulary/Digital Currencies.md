# Defining and Describing Digital Currencies

![Diagram comparing cryptocurrencies, stablecoins, electronic money, and central bank digital currencies](https://www.visualcapitalist.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Central_Bank_Digital_Currencies_Main.jpg)
- _Digital currencies are electronically represented monetary or payment assets that startups, institutions, and consumers can use to transfer value without relying on physical cash._
- In innovation consulting, the term is most useful as an umbrella for several distinct models: privately issued cryptocurrencies and stablecoins, electronic money, and central bank digital currencies (CBDCs). [^uhje5m] It does **not** mean every digital payment: a card transaction or ordinary bank-account transfer may be digital without constituting a distinct digital currency. The distinction matters because each model creates different founder choices around issuance, trust, programmability, compliance, distribution, interoperability, and adoption.

# Disambiguation

## Primary sense — the innovation-consulting sense

**Digital currencies** are digitally native or digitally represented forms of value whose design changes how money is issued, stored, transferred, settled, or integrated into products and business models. [^tnkj0c] [^8jfk7p]

- The category includes decentralized cryptoassets such as Bitcoin and Ethereum, privately issued stablecoins, electronic money, and state-backed CBDCs. [^uhje5m]
- For a startup, the relevant question is not simply whether money moves online, but who issues the asset, what backs it, which ledger records it, and who bears settlement and regulatory risk. [^yiua8p] [^8jfk7p]
- Digital currency is broader than cryptocurrency: cryptocurrency generally emphasizes cryptographic or blockchain-based systems, whereas digital currency can also include centralized electronic money and CBDCs. [^kf8aqe] [^uhje5m]
- Digital currency is not synonymous with digital payment. Payment applications may transmit conventional bank deposits or fiat balances without creating a new currency.

## Other senses

### 1. Private digital currencies and cryptoassets

Private digital currencies are digitally native assets issued or maintained by non-state networks, companies, or communities, often using cryptography and distributed ledgers. [^vty9ex] [^uhje5m]

- Bitcoin was proposed in a 2008 white paper by the pseudonymous Satoshi Nakamoto and created in 2009. [^vty9ex] [^uhje5m]
- For founders, this sense raises questions about token utility, network effects, governance, liquidity, custody, security, and whether decentralization produces meaningful user or business value.
- Not every token is money: some tokens represent access, governance rights, securities, or claims on assets rather than a general medium of exchange.

### 2. Stablecoins

Stablecoins are privately issued digital tokens designed to maintain a relatively stable value, commonly through backing with safe assets or other collateral. [^8jfk7p]

- They are often designed for payment and global circulation, making them relevant to cross-border commerce, treasury operations, remittances, and programmable financial products. [^8jfk7p]
- A stablecoin is not a CBDC: it is a private issuer’s liability or tokenized claim, whereas a CBDC is a direct digital claim on the issuing central bank. [^yiua8p] [^8jfk7p]
- Product teams must evaluate reserve quality, redemption rights, issuer solvency, compliance obligations, and the reliability of the infrastructure on which the token operates.

### 3. Central bank digital currencies

CBDCs are electronic forms of sovereign currency issued or backed directly by a central bank and representing a liability of that central bank. [^yiua8p] [^f38h1h]

- CBDCs may be designed for retail users, interbank settlement, or financial-market infrastructure; wholesale CBDCs target institutional settlement rather than ordinary consumer payments. [^8jfk7p]
- For innovation strategy, CBDCs matter as possible changes to payment rails, public infrastructure, bank intermediation, identity systems, and cross-border settlement.
- Central banks had used digital forms of money and payment for decades before CBDC became established terminology. [^kf8aqe]

# Etymology and Origin

- “Digital currency” is a broad descriptive label rather than a term with one universally accepted inventor; contemporary usage groups electronic money, cryptoassets, stablecoins, and CBDCs under one category. [^kf8aqe] [^uhje5m]
- Earlier electronic-cash experiments included Finland’s Avant smart card in 1993, while David Chaum’s company developed an early form of anonymous electronic cash in 1995. [^vty9ex]
- Bitcoin’s 2008 proposal and 2009 launch made privately issued, decentralized digital currency commercially and ideologically prominent. [^vty9ex] [^uhje5m]
- CBDC terminology and policy attention developed later, with CBDCs proposed around 2013 and gaining momentum as central banks responded to cryptocurrencies and Facebook’s 2019 Libra proposal. [^9wbl9h]

# Adjacent Vocabulary

- **Synonyms**:
  - **Electronic money**: broader and often more centralized; can include stored monetary value used through payment systems. [^uhje5m]
  - **Digital money**: near-synonym, often emphasizing the monetary function rather than the asset or infrastructure.
  - **Cryptoasset**: narrower than digital currency; covers tokens that may not function as money.
  - **Virtual currency**: commonly used for privately issued or non-sovereign digital value, but its boundaries vary by jurisdiction. [^kf8aqe]

- **Antonyms**:
  - **Cash**: physical notes and coins rather than electronically represented value. [^uhje5m]
  - **Commodity barter**: exchange without a monetary digital or physical unit.
  - **Offline bearer payment**: value transfer relying primarily on physical possession rather than networked digital records.

# Usage in Practice

- “Digital currency” can function as an umbrella term covering “electronic money such as Suica and PayPay,” cryptoassets, CBDCs, and stablecoins. [^uhje5m]
- Bitcoin is described as “a decentralized digital currency” proposed in Nakamoto’s 2008 white paper. [^uhje5m]
- A CBDC is a “digital form of a country’s sovereign currency” and a direct digital claim on the state. [^yiua8p]
- Stablecoins are “privately issued digital tokens backed by safe assets,” often intended for global circulation. [^8jfk7p]
- Tokenization allows “financial claims—including money, securities, and derivatives—to be represented as programmable digital tokens recorded on shared ledgers.” [^8jfk7p]
- CBDC work has moved from terminology into institutional experimentation, with central banks and international institutions studying retail, wholesale, and cross-border applications. [^107a0u] [^8jfk7p] [^q118yp]

# Common Misuses

- Calling every card payment or bank transfer a “digital currency” is imprecise; **digital payment** is the better term when the underlying balance remains an ordinary bank deposit.
- Calling every crypto token a currency overstates its monetary role; use **cryptoasset**, **utility token**, **governance token**, or **security token** according to the instrument’s function.
- Describing a stablecoin as “digital cash” can conceal issuer and reserve risk; **privately issued payment token** is more precise.
- Treating a CBDC as equivalent to a commercial-bank deposit ignores the issuer relationship; **central-bank liability** or **central bank digital currency** is the accurate term. [^yiua8p] [^f38h1h]
