# Defining and Describing Screencasts

![Founder recording a short product demo screencast on a laptop, showing a SaaS dashboard with voiceover waveform below.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/Adjusting_photo_with_levels.ogv/500px--Adjusting_photo_with_levels.ogv.jpg)

_*In innovation and startup contexts, a **screencast** is a short, shareable video capture of a digital screen—typically with voiceover—used to demonstrate a product, workflow, or concept without needing a live meeting._ [^huyu2n] [^pw6mu9]

Screencasts apply whenever the core “thing to show” is what happens on a computer or device screen: a SaaS UI, a workflow in a tool, a prototype, or a data dashboard. [^huyu2n] [^pw6mu9] [^25pfvt] They do *not* cover broader cinematic marketing videos, talking-head content, or pure slideware; the defining feature is that the viewer primarily watches the **screen** being operated in real time. [^huyu2n] [^pw6mu9] [^k2bq3p] Innovation consultants care about screencasts because they compress product explanation, onboarding, and internal alignment into asynchronous artifacts that can be iterated, measured, and reused—making product understanding and change adoption faster and cheaper than live demos. [^25pfvt] [^6j2ejh] [^k2bq3p] [^d8h008] In founder-led companies, a handful of well-crafted screencasts often become the de facto “operating manual” for prospects, new hires, and customers. [^25pfvt] [^6j2ejh] [^9ulthn] [^d8h008]

# Disambiguation

## Primary sense — the innovation-consulting sense

A **screencast** is a video recording of what happens on a device screen, typically accompanied by narration, used to teach, demonstrate, or validate a digital product or process. [^huyu2n] [^kv6tqt] [^pw6mu9]

- Screencasts are “videos of computer screens with narration that are used to teach or demonstrate computer-related tasks,” making them ideal for showing software workflows, not just describing them verbally. [^huyu2n]  
- Typical startup uses include **product demos**, walkthroughs, bug reports, onboarding guides, and internal training—any situation where showing “what to click” beats explaining it in text. [^25pfvt] [^6j2ejh] [^k2bq3p] [^9ulthn] [^d8h008]  
- A screencast usually records a **specific application window** or workflow rather than the entire desktop, to avoid distractions and keep the focus on the product experience. [^6j2ejh] [^d8h008]  
- This sense is *not* the same as highly produced brand films or animated explainer videos; those may show stylized interfaces or storylines, while screencasts emphasize real, live interaction with the actual product UI. [^k2bq3p] [^2w2pvv] [^s9uohb]

## Other senses

### 1. Screencasts in education and training

A screencast here is a pedagogical screen recording, typically with voiceover, used to teach software use or explain on-screen content step by step. [^huyu2n] [^pw6mu9]

- Educational glossaries define a screencast as a “grabación de la pantalla… acompañada habitualmente de narración en audio… para enseñar a usar un programa [or] mostrar un proceso paso a paso.” [^pw6mu9]  
- Writing and composition scholars describe screencasts as a genre of internet video used to demonstrate computer-related tasks and tell process stories, such as explaining the evolution of a Wikipedia page. [^huyu2n] [^y7qx47]  
- This sense overlaps heavily with innovation contexts when organizations use screencasts for staff training, process documentation, and change management around new digital tools. [^huyu2n] [^pw6mu9] [^d8h008]

- Also used in consumer tech and gaming to mean “recorded gameplay or on-screen activity for entertainment”; this is generally not relevant to innovation consulting beyond illustrating the same underlying recording technique. [^kv6tqt] [^pw6mu9]

# Etymology and Origin

- The term **“screencast”** combines *screen* and *broadcast* and entered English in the early 2000s, by analogy with “podcast,” to describe videos that “emit” what happens on the screen. [^kv6tqt] [^pw6mu9]  
- Journalist **Jon Udell** is widely credited with coining “screencast” in 2004 after a competition to name this emerging genre of internet video, with early print appearance in a *Macworld* article. [^huyu2n] [^kv6tqt] [^y7qx47]  
- Before dedicated screencasting software, early analogs in the 1970s literally filmed computer screens with a video camera; in the 1990s, the first screen-recording software allowed direct digital capture. [^kv6tqt]  
- With the rise of YouTube and improved recording tools in the 2010s, screencasts proliferated and became standard for software tutorials, SaaS demos, and onboarding materials, migrating firmly into startup and business practice vocabulary. [^kv6tqt] [^pw6mu9] [^k2bq3p] [^9ulthn] [^d8h008]

# Adjacent Vocabulary

- **Synonyms**
  - **Screen recording** – Generic term for capturing on-screen activity; lacks the implicit teaching/demo and narration connotations of “screencast.” [^kv6tqt] [^pw6mu9] [^25pfvt]  
  - **Product demo video** – Any video showing a product in action; may be live-action or animated, whereas screencasts are specifically *screen-based*. [^k2bq3p] [^2w2pvv] [^s9uohb]  
  - **Async demo** – Founder or team shorthand for a recorded walkthrough sent instead of a live call; usually implemented as a screencast. [^25pfvt] [^6j2ejh] [^9ulthn] [^s9uohb]

- **Antonyms**
  - **Live demo** – Real-time, synchronous product walkthrough in a meeting, the opposite of an asynchronous screencast artifact. [^25pfvt] [^6j2ejh] [^9ulthn]  
  - **Static documentation** – Text and screenshots without motion or narration; screencasts substitute dynamic, narrated visuals for static instructions. [^huyu2n] [^pw6mu9] [^6j2ejh] [^d8h008]

- **Adjacent terms**
  - [[Product demo video]] – Broader category of videos showing product capabilities. [^k2bq3p] [^2w2pvv] [^s9uohb]  
  - [[Customer onboarding]] – Process where screencasts are used to shorten time-to-value and reduce churn. [^6j2ejh] [^k2bq3p] [^d8h008] [^9ulthn]  
  - [[Founder-led sales]] – Founders using screencast demos to validate and sell before full product maturity. [^02dwbz] [^elzv6o] [^s9uohb]  
  - [[Minimum viable product]] – Screencast demos can serve as an MVP-like artifact to test demand before full build. [^elzv6o] [^s9uohb]  
  - [[Asynchronous communication]] – Screencasts as async updates, walkthroughs, and internal training. [^25pfvt] [^6j2ejh] [^d8h008]  
  - [[Knowledge base]] – Help centers embedding screencast tutorials for self-serve support. [^9ulthn] [^d8h008]

# Usage in Practice

- Jon Udell used a screencast “to tell a story about the evolution of a Wikipedia page,” illustrating how this format can narrate complex, screen-based processes over time. [^y7qx47]  
- A SaaS demo guide explains: “Screencast demos are the straight test drive. You show the product on screen, walk through real workflows, and let people see what they would actually click.” [^k2bq3p]  
- A startup-focused article notes that **ScreenPal** “helps teams record what is happening on their screen and turn it into shareable video communication… commonly used for walkthroughs, bug reports, onboarding guides, product updates, and internal training.” [^25pfvt]  
- A founder tooling blog advises: “Recorded onboarding videos let you deliver a consistent, high-quality onboarding experience without a live call,” by “record[ing] a library of short task-specific videos” for key first-week tasks. [^6j2ejh]  
- An onboarding playbook states that “screencast tutorials and onboarding videos take over” post-purchase and “shorten time to value and reduce churn by helping new users get quick wins.” [^k2bq3p]  
- A SaaS onboarding guide instructs teams to “screen record your product walkthrough, keep it 60–90 seconds,” and then embed the video into welcome modals, empty states, and help centers for maximum impact. [^9ulthn]  
- A LinkedIn breakdown of Dropbox’s early days recalls that Drew Houston “validated Dropbox with a 3-minute screencast… [that] showed what Dropbox would do if it existed,” using a simple screen-recorded demo to test demand before building the full product. [^elzv6o]

# Common Misuses

- Calling highly produced **brand films** or animated explainers “screencasts” when they do not primarily show real on-screen interaction; in these cases, **product demo video** or **explainer video** is more accurate. [^k2bq3p] [^2w2pvv]  
- Labeling any **raw screen capture** as a “screencast” even when it lacks narration or instructional intent; here **screen recording** or **capture** is the better term. [^kv6tqt] [^pw6mu9] [^25pfvt]  
- Using “screencast” for long, unfocused recordings of an entire desktop with notifications and unrelated windows; these are better framed as **raw screen captures** or **session recordings**, whereas screencasts imply curated, task-specific walkthroughs. [^6j2ejh] [^d8h008]  
- Describing generic marketing reels that briefly flash UI mockups as “screencasts”; such assets are closer to **promo videos** or **launch trailers**, not true instructional or demo screencasts. [^k2bq3p] [^2w2pvv] [^s9uohb]
