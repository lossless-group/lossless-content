# Chips Act
![Official European Chips Act semiconductor wafer or chip-fabrication facility image](https://qz.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=1920,quality=85,format=auto/https://assets.qz.com/media/3a4d737827be855a2f7aee42bfb72295.jpg)

- _“Chips Act” is best catalogued as a policy framework rather than a conventional media source: a government-led intervention designed to build semiconductor capacity, resilience, and innovation._
- The name is ambiguous: it refers most prominently to the **European Chips Act**, an EU regulation that entered into force on 21 September 2023,[1] and the U.S. **CHIPS and Science Act**, enacted as Public Law 117-167 on 9 August 2022.[4] Both were created by public institutions rather than an individual author or publisher, and consultants return to them as strategic case studies in industrial policy, supply-chain resilience, and state-backed innovation.

## Type and Format

- **Type:** This source is a **policy framework / legislative act**, with two major jurisdictions: the European Union and the United States.
- **Format details:** The U.S. act funds domestic semiconductor production and authorizes programs for federal science agencies,[4] while the European act establishes measures spanning semiconductor research, manufacturing, investment, monitoring, and crisis coordination.[6]
- **Where it lives:** [European Chips Act — European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/european-chips-act) · [U.S. CHIPS and Science Act — Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/house-bill/4346)

## The People Behind It

- The **European Chips Act** was developed through the EU legislative process by the European Commission, the European Parliament, and the Council of the European Union; the Council gave final approval on 25 July 2023.[8]
- The European framework entered into force on 21 September 2023 and is formally established as Regulation (EU) 2023/1781.[5]
- The U.S. CHIPS and Science Act was enacted by the 117th Congress and signed into law by President Joe Biden on 9 August 2022.[7]
- The U.S. legislation combines semiconductor incentives with authorization for programs across federal science agencies.[4]

## Catalog of Notable Works

- [European Chips Act — European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/european-chips-act) — the EU’s policy package for strengthening semiconductor resilience, investment, research, and industrial capacity.[2]
- [European Chips Act Regulation — EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/EN/legal-content/summary/strengthening-the-eu-s-semiconductor-ecosystem-chips-act.html) — the binding legal framework, including the objective of increasing the EU’s global semiconductor share to 20% by 2030.[5]
- [Chips for Europe Initiative](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/digital-sovereignty-european-chips-act-enters-force) — the EU pillar intended to move knowledge from laboratories into fabs and industrial applications.[3]
- [European Chips Act investment and manufacturing framework](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/cipr/items/806180/) — measures supporting manufacturing facilities, suppliers, “first-of-a-kind” facilities, and open EU foundries.[6]
- [European semiconductor coordination mechanism](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/cipr/items/806180/) — a crisis-monitoring and coordination system for anticipating shortages and responding to supply disruptions.[6]
- [CHIPS and Science Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/house-bill/4346) — the U.S. law supporting domestic semiconductor production and federal science programs.[4]
- [CHIPS Act of 2022: Provisions and Implementation](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R47523) — a Congressional Research Service guide to the U.S. law’s provisions and implementation.[7]

## Why It Matters to Innovators

- It frames semiconductor capacity as a **strategic innovation capability**, not merely a procurement issue: the EU explicitly links manufacturing, design, research, scale-up, and resilience across the full value chain.[3]
- It provides a case study in **mission-oriented innovation policy**: public funding is used to move technologies from research into industrial production, bridging the lab-to-fab gap.[6]
- It shows how governments use ecosystem design to reduce dependency: the European framework combines investment incentives, supply monitoring, crisis coordination, and cross-border collaboration.[6]
- It illuminates [[concepts/Strategic Autonomy]] and [[concepts/Supply Chain Resilience]] by treating technological sovereignty and continuity of supply as innovation outcomes.
- It is useful for diagnosing [[concepts/Innovation Ecosystems]]: competitive advantage depends not only on breakthrough products, but also on fabs, suppliers, talent, research infrastructure, capital, and coordination.
