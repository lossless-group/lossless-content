# The Age of Surveillance Capitalism

- [IMAGE 1: Cover of Shoshana Zuboff’s The Age of Surveillance Capitalism]
- _A landmark business book that explains how digital platforms turn human experience into a raw material for prediction, monetization, and behavioral influence._
- This source is a **book** by Shoshana Zuboff, first published in 2019 by PublicAffairs in the United States and Profile Books in the United Kingdom.[2][10] Innovation consultants return to it because it supplies a vocabulary for analyzing the hidden economic logic behind data-driven products and platforms.

## Type and Format

- **Type:** This source is a book.
- **Format details:** The 2019 illustrated PublicAffairs edition runs 704 pages; the Profile edition is listed at 691 pages.[1][2]
- **Where it lives:** [Publisher preview](https://profilebooks.com/wp-content/uploads/wpallimport/files/PDFs/9781782832744_preview.pdf)[10] · [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books?id=lRqrDQAAQBAJ)[2]

## The People Behind It

- **Shoshana Zuboff** is the author of the book, which presents surveillance capitalism as a new economic logic organized around the extraction and use of behavioral data.[2]
- Zuboff previously wrote *In the Age of the Smart Machine*, a major study of information technology and work; *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism* extends that inquiry from workplace automation to platform power and human behavior.
- The book was published by PublicAffairs in the United States and by Profile Books in the United Kingdom in 2019.[2][10]

## Catalog of Notable Works

The book’s central arguments can be organized as follows:

- **“The Definition”** — Defines surveillance capitalism as an economic logic that treats private human experience as material for predictive products and services.
- **“The Origins of Surveillance Capitalism”** — Traces the model’s emergence within Google and the early online advertising economy.
- **“The Discovery of Behavioral Surplus”** — Explains how firms separated data needed to improve services from excess behavioral data that could be monetized.
- **“The Moat Around the Castle”** — Describes how proprietary data, infrastructure, and prediction systems protect platform advantages.
- **“The Reality Business”** — Shows how prediction markets increasingly depend on intervening in behavior, not merely observing it.
- **“The Right to the Future Tense”** — Connects privacy and autonomy to people’s ability to shape their own future choices.
- **“A Coup from Above”** — Argues that surveillance capitalism creates a novel concentration of knowledge and power over individuals and societies.

The book is documented as a 704-page 2019 work by Zuboff, published by PublicAffairs.[2]

## Why It Matters to Innovators

- **Names the business model beneath “free” digital services.** The book distinguishes user-facing functionality from the hidden monetization of behavioral data, a useful diagnostic for [[Data as Infrastructure]] and [[Sources/Books/Platform Scale|Platform Scale]].
- **Separates personalization from behavioral control.** Its key question is whether a product merely responds to user intent or begins shaping that intent through prediction and intervention.
- **Makes power asymmetry visible.** Zuboff’s framework highlights the gap between what users know about a system and what the system knows about users, strengthening analysis of [[concepts/Information Asymmetry]].
- **Connects product design to institutional legitimacy.** The book treats privacy, consent, and autonomy as strategic design constraints rather than compliance details.
- **Provides a governance lens for AI innovation.** As systems become more predictive and agentic, the surveillance-capitalism framework helps teams ask who owns the data, who benefits from inference, and who can contest automated decisions.
