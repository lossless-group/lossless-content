# Human + Machine

- [IMAGE 1: Cover of *Human + Machine: Reimagining Work in the Age of AI*]
- _A practical business book about redesigning work so humans and artificial intelligence amplify one another rather than compete for the same tasks._
- *Human + Machine* is a **book** by Accenture technology leaders Paul R. Daugherty and H. James Wilson. The first edition was published by Harvard Business Review Press in 2018, and an updated and expanded edition—adding a chapter on generative AI—was published in 2024. [^jv9dol] [^ojsah6] Consultants return to it for its operating model for combining human judgment, creativity, and leadership with machine sensing, prediction, and automation. [^ojsah6] [^8b03j6]

# Type and Format

- **Type:** This source is a **book**.
- **Format details:** The original edition was published by [[Sources/Media/Harvard Business Review|Harvard Business Review]] Press in 2018 and is structured around an introduction, two parts, eight substantive chapters, and a conclusion. [^jv9dol] [^o4dkls] The updated and expanded edition was published in 2024, runs 324 pages, and includes a new chapter on generative AI. [^ojsah6]
- **Where it lives:** [Accenture overview](https://www.accenture.com/en/insights/technology/human-plus-machine) · [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Human_+_Machine_Updated_and_Expanded.html?hl=de&id=jAkQ0AEACAAJ)

# The People Behind It

- **Paul R. Daugherty** is an [[organizations/Accenture|Accenture]] technology and innovation leader and co-authored *Human + Machine* with Wilson. [^jv9dol] Accenture identifies him as its chief technology and innovation officer in the book’s launch announcement. [^jv9dol]
- **H. James Wilson** is a managing director associated with information-technology and business research at Accenture Research. [^jv9dol] More recent Accenture material identifies him as global managing director of technology research and thought leadership at Accenture Research. [^4gdkoj]
- Together, Daugherty and Wilson frame AI as a capability that can extend human performance rather than merely replace human labor. [^8b03j6] Their later book, *[[Radically Human]]*, is described by Accenture as a follow-up to *Human + Machine*. [^ds2i8a]

# Catalog of Notable Works

- **Introduction: What’s Our Role in the Age of AI?** — establishes the central question: how should organizations redesign work when intelligent systems become embedded in business processes? [^o4dkls]
- **The Self-Aware Factory Floor** — examines AI in production, supply chain, and distribution, including the move toward more adaptive operations. [^o4dkls] [^9s46ce]
- **Accounting for Robots** — applies the human–machine model to corporate functions and the changing relationship between automation and professional work. [^o4dkls] [^9s46ce]
- **The Ultimate Innovation Machine** — focuses on AI in research, development, and business innovation. [^o4dkls] [^9s46ce]
- **Say Hello to Your New [[Front-Office Bots]]** — explores AI in customer service, sales, and marketing. [^o4dkls] [^9s46ce]
- **Rearing Your Algorithms Right** — argues that humans remain responsible for developing, training, governing, and deploying AI responsibly. [^o4dkls] [^9s46ce]
- **Super Results from Everyday People** — presents the “missing middle” idea: organizations need people who can combine human strengths with machine capabilities. [^o4dkls]
- **A Leader’s Guide to Reimagining Process** and **Extending Human + Machine Collaboration** — move from individual use cases to enterprise-wide redesign of processes and collaboration. [^o4dkls]

# Why It Matters to Innovators

- **It replaces the automation-versus-jobs binary with augmentation.** The book’s central proposition is that AI’s greatest value comes from combining machine capabilities with human strengths, producing outcomes that neither can achieve alone. [^ojsah6] [^8b03j6] This is a useful foundation for [[Human-AI Collaboration]].
- **It identifies the “missing middle.”** The authors argue that organizations need hybrid skills for translating between business judgment and machine intelligence, a capability closely related to [[Cross-Functional Teams]] and [[Boundary Spanning]].
- **It treats AI as process innovation, not merely software deployment.** The updated edition emphasizes transforming complete business processes—from innovation and customer service to everyday productivity—rather than inserting isolated tools into existing workflows. [^ojsah6]
- **It gives innovators a responsible-AI lens.** “Rearing Your Algorithms Right” places human responsibility across development and deployment, helping diagnose failures caused by weak governance, poor training data, unclear accountability, or inadequate human oversight. [^9s46ce]
- **It connects AI adoption to organizational design.** The book’s examples span factories, corporate functions, R&D, marketing, and service operations, making it useful for [[Business Model Innovation]], [[Service Innovation]], and [[Process Innovation]]. [^9s46ce] [^o4dkls]

# Best Starting Points

- [Human + Machine, Updated and Expanded](https://books.google.com/books/about/Human_+_Machine_Updated_and_Expanded.html?hl=de&id=jAkQ0AEACAAJ) — the most current edition and the accessible entry point for the book’s human–AI thesis, including generative AI. [^ojsah6]
- [The Self-Aware Factory Floor](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/human-machine/9781633693876/01_fm01.html) — start here for a concrete operational example of AI applied to production and supply chains. [^o4dkls]
- [The Ultimate Innovation Machine](https://www.accenture.com/en/insights/technology/human-plus-machine) — the best chapter-level entry point for innovation leaders because it directly addresses AI in R&D and business innovation. [^9s46ce]
- [Rearing Your Algorithms Right](https://www.accenture.com/en/insights/technology/human-plus-machine) — the essential section for responsible deployment, human oversight, and algorithm development. [^9s46ce]
