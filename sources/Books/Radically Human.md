# Radically Human

![Cover of *Radically Human: How New Technology Is Transforming Business and Shaping Our Future*](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/41f-WDRFGmL._SL10_UR1600,800_CR200,50,1200,630_CLa%7C1200,630%7C41f-WDRFGmL.jpg%7C0,0,1200,630+82,82,465,465_PJAdblSocialShare-Gradientoverlay-largeasin-0to70,TopLeft,0,0_PJAdblSocialShare-AudibleLogo-Large,TopLeft,600,270_OU01_ZBLISTENING%20ON,617,216,52,500,AudibleSansMd,30,255,255,255.jpg)

- _A practical business book arguing that the next wave of technology will create more value when it amplifies human judgment, creativity, and agency rather than simply replacing people._
- *Radically Human* is a **book** by Accenture technology executives Paul R. Daugherty and H. James Wilson. [^w39w5y] It was first published by Harvard Business Review Press on April 26, 2022. [^v4ug0v] [^w39w5y] An innovation consultant returns to it for its human-centered framing of AI, digital transformation, and organizational change. [^w39w5y]

## The People Behind It

- **Paul R. Daugherty** is Accenture’s group chief executive for Technology and chief technology officer. [^w39w5y] His perspective in the book comes from leading technology strategy and transformation at a global professional-services firm. [^w39w5y]
- **H. James Wilson** is Accenture’s global managing director of thought leadership and technology research. [^w39w5y] He contributes the research and analytical perspective behind the book’s account of human-centered technology. [^w39w5y]
- The authors position the book as a guide for business leaders adopting “a new generation of human-centric technology” to drive innovation during rapid change. [^w39w5y]

## Catalog of Notable Works

- **Chapter 1 —** Introduces the book’s case for treating technology as a force that should strengthen human capabilities rather than merely automate tasks. [^sbecr9]
- **Chapter 2 —** Develops the shift from technology-first transformation toward approaches designed around human needs and behavior. [^sbecr9]
- **Chapter 3 —** Examines how organizations can use emerging technology to expand creativity, judgment, and problem-solving. [^sbecr9]
- **Chapter 4 —** Connects human-centered design with innovation and new forms of customer and employee value. [^sbecr9]
- **Chapter 5 —** Considers how businesses can build trust while deploying data-intensive and AI-enabled systems. [^sbecr9]
- **Chapters 6–15 —** Extend the argument across organizational transformation, technology adoption, and the future relationship between people and intelligent systems. [^sbecr9]

## Why It Matters to Innovators

- It frames AI and automation as **augmentation strategies**, encouraging teams to ask which human capabilities a technology should amplify before asking which jobs it can eliminate. [^w39w5y] This connects directly to [[concepts/Human-Centered Design|Human-Centered Design]], as promoted by the [[organizations/Stanford Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence|Stanford Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence]]
- Its core thesis is useful for diagnosing technology programs that optimize efficiency while weakening trust, creativity, or customer experience. [^w39w5y]
- The book treats innovation as a socio-technical challenge: successful adoption depends not only on algorithms and infrastructure but also on redesigned work, leadership, and user relationships. [^w39w5y]
- It offers a counterweight to purely automation-driven views of digital transformation, making it a useful companion to [[concepts/Responsible Innovation]] and [[concepts/AI Strategy]].
- Its credibility comes from the authors’ senior roles in Accenture’s technology leadership and research functions, combined with a publisher focused on management and business strategy. [^w39w5y]
