# The Attention Economy (Book)

- [IMAGE 1: Cover of Thomas H. Davenport and John C. Beck’s book *The Attention Economy: Understanding the New Currency of Business*]
- _A foundational business book that treats human attention as a scarce organizational resource—and shows innovators how to measure, attract, allocate, and protect it._
- *The Attention Economy* is a **book** by Thomas H. Davenport and John C. Beck, first published by Harvard Business Press in 2001. [^bq2dly] It remains useful to consultants because it reframes information overload, customer engagement, leadership, and organizational focus as problems of attention management rather than merely information management. [^bq2dly] [^xm6adi]

## Type and Format

- **Type:** This source is a book.
- **Format details:** The first edition was published by Harvard Business Press in 2001 and runs to 255 pages. [^bq2dly] Its 12-chapter structure covers attention measurement, psychology, technology, advertising, e-commerce, leadership, strategy, organizational design, information management, and the future of the attention economy. [^vo5625] [^jvqt4k]
- **Where it lives:** [O’Reilly book page](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-attention-economy/9781422160800/) · [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Attention_Economy.html?id=j6z-MiUKgosC)

## The People Behind It

- **Thomas H. Davenport** was associated with the Accenture Institute for Strategic Change and held academic roles at Boston University and Babson College when the book was published. [^xm6adi]
- **John C. Beck** was a senior research fellow with the Accenture Institute for Strategic Change and a professor at UCLA’s Anderson School of Management. [^xm6adi]
- The authors’ central contribution is to identify attention management as a critical business competency for an information-saturated economy. [^xm6adi]

## Catalog of Notable Works

- **“A New Perspective on Business”** — Opens with the premise that information abundance makes attention, rather than information, the scarce resource organizations must manage. [^vo5625] [^jvqt4k]
- **“Attention, the Story So Far”** — Defines attention and introduces the idea of internal attention markets and competing claims on human focus. [^vo5625]
- **“Doing a Number on You”** — Examines how attention can be measured, including psychological and physiological dimensions. [^vo5625]
- **“From Amoebas to Apes”** — Uses biology and psychology to explain how people select, process, and prioritize stimuli. [^vo5625]
- **“Luddites Beware”** — Explores how emerging technologies alter the balance between push and pull communication, customization, learning, and distraction. [^vo5625]
- **“The Hidden Persuaders”** — Draws lessons from traditional attention industries, especially advertising and other businesses built around capturing audiences. [^vo5625]
- **“Eyeballs and Cyber Malls”** — Applies attention economics to e-commerce, emphasizing metrics such as relevance, engagement, community, and convenience. [^vo5625]
- **“Command Performance”** — Connects attention to leadership and the ability of executives to direct organizational focus. [^vo5625]
- **“Focused Choices and Global Resources”** — Treats strategy as a process of making disciplined choices about scarce attention. [^jvqt4k]
- **“Off the Org Chart”** — Examines how organizational structures and cross-functional initiatives can redirect attention beyond formal reporting lines. [^vo5625] [^jvqt4k]
- **“You’ve Got (Lots and Lots of) Mail”** — Addresses the management of information, knowledge, and communication overload. [^vo5625] [^jvqt4k]
- **“From Myopia to Utopia”** — Concludes with implications for the future of the attention economy. [^vo5625] [^jvqt4k]

## Why It Matters to Innovators

- **Attention is a scarce design constraint.** The book’s central thesis converts “more information” into a strategic liability when users, employees, or customers cannot process it all. [^bq2dly] This connects directly to [[concepts/The Attention Economy|The Attention Economy]] and [[Vocabulary/Cognitive Load|Cognitive Load]].
- **Engagement must be measured, not assumed.** Its treatment of attention measurement gives innovators a basis for distinguishing visibility, relevance, engagement, and sustained use—useful when defining [[concepts/North Star Metrics]] or [[concepts/Product-Market Fit|Product-Market Fit]]. [^vo5625] [^bq2dly]
- **Technology changes the economics of focus.** The discussion of attention technologies shows that interfaces, communication systems, and personalization tools do not merely transmit information; they compete to shape user behavior. [^vo5625]
- **Strategy is allocation.** The book frames leadership, organizational design, and strategy as mechanisms for directing scarce attention toward high-value opportunities, a useful complement to [[concepts/Strategic Focus]] and [[Opportunity Cost]]. [^vo5625] [^jvqt4k]
- **It diagnoses the failure mode of information-rich organizations.** Email overload, fragmented initiatives, excessive choice, and weak prioritization appear as attention-allocation failures rather than isolated productivity problems. [^vo5625] [^jvqt4k]
