# Drive

![Cover of Daniel Pink’s book *Drive: The Surprising Truth About What Motivates Us*](https://www.danpink.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/Drive-scaled.jpg)

- _A practical motivation framework for designing workplaces where people do better work because they have meaningful autonomy, mastery, and purpose._
- **Drive** is a business and management book by Daniel H. Pink, first published in 2009 and later issued in multiple editions. [^34cc6y] [^g9hfgk] Pink argues that high performance and satisfaction come less from external rewards and punishments than from the human need to direct one’s life, learn, create, and contribute. [^34cc6y] [^g9hfgk] Innovation consultants return to it when diagnosing incentive systems that suppress creativity or turn intrinsically motivated work into compliance.

## The People Behind It

- **Daniel H. Pink** is the book’s author and presents *Drive* as a synthesis of decades of behavioral-science research on human motivation. [^34cc6y] [^2exztp]
- Pink is also the author of *A Whole New Mind* and *To Sell Is Human*, both of which extend his interest in creativity, work, persuasion, and changing economic conditions. [^2exztp] [^xy4dyo]
- *Drive* became a major commercial success, appearing on multiple bestseller lists and remaining on the *New York Times* lists for 159 weeks, according to Pink’s biography. [^2exztp]

## Catalog of Notable Works

- **“Type I and Type X”** — Pink contrasts behavior driven by intrinsic satisfaction with behavior primarily driven by external rewards and pressures. [^34cc6y]
- **“The Seven Deadly Flaws of Carrots and Sticks”** — The book challenges the assumption that rewards reliably improve performance, especially for complex or creative work. [^34cc6y]
- **“Autonomy”** — People perform better when they have meaningful control over what they do, how they do it, when they do it, and with whom. [^34cc6y]
- **“Mastery”** — Sustained engagement comes from the desire to improve at something meaningful, even though mastery is inherently incomplete. [^34cc6y]
- **“Purpose”** — Motivation strengthens when individual effort connects to a cause or contribution larger than the self. [^34cc6y]
- **“The Type I Toolkit”** — Pink translates the framework into practical guidance for individuals, organizations, parents, and educators. [^34cc6y] [^k2gwuy]

## Why It Matters to Innovators

- **Diagnoses outdated incentive systems:** *Drive* challenges command-and-control management and highlights how excessive extrinsic rewards can undermine curiosity, experimentation, and intrinsic motivation. [^34cc6y] [^g9hfgk]
- **Provides a human-centered design lens for organizations:** Autonomy, mastery, and purpose offer a practical way to evaluate roles, team structures, innovation programs, and [[Employee Experience]].
- **Connects motivation to creative work:** Innovation depends on learning, creating, and improving—activities that are poorly served by purely transactional reward systems. [^34cc6y] [^g9hfgk]
- **Strengthens the case for empowered teams:** Autonomy supports [[Self-Managed Teams]], local decision-making, and [[Psychological Safety]] by giving people room to exercise judgment.
- **Links innovation to meaning:** Purpose helps organizations frame experimentation as contribution rather than merely process compliance, reinforcing [[Mission-Driven Innovation]].
