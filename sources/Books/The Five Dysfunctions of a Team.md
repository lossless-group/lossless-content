# The Five Dysfunctions of a Team

![Cover of *The Five Dysfunctions of a Team: A Leadership Fable* by Patrick M. Lencioni](https://ministryarchitects.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/five.jpg)

- _A practical leadership fable that helps innovators diagnose why capable teams still struggle to collaborate, decide, and execute._
- This source is a **book** by Patrick M. Lencioni, first published in 2002 by Jossey-Bass/Wiley.[1][4] It is a leadership fable built around a five-layer model of team failure, and consultants return to it because it turns vague collaboration problems into a memorable diagnostic sequence.[1][8]

## Type and Format

- **Type:** This source is a book.[1]
- **Format details:** The original edition is 256 pages and was published by John Wiley & Sons in 2002; later versions include an illustrated 20th-anniversary edition.[1][8]
- **Where it lives:** [Publisher / Wiley listing](https://www.wiley.com/) · [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books?id=vyXT0AEACAAJ)[1]

## The People Behind It

- **Patrick M. Lencioni** is the author of the book and is identified in bibliographic records as its sole author.[1][4]
- Lencioni’s work focuses on leadership, management, organizational health, and team effectiveness, with this book presenting those themes through a fictional business narrative.[1][8]
- The book’s practical companion, *Overcoming the Five Dysfunctions of a Team*, extends the model with exercises and tools for leaders, managers, and facilitators.[3]

## Catalog of Notable Works

- **Inattention to Results** — The top-level failure: team members prioritize individual status, departmental interests, or personal goals over collective outcomes.
- **Avoidance of Accountability** — Without shared commitment, peers hesitate to challenge one another on behavior or performance.
- **Lack of Commitment** — Teams struggle to align when debate is avoided or decisions remain ambiguous.
- **Fear of Conflict** — Productive disagreement is suppressed, leaving important issues unresolved beneath artificial harmony.
- **Absence of Trust** — The foundational dysfunction: people avoid vulnerability, conceal mistakes, and protect themselves rather than collaborate openly.
- [*Overcoming the Five Dysfunctions of a Team*](https://books.google.com/books?id=Gl7HAgAAQBAJ) — 2005 — A field guide that translates the framework into practical exercises and implementation tools.[3]

## Why It Matters to Innovators

- It provides a simple diagnostic stack: trust enables conflict, conflict enables commitment, commitment enables accountability, and accountability supports results.[1][8]
- It explains why innovation teams can possess strong individual expertise yet fail at collective execution when psychological safety and candor are weak.
- It treats disagreement as a productive input to decision quality rather than evidence of team failure, connecting directly to [[concepts/Psychological Safety|Psychological Safety]] and [[concepts/Constructive Conflict]].
- It helps leaders distinguish a **coordination problem** from a **commitment problem**: unclear decisions often reflect avoidance of debate rather than lack of information.
- Its team-health lens complements [[concepts/Cross-Functional Collaboration]], [[Vocabulary/Decision Science|Decision Science]], and [[concepts/Accountability]].
