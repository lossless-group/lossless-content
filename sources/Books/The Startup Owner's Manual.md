# The Startup Owner's Manual

![Cover of *The Startup Owner’s Manual* by Steve Blank and Bob Dorf](https://target.scene7.com/is/image/Target/GUEST_f354e499-75bd-422b-a5cc-8d51c77f92f4)

- _A practical field guide for testing, validating, and building a startup around a repeatable, scalable business model._
- *The Startup Owner’s Manual* is a **book** by [[Sources/People/Influencers/Steve Blank|Steve Blank]] and Bob Dorf, first published by K&S Ranch Publishing in 2012 and later issued by John Wiley & Sons. [^cwvc68] Its central method is Customer Development: a structured process for discovering and validating customer needs before scaling the company. [^e6bxlj] [^8fdwt4] An innovation consultant returns to it when a team is mistaking product-building activity for evidence of a viable business.

# Type and Format

- **Type:** This source is a **book**.
- **Format details:** The original edition was published in 2012 by K&S Ranch and runs to 571 pages; the Wiley edition was published in 2020, is 608 pages, and retains the earlier cover, design, and content rather than constituting a new or updated product. [^1x15uq] [^cwvc68] [^e6bxlj]
- **Where it lives:** [Steve Blank’s Startup Owner’s Manual page](https://steveblank.com/startup-owners-manual-1in/) [^hpvn4w] · [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books?id=eSrfDwAAQBAJ) [^1x15uq] · [Wiley edition](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/The+Startup+Owner%27s+Manual%3A+The+Step+By+Step+Guide+for+a+Great+Company-p-9781119690689) [^p998f2]

# The People Behind It

- **Steve Blank** is the creator of the [[concepts/Customer Development|Customer Development]] methodology, which organizes early-stage company-building around customer evidence rather than an assumed business plan. [^p998f2] [^8fdwt4]
- Blank teaches entrepreneurship at [[organizations/Stanford University|Stanford University]], the University of California, and Columbia University, and is associated with the development of the Lean Startup movement. [^p998f2] [^e6bxlj]
- **Bob Dorf** is a serial entrepreneur who founded his first successful company at age 22 and has since founded or advised multiple ventures. [^p998f2]
- Dorf helped critique early drafts of Blank’s *The Four Steps to the Epiphany* and is described by Wiley as a key practitioner of Customer Development. [^p998f2]

# Catalog of Notable Works

- **“The Path to the Epiphany: The Customer Development Model”** — introduces the Customer Development framework and its four-stage structure for startup learning. [^e6bxlj]
- **Customer Discovery** — tests whether the proposed problem, customer, and value proposition reflect a real market need. [^8fdwt4]
- **Customer Validation** — looks for evidence that customers will buy and that the venture has a repeatable sales process. [^8fdwt4]
- **Customer Creation** — develops demand after the company has evidence of a validated opportunity. [^8fdwt4]
- **Company Building** — transitions from searching for a business model to executing and scaling a proven one. [^8fdwt4]
- **The Customer Development Manifesto** — states that a startup is “a temporary organization designed to search for a repeatable and scalable business model.” [^lg1bss]
- **Startup execution checklists and diagrams** — the Wiley edition includes more than 100 charts, graphs, and diagrams and 77 checklists intended to guide practical implementation. [^e6bxlj]

# Why It Matters to Innovators

- It separates **search** from **execution**: a startup initially searches for a repeatable, scalable business model rather than operating as a smaller version of an established company. [^lg1bss] This is a useful foundation for [[concepts/Startup as Search]].
- It makes customer contact a core operating activity. Customer Development requires teams to test hypotheses through customer conversations, product iteration, and evidence of purchasing behavior. [^o896km] [^8fdwt4] This connects directly to [[concepts/Customer Discovery]] and [[concepts/Minimum Viable Product]].
- It diagnoses premature scaling: sales, marketing, and business-development teams should be built after the company has evidence of a viable business, including a tested sales roadmap and valid purchase orders. [^8fdwt4]
- It provides a four-step learning loop—[[concepts/Customer Discovery]], [[concepts/Customer Validation]], [[concepts/Customer Creation]], and [[concepts/Company Building]]—that helps innovators identify whether they are still exploring the problem or are ready to scale. [^8fdwt4]
- Its methodology helped establish the intellectual and practical foundations of the Lean Startup movement, especially the discipline of testing assumptions before committing significant resources. [^p998f2] [^e6bxlj]
