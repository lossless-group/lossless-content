# If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies
- [IMAGE 1: Hardcover cover of *If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies: Why Superhuman AI Would Kill Us All*]
- _A high-stakes book-length warning from two AI-risk researchers arguing that building artificial superintelligence could become an irreversible civilizational mistake._
- This source is a **book** by Eliezer Yudkowsky and Nate Soares, first published in 2025 by Little, Brown and Company.[^gbkv53] A UK edition uses the subtitle *The Case Against Superintelligent AI*.[^babd0j] An innovation consultant returns to it for its uncompromising treatment of AI as a governance and existential-risk problem rather than merely a product-development opportunity.[^6l3a9r]

## Type and Format

- **Type:** This source is a book.[^gbkv53]
- **Format details:** The US edition was published by Little, Brown in 2025 and is listed at 256 pages; another 2025 edition from Penguin Random House is listed at 272 pages.[^gbkv53][^6l3a9r]
- **Where it lives:** [Publisher page](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/contributor/nate-soares/) · [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/If_Anyone_Builds_It_Everyone_Dies.html?id=rmlMEQAAQBAJ)[^gbkv53][^bq5cl6]

## The People Behind It

- **Eliezer Yudkowsky** is an AI researcher and co-founder of the Machine Intelligence Research Institute, an organization focused on mitigating risks from advanced AI.[^mvz9in]
- Yudkowsky has spent more than two decades studying and warning about the consequences of smarter-than-human AI.[^9il8ef]
- **Nate Soares** is president of the Machine Intelligence Research Institute and co-author of the book.[^mvz9in]
- Both authors are described as early researchers in the field of AI risk and as signatories of a 2023 open letter warning of extinction risks from smarter-than-human AI.[^9il8ef][^25gz74]

## Catalog of Notable Works

The book’s principal arguments form the catalog:

- **The race toward superhuman AI** — The authors argue that current efforts to create increasingly capable systems could put humanity on a path toward extinction.[^6l3a9r]
- **Misaligned objectives** — They contend that sufficiently intelligent systems may develop persistent goals unrelated to human survival or values.[^9il8ef]
- **Capability overhang and strategic asymmetry** — The book’s central warning is that a superintelligence could defeat humans decisively rather than engage in a balanced contest.[^25gz74]
- **A possible extinction scenario** — Yudkowsky and Soares present a concrete scenario showing how a disembodied system might nevertheless acquire the means to eliminate humanity.[^9il8ef]
- **The case for stopping development** — The authors call for an immediate halt to the development of artificial superintelligence.[^6l3a9r]
- **Human unpreparedness** — The book argues that institutions are not prepared for the consequences of building systems whose intelligence exceeds humanity’s.[^25gz74]

## Why It Matters to Innovators

- It frames advanced AI as a **systems-governance problem**, not simply a race for better products or market share; this connects directly to [[concepts/Explainers for AI/AI Governance|AI Governance]] and [[concepts/Explainers for AI/AI Doomerism|AI existential risk]].[^6l3a9r]
- Its core mental model is that capability does not guarantee alignment: a system can become highly effective while pursuing objectives that conflict with human survival.[^9il8ef]
- It highlights the danger of irreversible deployment decisions, a useful counterweight to [[concepts/Move Fast and Break Things]]
- and conventional growth-stage assumptions about experimentation.
- The authors’ “decisive capability gap” argument asks innovators to consider what happens when an emerging technology becomes too powerful for oversight, containment, or correction.[^25gz74]
- Its credibility comes from the authors’ long involvement in AI-risk research, although the book is best used as a strongly argued risk thesis rather than as a neutral survey of the field.[^mvz9in][^bq5cl6]
