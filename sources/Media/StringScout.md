# StringScout

- ![StringScout website interface showing the catalog of applied-for ICANN 2026 gTLD strings](https://tldz.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2026-Applied-for-String-List.png)
- _StringScout is a specialist website for mapping every applied-for string in ICANN’s 2026 new-gTLD round to its applicant and backers._
- StringScout is a **website**, not a book, person, publication, or recurring media channel. Its catalog covers applied-for strings and applicants in ICANN’s 2026 round, whose application information was publicly released by ICANN during the 2026 program timeline.[2][3] The site is useful to innovation consultants because it turns a dense registry-policy dataset into a faster way to scan emerging categories, naming strategies, and competitive intent.

## Type and Format

- **Type:** This source is a website.
- **Format details:** StringScout is a searchable digital directory organized around applicants and applied-for gTLD strings; its public Applicants page presents strings such as `.wellness`, `.west`, `.wood`, `.zen`, and `.zzz`.[4]
- **Where it lives:** [Homepage](https://stringscout.com/) · [Applicants](https://stringscout.com/applicants)

## The People Behind It

The available public search results identify StringScout’s website and catalog, but do not identify its founder, editorial team, or operating organization.[4]

## Catalog of Notable Works

StringScout’s catalog is the website itself: its applicant directory maps organizations to the strings they applied for in ICANN’s 2026 round.[4] ICANN describes the underlying official dataset as including applied-for strings, applying entities, public application portions, and contention information.[2][3]

## Why It Matters to Innovators

- **Maps strategic intent through naming:** Applied-for top-level domains reveal how companies, communities, and registries attempt to define or own emerging categories before those categories become crowded.
- **Supports competitive landscaping:** The applicant-to-string relationship helps identify potential incumbents, challengers, brand extensions, and coalition-backed initiatives in a future-facing domain.
- **Makes contention visible:** ICANN’s 2026 process includes contention sets and a formal evaluation path, so overlapping applications can signal competitive pressure around a term or category.[2][6]
- **Useful for [[concepts/Category Design|Category Design]]:** A proposed domain ending can function as a claim about who a category is for, what it means, and which actors want to become its default reference point.
- **Pairs well with [[Market Mapping]]:** StringScout provides a naming-level view of market formation; ICANN’s official application data remains the authoritative source for complete and verified application information.[8]
