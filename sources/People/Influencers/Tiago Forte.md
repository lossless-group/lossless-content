# Tiago Forte
- [IMAGE 1: Tiago Forte author headshot or Forte Labs homepage portrait]
- _Tiago Forte is a person and influential author whose work gives knowledge workers practical systems for turning scattered digital information into usable creative output._
- Tiago Forte is the founder of Forte Labs and creator of the Building a Second Brain methodology.[4][6] His public work began as a body of online teaching and writing before his first major book, *Building a Second Brain*, was published in June 2022.[1][3] Innovation consultants return to Forte for pragmatic frameworks for [[Personal Knowledge Management]], information architecture, and [[Creative Productivity]].

## Type and Format

- **Type:** This source is a person.
- **Format details:** Forte’s primary public surface is Forte Labs, where he publishes articles, courses, and material on digital organization, creativity, and knowledge work.[4] His work is also distributed through books, online programs, and public speaking; Forte Labs reports that his programs have reached more than 20,000 people worldwide.[6]
- **Where it lives:** [Forte Labs](https://fortelabs.com/) · [About Forte Labs](https://fortelabs.com/about-forte-labs/)

## The People Behind It

- Forte is the founder of Forte Labs and the creator of [[Sources/Books/Building a Second Brain|Building a Second Brain]].[6]
- Before focusing on knowledge management, he worked in microfinance in Latin America, served in the Peace Corps in Ukraine, and consulted on product development in San Francisco.[6]
- His work draws on information science, user-experience design, and experience working with organizations and leaders in Silicon Valley.[6]
- Forte’s central public question is how humans can reach their creative potential in a connected, digital world.[4]
- He lives in Long Beach, California, according to Forte Labs’ biography.[6]

## Catalog of Notable Works

- [Building a Second Brain](https://www.simonandschuster.net/books/Building-a-Second-Brain/Tiago-Forte/9781982167400) — 2022 — Forte’s flagship book on capturing, organizing, distilling, and expressing digital knowledge.[1]
- [The PARA Method](https://fortelabs.com/) — 2023 — A system for organizing information by Projects, Areas, Resources, and Archives; Forte Labs presents it as a way to find information when needed.[4]
- [CODE framework](https://fortelabs.com/) — developed through Forte’s Building a Second Brain work — a workflow for capturing, organizing, distilling, and expressing knowledge.[4][10]
- [Building a Second Brain course](https://fortelabs.com/about-forte-labs/) — online course — an applied program teaching Forte’s knowledge-management methodology; Forte Labs reports more than 5,000 graduates across over 70 countries.[6]
- [I Got a Book Deal for Building a Second Brain!](https://fortelabs.com/blog/book-deal-for-building-a-second-brain/) — 2020 — a behind-the-scenes account of the book’s acquisition by Atria Books, an imprint of Simon & Schuster.[3]
- [The Making of a Bestseller](https://fortelabs.com/blog/was-traditional-book-publishing-worth-it/) — 2023 — a retrospective on the publishing process and the first ten months after the book’s release.[9]

## Why It Matters to Innovators

- Forte treats personal knowledge management as an innovation capability: externalizing ideas into a searchable system reduces the friction between research, synthesis, and action.[4][6] This connects directly to [[Personal Knowledge Management]] and [[Knowledge Work]].
- The “second brain” concept reframes note-taking from passive storage into an [[Idea Pipeline]] for producing proposals, briefs, experiments, and other creative outputs.[4]
- PARA offers a deliberately lightweight information architecture: organize material according to current Projects, ongoing Areas, useful Resources, and inactive Archives.[4][10] The model is useful when teams need a system that supports retrieval without imposing a complex taxonomy.
- CODE emphasizes movement from raw inputs toward usable expression rather than endless collection.[4][10] It complements [[Design Thinking]] by making evidence, observations, and prior learning easier to recombine.
- Forte’s work diagnoses a common innovation bottleneck: organizations accumulate information faster than they can retrieve, interpret, and apply it.[4][6]
