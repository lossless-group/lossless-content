# Geoffrey Moore

 ![Geoffrey Moore headshot or official author portrait](https://cagspeakers.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/moore-geoffrey.jpg)

- _Geoffrey Moore is a person—an author, advisor, and innovation strategist whose frameworks help technology companies move from promising breakthroughs to mainstream adoption._
- Moore is an American organizational theorist, management consultant, and author best known for *[[Sources/Books/Crossing the Chasm|Crossing the Chasm]]*. [^vbduj1] He began publishing business books with *Crossing the Chasm* in 1991, after earlier work in teaching, sales, marketing, and high-tech consulting. [^13r9i6] [^5yacgv] Innovation consultants return to his work for practical models of technology adoption, disruptive innovation, and organizational execution.

## The People Behind It

- Moore earned a bachelor’s degree in American literature from Stanford University and a Ph.D. in English literature from the University of Washington. [^13r9i6] [^4b0rm9]
- He taught at Olivet College before moving into high-tech training, sales, marketing, and consulting. [^13r9i6] [^5yacgv]
- Moore worked at Regis McKenna Inc. and helped found The Chasm Group, Chasm Institute, and TCG Advisors. [^13r9i6] [^5yacgv]
- He has served as chairman emeritus of those consulting firms and as a venture partner with Mohr Davidow Ventures. [^0voa7h]
- His signature contribution is translating disruptive-innovation dynamics into operating frameworks for positioning, go-to-market strategy, and enterprise transformation. [^13r9i6] [^dhn1vo]

## Catalog of Notable Works

- [Crossing the Chasm](https://books.google.com/books/about/Crossing_the_Chasm.html?id=yJXHUDSaJgsC) — 1991 — Moore’s foundational go-to-market model for moving technology products from early adopters into the mainstream market. [^13r9i6] [^0voa7h]
- [Inside the Tornado](https://www.geoffreyamoore.com/) — 1995 — examines marketing and competitive strategy during periods of hypergrowth. [^vbduj1]
- [The Gorilla Game](https://www.geoffreyamoore.com/) — 1998 — co-authored with Paul Johnson and Tom Kippola; focuses on identifying market leaders in high technology. [^vbduj1]
- [Living on the Fault Line](https://www.geoffreyamoore.com/) — 2000 — addresses management and shareholder value in the internet era. [^vbduj1]
- [Dealing with Darwin](https://www.geoffreyamoore.com/) — 2005 — explores how companies innovate across different phases of organizational and market evolution. [^vbduj1]
- [Escape Velocity](https://www.geoffreyamoore.com/) — 2011 — addresses how established enterprises can create new growth businesses without being constrained by their existing portfolio. [^hav0p2] [^dhn1vo]
- [Zone to Win](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Zone-to-Win/Geoffrey-A-Moore/9781682302118) — 2015 — offers an organizational playbook for competing while pursuing disruptive innovation inside large enterprises. [^dhn1vo]

## Why It Matters to Innovators

- Moore’s **technology-adoption lifecycle** diagnoses why technically strong products can stall between enthusiastic early adopters and pragmatic mainstream customers; that transition is the “chasm” his best-known framework addresses. [^13r9i6] [^0voa7h]
- His positioning model encourages innovators to define a narrow target segment, articulate a compelling “whole product,” and win a beachhead before expanding—useful for [[concepts/Beachhead Markets]] and [[Vocabulary/Go-to-Market]]. [^13r9i6]
- *Inside the Tornado* and *The Gorilla Game* shift attention from invention alone to category dynamics, competitive positioning, and the race to establish market leadership. [^vbduj1]
- *Escape Velocity* and *Zone to Win* diagnose the structural conflict between protecting an incumbent business and funding an emerging one, making them useful companions to [[concepts/Disruptive Innovation]] and [[concepts/ ambidextrous organization]]. [^vbduj1] [^dhn1vo]
- Moore’s body of work is especially credible for technology commercialization because it combines decades of high-tech consulting with frameworks repeatedly applied to startups and established technology companies. [^13r9i6] [^dhn1vo] [^4b0rm9]

- [Crossing the Chasm](https://books.google.com/books/about/Crossing_the_Chasm.html?id=yJXHUDSaJgsC) — the essential entry point for understanding technology adoption and mainstream-market strategy. [^13r9i6] [^0voa7h]
