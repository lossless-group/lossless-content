# Patrick Lencioni

![Patrick Lencioni headshot or official Table Group portrait](https://www.theartof.com/assets/images/speaker-headshots/large/PatrickLencioni.jpg)

- _Patrick Lencioni is a management thinker and author whose practical models help innovation leaders diagnose team dysfunction, clarify organizational health, and improve execution._
- Patrick Lencioni is a **person**: an author, consultant, keynote speaker, and founder of The Table Group, a firm focused on executive-team development and organizational health. [^4xix32] He founded The Table Group in 1997 after working at [[organizations/Bain & Company|Bain & Company]], [[organizations/Oracle|Oracle]], and Sybase. [^47nglw] Consultants return to his work because it translates abstract leadership and culture problems into memorable, operational frameworks. [^a589sy] [^47nglw]

- **Type:** This source is a person.
- **Format details:** Lencioni’s primary public surface is The Table Group and related Working Genius sites; his work is delivered through books, consulting, speaking, and practical team-development tools. [^a589sy] [^47nglw]

## The People Behind It

- Lencioni is a founder of The Table Group and is described as a pioneer of the organizational-health movement. [^47nglw]
- Before founding The Table Group, he worked at Bain & Company, Oracle Corporation, and Sybase. [^47nglw]
- His work focuses on leadership, teamwork, organizational health, human dignity, and candid communication. [^47nglw]
- He has authored 13 books, including *The Five Dysfunctions of a Team*, *The Advantage*, and *The Ideal Team Player*. [^a589sy] [^47nglw]
- His books have sold more than 8 million copies and been translated into more than 30 languages. [^47nglw]

## Catalog of Notable Works

- [The Five Dysfunctions of a Team](https://www.tablegroup.com/books/five-dysfunctions/) — 2002 — a leadership fable that diagnoses five recurring barriers to team effectiveness; the book was published by Jossey-Bass and is 256 pages in its hardcover edition. [^htvb50] [^nic8u3]
- [The Five Temptations of a CEO](https://www.tablegroup.com/books/five-temptations/) — a fable about the leadership choices that can undermine executive effectiveness. [^u9azwr]
- [Death by Meeting](https://www.tablegroup.com/books/death-by-meeting/) — a practical critique of unfocused meetings and a framework for making them more engaging and purposeful. [^u9azwr]
- [The Three Signs of a Miserable Job](https://www.tablegroup.com/books/three-signs/) — a model explaining how anonymity, irrelevance, and immeasurement damage employee experience. [^u9azwr]
- [The Advantage](https://www.tablegroup.com/books/the-advantage/) — an argument that organizational health can become a durable competitive advantage. [^a589sy]
- [The Ideal Team Player](https://www.tablegroup.com/books/ideal-team-player/) — a framework centered on humility, hunger, and people smarts as core team-member virtues. [^a589sy]
- [The 6 Types of Working Genius](https://www.workinggenius.com/) — a model identifying six fundamental activities required for work and helping individuals and teams understand their natural gifts. [^a589sy] [^ab3ci5]

## Why It Matters to Innovators

- **Diagnoses collaboration failure before it becomes an innovation failure.** The Five Dysfunctions framework focuses on trust, conflict, commitment, accountability, and results—the social infrastructure required for cross-functional experimentation. [^htvb50] [^4xix32] This connects directly to [[concepts/Team Effectiveness]] and [[concepts/Psychological Safety|Psychological Safety]].
- **Reframes organizational health as an innovation capability.** Lencioni’s work treats clarity, cohesion, and healthy debate as operating conditions for better decisions, rather than as “soft” culture initiatives. [^a589sy] [^47nglw] It complements [[concepts/Organizational Health|Organizational Health]]. 
- **Makes productive conflict explicit.** Teams that avoid disagreement often produce false alignment; Lencioni’s model encourages trust-based, idea-focused conflict before commitment. [^htvb50] [^4xix32] This supports [[concepts/Constructive Conflict]].
- **Improves meeting design as a flow problem.** *Death by Meeting* treats recurring meetings as mechanisms for context, debate, decisions, and alignment rather than status-report rituals. [^u9azwr] This is useful for [[concepts/Innovation Governance]].
- **Maps individual contribution to the work process.** Working Genius identifies six activities required for work and helps teams see where energy, capability, or handoffs are missing. [^a589sy] [^ab3ci5] It can complement [[concepts/Team Topologies|Team Topologies]] and [[concepts/Role Clarity]].

## Best Starting Points

- [The Five Dysfunctions of a Team](https://www.tablegroup.com/books/five-dysfunctions/) — the most accessible entry point to Lencioni’s core team-effectiveness model. [^htvb50] [^nic8u3]
- [The Advantage](https://www.tablegroup.com/books/the-advantage/) — the best starting point for connecting team health to organizational performance. [^a589sy]
- [The 6 Types of Working Genius](https://www.workinggenius.com/) — a practical entry point for understanding contribution patterns and work handoffs. [^a589sy] [^ab3ci5]
- [Death by Meeting](https://www.tablegroup.com/books/death-by-meeting/) — useful when innovation work is slowed by recurring, low-value meetings. [^u9azwr]
